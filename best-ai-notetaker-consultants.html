Best AI notetaker for consultants and agencies (2026): Fireflies vs MeetGeek vs Krisp

I checked official vendor pricing pages and help docs on 18 November 2026. I have not long-term tested every tool, so this is not a hands-on review of accuracy, of whether a client actually sees a notice, or of which official page a product follows when two pages disagree. Recording-consent laws vary by state and by country. This page is not legal advice. Tell participants they are being recorded and follow the rules that apply to your calls.

Prices and limits below are as of November 2026. Please recheck each official page before you buy. Yearly figures are the monthly equivalent when billed annually. Seat totals are that per-person list price multiplied by 3, 5, or 10. They do not include tax, add-ons, or discounts.

Related guides: the full comparison and free plans and trials. I compared Fireflies, MeetGeek, and Krisp on client calls.

1. Will the client see a bot?

Fireflies help says the bot appears in the participant list as Fireflies.ai Notetaker, may wait to be admitted, and posts a message in the meeting chat. The same article says you cannot hide that presence, that the chat message alone may not meet consent laws, and that you should tell participants and get consent where required. A Meeting Compliance setting can show a pre-meeting disclaimer. If someone opts out, that article says Fireflies will not join. Google Meet may label the bot a “potential risk”; Fireflies says that label is expected and is not a risk warning.

The same joins article says you can record without the bot using the Chrome extension, which the extension article limits to Google Meet audio, not Zoom or Teams and not video. The desktop article, updated the day before this check, says “Take Notes” captures system audio and does not put a bot in the call. It also says that mode does not save separate audio or video files; it generates a transcript and summary. Notices for Take Notes can be turned on or off: an audio message about 10 seconds after start, a Mac-only meeting-chat message, and a camera watermark. The article says those options are on by default, that Windows does not get the chat message, and that if you turn a notice off you are responsible for telling people and for complying with recording laws. It also says some states require everyone’s consent for phone and in-person recordings.

MeetGeek’s FAQ says the usual path is a Notetaker on Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, and that participants are told through recording-consent notifications. It says the Chrome extension is bot-free and invisible to other participants, and that the desktop app records with no bot joining. I have not tested whether other people can tell an extension or desktop recording is running.

Krisp’s product page leads with “No bot joins your meeting.” The Krisp Bot help article says an optional bot, from the Core plan, joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Teams as a separate participant after the host admits it. The recording help article says you can turn on “Notify others you’re using Krisp” under Privacy & Consent, and that some platforms may block the chat message or audio prompt. The product-page FAQ says note takers are legal when used with consent. It also says “no audio is sent to third parties” and, in another answer, describes Krisp as working without cloud recordings. The cloud-data help article says transcription is processed in the cloud and recordings are stored encrypted in the cloud. Those product-page sentences and the help article do not say the same thing.

2. Notes and action items in HubSpot and Slack

Fireflies’ HubSpot page says the integration logs meeting notes, transcriptions, and action items to contacts, companies, and deals, can create a contact if the person is not already there, and can turn action items into HubSpot tasks. It does not name a minimum plan. The pricing FAQ says app and CRM integrations are on premium tiers, and that sending audio through Zapier starts on Pro. Free-plan help says integrations are available on Free. I am not choosing which of those is current.

The Slack setup article says Fireflies can post summaries and notes to a public channel, a private channel, or a DM, with action items in a checklist in the thread. You pick the destination; notes do not post until a configuration exists. Integration Rules that filter which meetings are sent, including a filter for external meetings, are Business or higher. Free and Pro can still apply one Slack configuration to the workspace, and private meetings are not pushed unless a super admin can see them. Admins on Business and Enterprise get finer control of who may connect the integration.

MeetGeek’s HubSpot guide, updated the day of this check, says a past meeting can be sent by hand, or a workflow can send the summary or selected highlights after analysis. It can log a Meeting or a Note on the matching contact, company, and deal, and it can create records that are missing. The guide does not name a minimum plan. The pricing comparison marks the Integrations row and workflow automation off for Basic and on from Pro, while API and MCP, calendars, and the Slack assistant are marked on for Basic. The homepage paragraph says Pro adds unlimited integrations and Zapier, Make, and n8n.

The Slack guide says you can post a meeting or a highlight to a channel with a workflow, or share one highlight by hand. It does not name a minimum plan. The FAQ also says calendar settings can share automatically with teammates or customers, depending on settings.

Krisp HubSpot help is marked for the Core plan. It says you can send meeting notes automatically or, from the meeting’s Share button, pick a HubSpot contact, company, or deal. The product page says HubSpot logs call summaries and action items to the contact timeline. Slack help, also marked Core, describes notifications: an access request, a meeting shared with you, and an invite to collaborate on an agenda and action items. The product page separately says Slack can “share the summary to a channel the minute the meeting ends.” The help article I read does not describe that channel post. The product page also lists Zapier for routing transcripts and notes.

3. Sharing a summary with the client

Fireflies free-plan help says you can share recaps with teammates or external guests, and that someone who was not in the meeting can view a transcript without a Fireflies account. The desktop article says recap email goes to participants when a Take Notes recording is linked to a calendar event, following the “Send email recap to” setting. The joins FAQ says one path to free transcription is the unlimited-transcription toggle plus sharing recaps with all invitees. The dedicated unlimited-transcriptions article does not list that sharing requirement. For a client call, those two pages matter: one of them connects a free transcription path to sending the recap to every invitee.

MeetGeek getting started says the default is that everyone in the meeting gets an email with the summary and a link to the transcript. The same article says the controls (all participants, team members only, or yourself only) start on Pro and are not on Basic. The FAQ says nobody can access a meeting unless you share it, and that at first only participants can access the recording. Read together, the client who was on the call is a participant. Past meetings help says you can see who has access, invite more people, share to a connected tool, and bulk-share or bulk-delete. If you were recorded but do not own the account, the deletion guide says MeetGeek will not delete that recording for you; the owner has to.

Krisp help shows sharing as an action you take (the Share button, and links that die after permanent deletion). I did not find an official sentence that says Krisp emails the client a recap by default.

4. Storage region and deletion

Fireflies says the default is storage and processing in the United States on AWS and GCP. Enterprise private storage can keep meeting data in dedicated storage, including the EU, and the same article says processing still happens in the US. It says customers own their data and that meeting content is not used to train Fireflies models. Deletion help says only the meeting owner can delete a meeting, deletion is permanent, bulk delete starts on Pro, and automatic deletion of old meetings is Enterprise.

MeetGeek’s residency article says data is on AWS, with EU or US residency. For European customers it says production data stays in the EEA and LLM analysis uses an EU residency instance, so meeting content for those customers is not sent outside the EEA for that analysis. The FAQ also says you can choose EU or US residency. The residency article says the region is set on the account and that you contact support or an account manager to choose or change it. Retention on the pricing page, as of this check: Basic, transcripts 3 months and audio 1 month, video shown as an em dash; Pro, transcripts 1 year and audio 6 months, video an em dash; Business, unlimited transcripts, audio 12 months, video 12 months; Enterprise, custom audio and video. Account deletion with “erase all data” has a 7-day recovery window. The FAQ says meeting content is not used to train models unless you opt in; the residency article says meeting content is not used to train external models.

Krisp says noise cancellation stays on the device. The cloud-data article says transcription processing data is deleted after processing, and that recordings are stored encrypted in the cloud. I did not find a default data-center region for Meeting AI. The pricing page lists a data-center location choice on Enterprise. Meetings can sit in Trash for 30 days and then be restored or permanently deleted. An admin auto-delete setting can remove them sooner. Storage help says Core is 10 GB per seat and Advanced is 60 GB per seat, not pooled.

6. Where official pages disagree

Fireflies video. The pricing comparison shows video capture at 720p on Pro and Business, and 1080p on Enterprise. The joins FAQ says Fireflies records video if you are on Business or higher, and only when the bot joins.

Fireflies CRM and integrations. The HubSpot marketing page describes the sync and does not name a plan. The pricing FAQ says app and CRM integrations are on premium tiers. Free-plan help says integrations are on Free.

Fireflies free transcription and client recaps. The joins FAQ says free transcription is available with the Chrome extension or mobile app, or with the unlimited-transcription toggle and sharing recaps with all invitees. The unlimited-transcriptions article describes auto-join and does not mention sharing recaps with all invitees.

MeetGeek Pro hours. A homepage paragraph says Pro at $9.99 per user per month raises the allowance to 20 hours. The pricing page’s comparison also lists Pro at 20 hours a month. The Pro cards on the homepage say “Unlimited recording.”

MeetGeek who receives the email. Getting started says everyone in the meeting is emailed by default, and that limiting recipients starts on Pro. The FAQ says nobody can access a meeting unless you share it, and that at first only participants can.

Krisp cloud and languages. The product page says notes work without cloud recordings, that audio is not sent to third parties, and that there are 17+ languages. Help says transcription is processed in the cloud, recordings are stored in the cloud, and the language list names 16 languages and does not include Japanese. The product page also answers “is it free?” with a free trial. The pricing page lists 7 days and no ongoing free plan.

Krisp Slack. The product page says the summary can be shared to a channel when the meeting ends. The Slack help article I read describes notifications and collaboration on action items, for Core, and does not describe that channel post.

7. How a consultancy might choose

This is a reading of the pages above, not a test.

Fireflies may fit a team that can accept a visible notetaker, or that will use the Meet extension or desktop Take Notes when a bot is unwelcome, and that wants HubSpot tasks and Slack action items described in official setup guides. The bot cannot be hidden. Video, CRM access, and who must receive a recap are described differently on different Fireflies pages.

MeetGeek may fit a team that wants both a bot and a path the FAQ calls invisible, EU or US residency, and HubSpot and Slack workflows. On Basic, getting started says you cannot yet stop the summary email from going to everyone in the meeting. The Pro hour cap is 20 hours on the pricing page and “unlimited recording” on the homepage cards.

Krisp may fit client calls where a bot is unwelcome, if the team uses device recording rather than Krisp Bot, and Core is enough for the HubSpot notes path. The default storage region is not stated. The product page and the help center do not agree about cloud recordings or the language count.
