How these tools record Zoom

Official pages describe two broad methods. A meeting bot joins as a participant, which other people can see, and which may wait in a lobby. A device or browser recorder captures audio, captions, or both from your computer, often without adding a guest. Some vendors offer both. I have not tested whether a “bot-free” or “invisible” mode is obvious to the other person, and several help articles still tell you to get consent.

Fireflies

Fireflies documents Zoom in several official places. The supported platforms article says Fireflies can “automatically join, record, and transcribe Zoom meetings.” A Zoom integration article says that as long as the Zoom meeting link is in the calendar invite, Fireflies (Fred) can detect it and join, and that you do not have to complete the Zoom integration for that to work. It says Fireflies “joins your meeting just like a participant would,” and that if Zoom security settings require it, the host may need to approve Fireflies the first time.

If you do not want a bot in the call, the desktop Take Notes article lists Zoom among conferencing platforms the desktop app can detect, and says this mode “captures meetings using system audio, so no Fireflies.ai Notetaker joins the meeting.”

As of 20 November 2026, list prices on the pricing page were Free at $0, Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually or $18 monthly, Business at $19 or $29, and Enterprise at $39 annual only. Official pages still disagree on Free-plan limits; that conflict is walked through in Fireflies.ai pricing explained. See also the Fireflies review.

Noota

Noota publishes a dedicated Zoom integration page. It says you can “record, transcribe and take notes of your Zoom meetings, with a bot that joins the call or without one, from the desktop app.” After you connect Google Calendar or Outlook, Noota “sees your upcoming Zoom meetings, and you choose meeting by meeting which ones to record.” The desktop path, that page says, records “with no bot in the call.” The pricing comparison table still marks Free as “Bot only,” and “Bot or invisible” as a Pro, Business, and Enterprise feature.

As of 20 November 2026, list prices were in euros: Free €0, Pro €19 per user per month billed annually or €29 monthly, and Business €39 or €49. The same pricing page still advertised 40% off the first year of a new annual Pro or Business plan, until 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time, with no code, and said there is no discount on monthly plans. The discounted first-year annual figures shown were €11.40 per user per month for Pro (€136.80 for the first year instead of €228) and €23.40 for Business (€280.80 instead of €468). Bluedot

Bluedot is the clearest bot-free-only option in this group. The homepage says it can “capture meetings on Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, and in-person,” and its FAQ answers “Does Bluedot work with Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams?” with yes: it “captures audio/video from your browser or desktop rather than joining as a bot,” and is “compatible with any platform, including Zoom, Google Meet, MS Teams, Webex, and even Slack huddles or phone calls.” As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page listed Free at $0 with 5 lifetime meetings; Basic at $14 per user per month billed annually or $18 monthly; Pro at $20 or $25; and Business at $32 or $39. Circleback

Circleback publishes a dedicated Zoom integration page. It says the bot “joins your Zoom meetings as a participant, automatically from your connected calendar or from a pasted link,” and that the host may need to admit it. The same page says the desktop app for macOS and Windows can record Zoom with no bot, by capturing your microphone and computer audio. If the bot’s join request is denied while you are on the call and you have the desktop app, Circleback says it prompts you to record from the desktop app. Recording with the bot or the desktop app, that page says, is included on every plan, including Free.

As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page yearly cards showed Free at $0, Pro at $15 per user per month, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35. The FAQ says that on annual billing, 50 users cost $9,000 a year on Pro, which matches $15 per user per month, and that every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings, with 30-day history on Free. I did not find a clearly labeled monthly Pro or Business dollar amount in the HTML I fetched. See the Circleback review.

Laxis

Laxis help on integrations says “the bot joins Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams meetings.” The same article says the Chrome extension “adds live transcripts, notes, and AI chat to Google Meet, and to Zoom and Teams in the browser,” and that Laxis also has an app that runs inside Zoom. Desktop help describes recording from the desktop app without the bot joining as a participant.

As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page listed Basic at $0 with 300 transcription minutes a month, a 45-minute cap per recording, and 30-day conversation history; Premium at $9.99 a month with 2,000 minutes and a 2-hour cap; and Business at $19.99 a month with unlimited transcription and a 4-hour cap. The page’s own meta description labels the Premium and Business figures as billed yearly. I did not find a separate monthly list price in the HTML I fetched. A FAQ says every new account starts with a 14-day free trial of Premium, with no credit card required, then moves to Basic. See the Laxis review.

Tactiq

Tactiq describes itself as an AI note taker for Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams. The homepage says you get live transcriptions without an AI bot joining the meeting. The download page lists a Chrome extension for Zoom in the browser, and a Mac app that transcribes Zoom desktop calls without the browser. That FAQ says the Mac app is limited to Zoom desktop calls for now. A help article on installing the extension says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions, and to keep captions turned on.

As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page listed Free at $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month; Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited transcripts and 10 AI credits; Team at $16.67 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited AI credits; and Business at $29.17 per user per month billed annually. A help article dated 30 June 2026 also lists monthly prices of $12, $20, and $40 for Pro, Team, and Business. See the Tactiq review, Tactiq vs Otter.ai, and Tactiq alternatives.

Zoom’s own notes: ZoomMate and My Notes

Zoom is not an affiliate on this site. This section uses only facts from zoom.com pages fetched on 20 November 2026. The product URL zoom.com/en/products/ai-assistant/ is titled “Meet ZoomMate, your AI teammate.” I did not find “AI Companion” in the visible product copy on that page.

A linked feature page, Zoom My Notes, is titled “Zoom My Notes: Your new AI note taker.” It says My Notes “captures insights from your conversations — on Zoom, in-person, on mobile, and across third-party platforms,” and that it can “automatically take notes across Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, and other third-party meeting platforms — no bots required.” The how-to section says you can start a meeting in Zoom, or join one on Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, or in person, and click the My Notes icon. After the meeting, it says My Notes organizes notes and a transcript into a summary with key takeaways and action items. The same page says “My Notes is sold as a stand alone license or part of Zoom Workplace and ZoomMate license,” and that it may not be available in all regions or industry verticals.

The ZoomMate pricing section on the AI assistant page lists ZoomMate Basic as limited features included with Workplace Basic, at no charge. That card lists meeting summaries for 3 hosted meetings per month, in-meeting questions for 3 hosted meetings per month, “AI note-taking (My Notes) for Zoom and third-party meeting platforms (3 uses per month),” 20 AI queries per month, and a 40-minute meeting limit with 100 participants. The paid ZoomMate card lists unlimited AI note-taking, unlimited custom meeting summary templates, and 2,200 AI credits per user per month, with annual and monthly billing toggles. Structured data on the same page lists a paid offer at $16.67 per user per month, billed annually. I could not read a clearly labeled monthly dollar amount next to the paid card in the static HTML I fetched. Check the live Zoom page before you buy.

FAQ

Which AI note takers work with Zoom?

Official pages checked on 20 November 2026 describe Zoom capture for Fireflies, Noota, Bluedot, Circleback, Laxis, and Tactiq, and also for Otter, MeetGeek, Krisp, tl;dv, Granola, Fathom, Sembly, and Read.ai. Zoom’s own My Notes page says it can capture Zoom meetings as well as Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, and in-person conversations. The way each tool records differs: some send a bot into the call, some record from a Chrome extension or a desktop app, and some offer both.

Can I record a Zoom meeting without a bot joining the call?

Yes, several official pages describe a no-bot path. Fireflies documents a desktop Take Notes mode that captures system audio, including Zoom, without the Fireflies.ai Notetaker joining as a participant. Noota’s Zoom page says you can record from the desktop app with no bot in the call; the pricing table marks that mode as Pro and above. Bluedot’s FAQ says it captures audio or video from your browser or desktop rather than joining as a bot, including on Zoom. Circleback documents a bot-free desktop app for macOS and Windows that can record Zoom. Laxis help says a desktop app and a Chrome extension can capture Zoom in the browser. Tactiq’s official pages say no bot joins, and describe a Chrome or Edge extension for Zoom in the browser plus a Mac app for Zoom desktop calls. Zoom’s own My Notes feature page says notes can be taken across Zoom and third-party platforms with no bots required. I have not tested whether any of those paths is obvious to the other person on the call.

Does Zoom have its own AI note taker?

As of 20 November 2026, Zoom’s official AI assistant page is titled ZoomMate. A linked feature page describes My Notes as Zoom’s AI note taker, including on Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, and in-person meetings, with no bots required. The free Basic card lists limited monthly use, including 3 My Notes uses per month. Structured data on the ZoomMate page lists a paid annual offer at $16.67 per user per month billed annually. Check the current Zoom page before you buy.

Which AI note taker is best for Zoom?

Official pages do not support a single winner, and this page is not a hands-on test. Fireflies documents calendar-based auto-join for Zoom and a desktop path without a bot. Noota publishes a dedicated Zoom integration page and marks bot-free desktop recording as a paid-plan feature. Bluedot is the clearest bot-free-only option among the tools with an approved affiliate link. Circleback and Laxis document both a bot and a no-bot path. Tactiq is a bot-free Chrome or Edge extension, with a Mac app for Zoom desktop, a $0 plan, and an $8 annual Pro seat, if you can keep captions on.
