How each one records

Circleback’s homepage FAQ, “Can I use Circleback without a bot?”, says yes: you can download the desktop app for macOS and Windows to record meetings without a bot joining your calls. By default it records meeting video, and what is shown on screen — the FAQ names slides, dashboards, and docs — makes it into the notes. Circleback can also send a bot. The pricing cards list “a bot or the bot-free desktop app for online meetings,” and “mobile and Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings.” The homepage says Circleback supports in-person meetings and names Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, WebEx, Slack huddles, and GoTo, and that it connects with Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook Calendar to detect and automatically join meetings. You can also import past meetings from Fathom, Fireflies, Granola, and Read AI, or from other apps with the Circleback API, according to that homepage FAQ.

Tactiq’s site describes a different capture model. The product navigation on the pricing page says you can transcribe meetings in real time with no bot, names Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams, and points to a “bot-free Tactiq extension.” The pricing FAQ says the transcription is only displayed for you, but you can notify everyone that you are transcribing during the meeting. I have not used the extension, and I am not describing how captions are captured beyond what those official pages say.

Free plans

Circleback Free is $0. The card includes AI meeting notes and action items, Ask Circleback, view and draft emails, online and in-person meetings, a bot or the bot-free desktop app, mobile and Apple Watch apps, speaker-labeled transcripts, automatic tagging and sharing, limited automations, 30-day meeting and recording history, and API, MCP, and CLI access, plus 2 teams. The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings.

Tactiq Free is $0, labeled free forever, with no credit card, for 1 user. The pricing cards list 5 AI credits a month, 10 transcripts a month, AI-generated insights, share and export transcripts, and in-meeting AI. The pricing FAQ says one AI credit covers a single AI action — generating a meeting summary, drafting action items, or asking Tactiq AI a question — and that when you hit a transcript or AI-credit limit, Tactiq keeps transcribing, but AI features pause until the limit resets at the start of the next month, or until you upgrade. Existing transcripts stay accessible. The help article adds that unused credits do not roll over, and that credits reset on the same day you signed up.

Paid prices, as of 21 November 2026

Circleback’s pricing page had the Yearly tab selected. The visible cards listed Pro at $15, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month. The same HTML includes a billingPlanPrices list in US cents: Pro $18 billed monthly or $180 a year; Business $29 billed monthly or $300 a year; and Enterprise $420 a year, with no monthly interval for Enterprise in that list. The annual amounts match the Yearly cards. The pricing FAQ says that on annual billing, 50 users cost $9,000 a year on Pro, $15,000 on Business, and $21,000 on Enterprise, and that Business and Enterprise include unlimited free viewers. A plan-by-plan walkthrough is in Circleback pricing explained.

Tactiq’s pricing page at tactiq.io/buy is currency-localized. From this check location the visible annual cards were priced in Japanese yen: Pro ¥1,249, Team ¥2,541, and Business ¥4,800 per user per month, plus Free at ¥0. A separate fetch of the same URL showed the USD annual cards as Pro $8, Team $16.67, and Business $29.17 per user per month. Tactiq’s help article “How to upgrade your Tactiq plan,” also checked on 21 November 2026, lists USD prices of Pro $8 per user per month billed annually, or USD $12 billed monthly (USD $96 a year), and Team $16.67 billed annually, or USD $20 billed monthly (USD $200 a year). That help article lists Business at $29.16 per user per month billed annually, one cent below the $29.17 on the USD pricing cards. I am quoting both official figures rather than combining them. The help article also says you save 33% on Pro and 17% on Team with annual billing.

Who should pick which

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested accuracy, captions, or the desktop app.

Look at Tactiq if you want a stated bot-free browser extension for Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams, a lower yearly Pro list price, and you can work within AI-credit caps on Free and Pro, or pay for Team when you want unlimited AI credits. Look at Circleback if you want a stated unlimited-meeting free plan, a desktop app that can record without a bot, optional bot join, in-person capture on mobile or Apple Watch, and you can accept 30 days of history until you pay. Japanese is named on Circleback’s homepage language list. I did not find a language list on Tactiq’s pricing page or on the upgrade help article I opened today, so I am not making a language claim for Tactiq here.

FAQ

Can I record without a bot?

Circleback’s homepage FAQ says yes: a desktop app for macOS and Windows records without a bot. By default it records meeting video, and what is on screen goes into the notes. Circleback can also send a bot. Tactiq’s site describes a bot-free Chrome extension that transcribes Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams with no bot. This page has not tested either product.

Which free plan records more meetings?

Circleback’s pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings. The Free card limits history to 30 days. Tactiq Free allows 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month. The plans are not equivalent.

Which costs less, Circleback or Tactiq?

On 21 November 2026, Circleback Pro was $15 per user per month on the Yearly cards, or $18 billed monthly in the page-source list. Tactiq’s help article lists Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, or USD $12 billed monthly. On those yearly Pro figures, Tactiq Pro is the lower list price. Tactiq Pro still caps AI credits at 10 a month. Check both live pages before you buy.
