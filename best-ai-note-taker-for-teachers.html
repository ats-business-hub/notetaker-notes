Best AI note taker for teachers: education plans, lectures, and classroom use

This guide compares six AI note takers for teachers using each vendor’s official pricing, feature, security and help pages, checked on 27 November 2026. It is not a hands-on classroom test. The “See plans” buttons are affiliate links, and I may earn a commission at no extra cost to you. Check your school’s policy on recording and student data before using any of these tools. #ad

Teachers use meeting-note tools differently from sales teams or freelancers. A 50-minute lecture runs longer than a quick check-in call. A classroom is a room, not a Zoom link. Department meetings, parent conferences and professional-development sessions each have their own privacy expectations. This guide looks only at the published plan facts that matter in those situations: education pricing, caps on recording length, in-person capture and storage time.

It does not rank tools on transcription accuracy, because no hands-on testing was done for this page. Every price and limit below comes from the vendor's official pages and is listed in the sources section. If you mainly want the lowest price, see Cheapest AI Meeting Note Takers in 2026 and Fireflies vs MeetGeek vs Krisp Free Plans (2026).

What teachers actually need from an AI note taker

Four questions sort out most of the options:

  1. Is there official education pricing? Few vendors print one. A discount that exists only in a sales conversation can't be planned around.
  2. Can it record a full lecture? Some plans cap the length of a single recording. A cap of 45 or 60 minutes can cut off a double period.
  3. Does it work in a physical room? A Chrome extension that transcribes Google Meet won't help in a classroom with no laptop call. Look for a mobile app or bot-free desktop recording.
  4. How long are notes kept? A 30-day history can't hold a semester of lectures. If you need notes until finals, check storage and retention.

For in-person capture in general, the dedicated guide is Best AI Note Takers for In-Person Meetings (2026). For transcribing files you already have, see Best AI Note Takers for Recorded Files (2026).

Which tools publish education pricing

Of the six tools here, three mention education pricing on an official pricing or help page:

  • Tactiq: The pricing page at tactiq.io offers special pricing for teachers and students through an education-plan application. It lists no price or discount amount. Tactiq's help center says education discounts need approval through a school-issued email (help.tactiq.io).
  • Circleback: The pricing page at circleback.ai lists a 50% education discount for students. It's applied automatically when a student signs up with a school email, or students can contact support with proof of status. The page doesn't say whether teachers or staff qualify, so ask Circleback before you rely on it.
  • Laxis: The FAQ on laxis.com mentions specialized pricing for non-profits and educational institutions, handled by the partnership team. No amount is listed.

Fireflies, Noota and Bluedot show no education discount on their official pricing pages. Teachers looking at those three should plan around list prices.

Tactiq: online staff meetings and virtual classes

Tactiq is a bot-free Chrome extension. It transcribes Google Meet, Zoom and Microsoft Teams in real time, and no bot joins the call (tactiq.io). That suits online staff meetings, virtual office hours and remote parent conferences, where a visible bot in the participant list can feel intrusive. Tactiq's homepage says it doesn't record or store meeting audio (tactiq.io).

Official plans (tactiq.io), per user per month, billed annually: - Free: $0, 10 meeting transcripts and 5 AI credits per month - Pro: $8, unlimited transcripts, 10 AI credits per month - Team (1–20 users): $16.67, unlimited transcripts and AI credits - Business (20–200 users): $29.17 - Enterprise (200+ users): custom pricing

The education plan is the main reason Tactiq stands out for teachers. You apply for it, and the price isn't published, so confirm the terms before committing a department budget. Tactiq's help center says unused AI credits don't roll over (help.tactiq.io).

The official pages don't describe capturing a lecture in a physical classroom. Tactiq fits teachers whose meetings mostly happen in a browser. Integrations include Google Docs, Notion, Confluence and Slack (tactiq.io), which helps if your department keeps shared minutes in Google Docs.

Circleback: in-person and online, with a published student discount

Circleback can send a bot to online calls. It also offers a bot-free desktop app for macOS and Windows and lets you record in-person meetings, including from a mobile app (circleback.ai). Supported platforms include Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack huddles and GoTo. The homepage lists more than 100 languages (circleback.ai).

Official plans (circleback.ai), per user per month: - Free: $0, unlimited meetings, 30-day meeting and recording history - Pro: $15, unlimited meeting history, 1 year of recording storage - Business: $25, 2 years of recording storage, admin roles, custom data retention - Enterprise: $35, SSO/SCIM, unlimited recording storage, HIPAA compliance

The free plan's unlimited meetings let you try it across several weeks of staff meetings. The 30-day history is the catch for lecture archives: a full term of notes needs Pro or higher. The 50% education discount is printed for students. A teacher should ask Circleback whether staff qualify.

Circleback's security documentation says it is SOC 2 Type II certified, encrypts data at rest and in transit, and doesn't use customer data to train models (circleback.ai). The page doesn't mention FERPA. A school handling student records should raise that with the vendor directly.

Fireflies: large free storage, education DSA on Enterprise

Fireflies offers a meeting assistant that joins video calls, plus desktop, Chrome extension, iOS and Android apps (fireflies.ai). Supported platforms include Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams and Webex.

Official plans (fireflies.ai), per seat per month, billed annually: - Free: $0, 400 mins/team of storage, 20 AI credits - Pro: $10, 8,000 mins/seat of storage - Business: $19, unlimited storage - Enterprise: $39

With the page's Monthly toggle, Pro is $18 and Business $29 per seat per month; Enterprise is annual-only at $39 (fireflies.ai). Check the comparison table on that page for current AI-credit allowances on paid plans.

For schools, the most relevant official detail is compliance. Fireflies says a BAA for healthcare and a DSA for education are available on Enterprise with Private Storage (fireflies.ai). That suits a district-level purchase more than an individual teacher on Pro. Fireflies shows no education discount.

Noota: EU hosting and bot-free desktop recording

Noota records with a bot (Google Meet, Teams, Zoom) or without one through its desktop app. Its mobile app captures in-person conversations (noota.io). Default hosting is in the EU, and there's an optional sovereign mode hosted in France (noota.io). That may matter to European schools that need data kept inside the EU.

Official plans (noota.io), per user, EUR: - Free: €0, unlimited meeting notes, 1 month of storage, 10 AI credits per user per month; live and standard recordings capped at 300 minutes per month unless a connected calendar has "Join all meetings" enabled - Pro: €29 monthly / €19 annually, 6 months of storage, 100 AI credits per user per month - Business: €49 monthly / €39 annually, unlimited storage and AI credits - Enterprise: custom, volume pricing for 50+ seats

Six months of storage on Pro covers roughly one semester. Teachers who want notes kept longer would need Business. Noota shows no education discount.

Bluedot: bot-free capture with lifetime free trial meetings

Bluedot records without a bot, using a Chrome extension or desktop app for online meetings and mobile apps or Apple Watch for in-person conversations. It also accepts MP4, MP3 and M4A uploads (bluedothq.com). Supported platforms include Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Slack and Webex.

Official plans (bluedothq.com), per user per month, annual / monthly billing: - Free: 5 meetings per lifetime - Basic: $14 / $18, audio only, unlimited meetings, max 1 hour per recording - Pro: $20 / $25, unlimited meetings with video, unlimited recording duration - Business: $32 / $39 - Flat rate: price on request

The lower figure in each pair is the Annual tab and the higher one the Monthly tab. For teachers, the cap matters: Basic's 1-hour maximum per recording can cut off a long lecture or a double period. Pro removes that cap. Bluedot's FAQ says it charges only for users who record meetings. Bluedot shows no education discount.

Laxis: in-person recording with per-recording length caps

Laxis records bot-free on your device, with an optional bot. It captures in-person conversations through its mobile app and other hardware, and accepts file uploads (laxis.com). It lists support for 100+ languages.

Official plans (laxis.com): - Basic: $0, 300 min/month of transcription, 45 min max per recording, 30 days of conversation storage - Premium: $9.99/month billed yearly, 2,000 min/month, 2 hours max per recording, unlimited storage - Business: $19.99/month billed yearly, unlimited transcription, 4 hours max per recording - Enterprise: custom

These per-recording caps matter for lectures. Basic's 45-minute limit is shorter than many class periods. Premium's 2-hour limit covers most single lectures. On monthly billing, Laxis's billing help page lists Premium at $15.99 and Business at $29.99 a month (help.laxis.com). The pricing-page FAQ mentions specialized pricing for educational institutions through its partnership team, with no amount published.

How to choose by teaching scenario

Online department and staff meetings. Tactiq's bot-free extension for Meet, Zoom and Teams is a lightweight fit, and it's the only tool here whose pricing page invites teachers to apply for an education plan.

Recording your own lectures in a classroom. Look at tools whose official pages describe in-person capture: Circleback, Noota, Bluedot and Laxis. Then check recording-length caps. Laxis Basic stops at 45 minutes and Bluedot Basic at 1 hour. Laxis Premium (2 hours) and Bluedot Pro (unlimited duration) remove most of that risk.

Keeping a full term of notes. Free tiers with 30-day or 1-month history (Circleback Free, Laxis Basic, Noota Free) won't hold a semester. Circleback Pro (1 year of recording storage), Noota Business (unlimited storage) and Laxis Premium (unlimited conversation storage) are the plans with longer official retention.

District or school-wide purchase. Compliance paperwork matters more than per-seat price here. Fireflies says a DSA for education is available on Enterprise with Private Storage. For other vendors, ask directly.

Students rather than teachers. Circleback's 50% student discount is the clearest published offer.

Before you record a class: policy and consent

An AI note taker doesn't replace your school's recording policy. Many schools and districts set rules on recording students, storing their voices and using third-party software with student data. Bluedot's own site recommends telling participants when an AI note-taker is in use, since rules vary by region (bluedothq.com). Check with your administration before you turn on recording in a classroom. This article is not legal advice.
