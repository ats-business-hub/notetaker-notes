Who it is for, and who should skip it

Bluedot may fit a small team that does not want a notetaker bot in the participant list. The homepage says recording uses a Chrome extension or a desktop app on Mac or Windows, and that you do not have to be the host. The same pages describe Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Slack, Webex, phone calls, and in-person notes on iPhone, Android, or Apple Watch.

Skip it if you need an ongoing free plan for new recordings. The free card is 5 meetings for the life of the account, then view and share only. Skip it if a one-hour cap is too short and you do not want Pro: Basic is audio only and lists a maximum of 1 hour per recording. Skip it if you need named CRM or ATS sync before the Business plan. The Business card is the one that says CRM and ATS integrations. The homepage shows logos for tools such as HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Greenhouse, and Workday, and it does not say which plan includes each logo.

How the official pages say it works

The homepage headline calls Bluedot an invisible, bot-free note taker. The FAQ says it never invites a bot and never asks for calendar access. It captures audio or video from the browser or desktop. Video is optional. A separate homepage line says “Click to try — no account required.” I have not tried that path, and the pricing page still describes a free account with a meeting cap.

Online meetings on the product page are Meet, Zoom, Teams, and Slack. The FAQ adds Webex, Slack huddles, and phone calls, and says platform compatibility comes from capturing on your device rather than joining as a bot. In-person notes are on the iOS and Android apps. Phone calls are described as calls you make from Bluedot using your own phone number. The pages do not spell out carrier setup.

On consent, the FAQ says Bluedot does not “announce” itself the way a bot does, that privacy laws vary by region, and that Bluedot recommends telling participants you are using an AI assistant for notes. This page is not legal advice. Recording rules vary by state and by country.

Plans and pricing

These are the list prices on the Bluedot pricing page on 19 November 2026. Each paid plan appears twice, both times as a per-user monthly amount. The Annual control is the one marked selected in the page HTML, and the first card row lists the lower prices. The higher prices sit in a second row. I am reading the lower row as annual and the higher row as monthly because of that markup. The feature lists on the two rows match. The FAQ’s prorate example uses the Pro price of $25, which is the higher of the two Pro prices. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

  • Free: $0. The card says “Start free, forever” and “5 meetings / lifetime.” It also lists the Chrome extension, desktop and mobile apps, and Slack, Notion, Zapier, and Make.
  • Basic: $14 or $18 per user per month. “The essentials, audio only.” Unlimited meetings (audio only), unlimited storage, max 1 hour per recording, webhook access, and MCP integration.
  • Pro: $20 or $25 per user per month. “No limits, with video.” Everything in Basic, plus unlimited meetings with video, unlimited recording duration, custom note templates, and Zoom and Google Drive imports.
  • Business: $32 or $39 per user per month. Everything in Pro, plus CRM and ATS integrations, advanced admin controls, SSO and SCIM, custom data retention, org-wide MCP and webhooks, and unlimited audio and video imports.
  • Flat rate: “One fixed price for your company, no matter how many people use Bluedot.” The page says to request pricing. It does not publish a number.

The pricing FAQ says Bluedot charges only users who record meetings, not every person in the workspace. Added paid users are billed at a prorated amount for the rest of the cycle. Payment is per workspace. If you cancel, you lose access to recordings. You have one month to restore the subscription. After that, the FAQ says recordings are permanently deleted. “Import” means uploading existing recordings from your computer, Google Drive, or other sources.

Because the price is per recording user, five people who all record would be five times the per-user list price. The FAQ’s own example is a Pro license at $25, then a second license added halfway through the month at $12.50. People who do not record are not charged, according to that FAQ. The page does not define a separate free-viewer role.

That strip is shorter than “100+.” The page does not publish the rest of the list.
  • AI chat across meetings, described on the homepage as questions and search over transcripts.
  • Uploads. The homepage FAQ says you can upload MP4, MP3, M4A, and other common video and audio formats, and that Bluedot transcribes the file and writes a summary in any of the 100+ languages. It does not name the other formats.
  • Apps listed in the site menu: Chrome extension, Mac, Windows, iOS, Android, and Apple Watch. Connectors for Claude, ChatGPT, and MCP are also in that menu. The pricing cards put MCP on Basic and “org-wide MCP and webhooks” on Business.
  • Sharing and permissions. The homepage says you can share with colleagues and control who sees a meeting. It does not publish a permission matrix.
  • Integrations. The pricing free card names Slack, Notion, Zapier, and Make. Business names “CRM & ATS integrations” without listing products. The homepage shows logos for Greenhouse, Zapier, Notion, Slack, Google Drive, Google Calendar, HubSpot, Make, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Copper, Zoho, and Workday.

Pros and cons from those pages

These are not scores. They are what the official pages say, and what they leave out.

Pros

  • The product pages describe recording without a bot, including when you are not the host, on Meet, Zoom, Teams, and the other platforms named above.
  • The privacy policy says Bluedot does not use customer content to train AI models. The security page says the same, and adds that AI providers do not retain data after generation.
  • Customer content is stored and processed in the European Union by default. U.S. storage is a requested arrangement, not the default.
  • The security page lists SOC 2 Type II, GDPR-related controls, AES-256, TLS, AWS hosting, annual penetration testing, SSO, SAML, and SCIM.
  • The pricing FAQ says you pay for users who record, and added seats are prorated.

Cons

  • Free recording stops after 5 lifetime meetings. The card still says “Start free, forever,” and the homepage FAQ says Bluedot is not a free AI meeting note taker.
  • Basic is audio only and caps a recording at 1 hour. Video and unlimited duration start on Pro.
  • CRM and ATS, SSO, SCIM, and custom retention are on Business, not on the cheaper cards.
  • Cancellation removes access to recordings immediately, with one month to restore them before permanent deletion.
  • Bluedot does not announce itself in the meeting. You have to tell people yourself. The FAQ says laws vary.
  • The homepage says Bluedot never asks for calendar access. The privacy policy describes calendar data if you connect Google Calendar or similar tools. Those two sentences are not the same.
  • Deletion backups are 30 days on the security page and 90 days in the privacy policy. The security page also says deleted data is removed “immediately,” while the privacy policy says “promptly.”

Privacy and security, including the conflicts

The privacy policy is effective 8 January 2026. Bluedot says it is the controller for account and website data, and the processor for customer content such as recordings and transcripts. Section 9.1 says customer content is stored and processed in the European Union by default. A customer can request United States storage by an agreed arrangement. Account, billing, support, and website data may be processed in other countries. Integrations you turn on follow those other companies’ terms.

The security page says meeting data is encrypted, stored in AWS, and never used to train AI models. It lists SOC 2 Type II and GDPR. It does not name a default region in the deletion blurb. A security FAQ later says Bluedot supports EU data residency and offers a data processing agreement. That FAQ fits the privacy policy’s EU default. The backup window does not.

Security page: “Deleted data is removed immediately, with backups cleared within 30 days.” Privacy policy section 6.3: when customer content is deleted, Bluedot removes it from active systems promptly and removes remaining backup copies within 90 days. I am not choosing one number. Both are on official pages as of this check.

Google API data is covered by Google’s Limited Use rules, according to the privacy policy. The policy also says Bluedot does not sell that data or use it to train models. It does not say Bluedot stores voice profiles. I am not adding a claim the policy does not make.

FAQ

Does Bluedot put a bot in the meeting?

The homepage says Bluedot does not invite a bot. It records with a Chrome extension or a desktop app, and the FAQ says you do not need to be the host. The same FAQ says it works with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack huddles, and phone calls because it captures from the browser or desktop. This page has not tested that.

Is Bluedot free?

The pricing card says Start free, forever at $0 and lists 5 meetings per lifetime. The homepage FAQ says Bluedot is not a free AI meeting note taker, that those 5 meetings are a lifetime trial of core features, and that after that a free member can view and share recordings or upgrade to record more.

How much does Bluedot cost?

On 19 November 2026 the pricing page showed two prices for each paid plan, both labeled per user per month. The first card row, with the Annual control marked selected, listed Basic at $14, Pro at $20, and Business at $32. A second row listed Basic at $18, Pro at $25, and Business at $39. A separate Flat rate option says to request a company price. The FAQ says Bluedot charges only users who record, and its prorate example uses the $25 Pro price. Check the pricing page before you buy.

Does Bluedot support Japanese?

The homepage says transcription and summaries are available in 100+ languages, and Japanese is one of the language names shown on that page. The upload FAQ also says summaries can be generated in any of those 100+ languages. This page has not tested accuracy.

Where does Bluedot store meetings?

The privacy policy, effective 8 January 2026, says customer content is stored and processed in the European Union by default, and that U.S. storage is available by an agreed arrangement. The security page says data is stored in AWS and does not name a region in its main deletion section, though a security FAQ says Bluedot supports EU data residency. Deleted customer content is described two ways: the security page says deleted data is removed immediately and backups are cleared within 30 days; the privacy policy says active systems are cleared promptly and backup copies within 90 days.

Does Bluedot need calendar access?

The homepage FAQ says Bluedot never asks for calendar access. The privacy policy says that if you connect a service such as Google Calendar, Bluedot may receive calendar event details, including title, time, and participants. The homepage FAQ does not describe that optional connection.
