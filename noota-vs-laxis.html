How each one records

Laxis’s homepage says the product transcribes every meeting into polished AI notes, tasks, and reports — “bot-free, in 100+ languages.” I am repeating that official wording; I have not watched a Laxis recorder on a call.

Noota’s AI note-taker page says Noota takes notes of online meetings, phone calls, and in-person conversations, “with or without a bot.” The same page’s FAQ says Noota records Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, and Webex meetings, with a bot that joins the call or without one from the desktop app, and that in-person meetings and phone calls are covered by the mobile app and the built-in recorder. The pricing FAQ says Noota can join as a bot with the name you choose, or record quietly from your computer with the desktop app on the Pro plan and above, and that participants are notified that the meeting is recorded. The pricing comparison table marks recording mode on Free as “Bot only,” and on Pro, Business, and Enterprise as “Bot or invisible.”

Free plans

Laxis’s pricing FAQ says every new account starts with a 14-day free trial of Premium, with no credit card required, and that when the trial ends you move to the free Basic plan and keep all your notes. The Basic card is $0 and lists 300 minutes of transcription a month, 30 days of conversation history, and recording of online and in-person meetings. The comparison table lists a 45-minute cap per recording on Basic. The pricing FAQ and the billing help article both say meetings older than 30 days on Basic are archived, not deleted, and that upgrading unlocks them again. The billing help article also says meeting transcription, Voice Keyboard dictation, and live translation share the same monthly minutes.

Noota’s Free card is €0, with no card needed, and lists unlimited meeting notes with an asterisk. The footnote, repeated in the FAQ, says unlimited recording on the Free plan requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live recordings and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. The Free card also lists one month of storage. The products are not equivalent, and I am not declaring a single winner from those different caps.

Paid prices, as of 21 November 2026

Laxis’s pricing page has Monthly and Yearly toggles. On the Yearly cards I saved, Premium is $9.99 per month and Business is $19.99 per month. The official billing help article, “Billing & Plans,” lists Premium at $15.99 a month or $9.99 yearly ($119.88 a year), and Business at $29.99 a month or $19.99 yearly ($239.88 a year). The pricing FAQ says website purchases are billed in USD through Stripe, while in-app purchases use Apple or Google regional price tiers. I am quoting both official wordings rather than collapsing them into one figure.

Noota’s pricing page says all prices are in euros (EUR). Pro is €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. Business is €39 billed annually, or €49 billed monthly. A banner and the plan cards described Black Friday as 40% off any new annual Pro or Business plan until Monday, November 30, 11:59 pm (Paris time), which brought the first-year annual rates on the cards I saved to €11.40 per user per month (€136.80 for the first year instead of €228) and €23.40 (€280.80 instead of €468). The page says there is no discount on the monthly plan. I have not converted euros to dollars.

Who should pick which

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested accuracy, the bot, or the desktop recorder.

Look at Laxis if you want a product whose official homepage calls itself bot-free, prices in U.S. dollars, a 14-day Premium trial with no credit card, and a $0 Basic plan with a monthly minute pool that is also shared with Voice Keyboard, as the official pages state. Look at Noota if you want prices and hosting described in European terms (the pricing and AI note-taker pages say meeting data is hosted in Europe, with a France option, and that data is not used to train AI models; the pricing page also lists ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II), a first-year Black Friday annual rate that was still on the page as of this check, and you can accept a bot-only Free plan, or pay for Pro when you want the desktop “invisible” mode.

FAQ

Can I record without a bot?

Laxis’s homepage presents the product as bot-free. Noota’s pricing table marks bot-free (“invisible”) recording on Pro and above; Free is marked bot-only. This page has not tested either product.

Which free plan records more meetings?

Laxis Basic is 300 minutes a month, with a 45-minute cap per recording and 30 days of history (older meetings are archived, not deleted). Noota’s Free unlimited line depends on a connected calendar, Join all meetings, and auto-sharing the summary; otherwise the cap is 300 minutes a month. I am not declaring a single winner from those different conditions.

Which costs less, Noota or Laxis?

They publish prices in different currencies, and I have not converted them. As of 21 November 2026, Noota Pro was €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly, with a first-year Black Friday annual rate of €11.40 still on the page through the dated offer ending Monday, November 30. Laxis Premium was $9.99 per month on the Yearly cards, or $15.99 monthly in the billing help article. Check both live pages before you buy.
