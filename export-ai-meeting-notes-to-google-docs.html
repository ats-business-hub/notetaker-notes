How to export AI meeting notes to Google Docs

Most AI meeting note takers can get notes into Google Docs, but the route differs: a direct integration, a file you upload, or copy and paste. This guide covers Fireflies, Otter, Fathom, and tl;dv, based on each vendor’s help pages checked on 26 November 2026. It is not a hands-on test. Menu labels and plan limits change, so confirm in the vendor’s current help before relying on a step.

Fireflies: Google Docs integration

Fireflies’ help says that once connected, it saves meeting summaries (on by default) and, if you turn it on, full transcripts as Google Docs in your Drive.

  1. In Fireflies, open Integrations, search for Google Docs, and click Connect.
  2. Sign in with the Google account for your Drive and click Continue to grant access.
  3. Choose Save Meeting Summary and/or Save Meeting Transcript.
  4. Click Create a sample summary to check that a test document appears, then finish the setup and click Save.
  • Where files go: Fireflies Meetings > Summaries and Fireflies Meetings > Transcripts. Fireflies says you can’t change the destination folder, but you can move files in Drive afterwards.
  • Which meetings: every meeting by default. Integration Rules (filter by title, host, participants, or meeting type) are on Business or higher plans.
  • Past meetings: meetings recorded after you connect are sent automatically. For earlier ones, use Push past meetings on the Google Docs integration page and pick the last 7, 14, 30, 60, or 90 days, or a custom range.
  • Teams: Fireflies lists admin access as a prerequisite for team-wide use.

Otter: export to Google Drive, or export a file

Export to Google Drive

  1. Go to Integrations and click Connect next to Google Drive. Sign in and click Continue.
  2. In the Google Drive settings, choose Google Docs or .txt, and what to include: Metadata, Overview, Action Items, Insights, Outline, Transcript, Custom Prompt, Audio.
  3. Open a conversation, click the three-dot menu, and select Export to Google Drive.
  • Exports go to a folder named Otter.ai Conversations, which you can rename or move.
  • Exports are manual by default. Otter says Enterprise admins can ask their Otter account manager to turn on automatic export, with filters for all, internal-only, or external-only meetings.
  • If you edit the conversation in Otter, click Update to replace the Drive file. Later edits are not synced automatically.
  • You can export conversations created before you connected Drive. Images are not included, and bulk export to Drive is not supported. The integration is on web and desktop only.

Otter’s Drive article doesn’t state a plan requirement for manual export. Its general export article says Enterprise plans include integrations for exporting to third-party platforms. Check what your Integrations page shows; see Otter pricing.

Export a file, then open it in Google Docs

Open the conversation, click the three dots, and select Export. Otter lists TXT, DOCX, PDF, SRT, and Clipboard. Otter says Basic plan users can export only TXT; paid plans get all formats. Bulk export of multiple conversations is on Business and higher plans.

Fathom: copy and paste

  • Summary: in a recorded call, click Copy Summary above the summary. Fathom copies it formatted, with or without hyperlinks. Paste it into a Google Doc.
  • Transcript: click Copy Transcript above the transcript and paste. Fathom says transcripts can’t be downloaded directly.

The Fathom help pages we checked don’t describe an automatic Google Docs export, so we don’t claim one. See Fathom pricing.

tl;dv: Google Docs integration

tl;dv’s Google Docs page says to open the integrations tab in the side menu, then pick a featured template or build a custom workflow. Its Google Meet to Google Docs page describes syncing meeting notes, action items, and full transcripts to Google Drive. tl;dv’s page doesn’t list plan requirements, so check in the app. See tl;dv review and tl;dv pricing.

No integration? Upload and convert

  1. Export the notes as DOCX or TXT from your note taker.
  2. Upload the file to Google Drive.
  3. Open it with Google Docs. Google says converting a file from another program creates a copy in Docs format. In Drive settings you can also turn on Convert uploaded files to Google Docs editor format.

DOCX keeps headings and lists better. TXT is plain and predictable. For a quick one-off, copying and pasting into a new document works too.

Keep exported notes tidy

  • Use one naming pattern, for example “2026-11-26 Client name – Weekly sync”.
  • Keep summaries and full transcripts in separate folders. Fireflies does this by default.
  • Check who already has access to a Drive folder before you save notes into it or share it.
  • Review AI summaries before sending them. They can get names, numbers, or decisions wrong. Our guide on turning AI notes into action items helps here.

Common problems

  • Can’t find the document: look in Fireflies Meetings or Otter.ai Conversations in the Drive of the Google account you connected.
  • Export option missing (Otter): Otter says to connect Google Drive in Integrations first. A workspace admin may also have disabled exports.
  • Older meetings missing (Fireflies): use Push past meetings.
  • Edits not showing (Otter): click Update after editing. Drive files don’t sync automatically.

FAQ

Can AI meeting notes go straight to Google Docs?

Yes for Fireflies (Google Docs integration), Otter (Export to Google Drive as a Google Doc), and tl;dv (Google Docs integration). For Fathom, the help pages we checked describe copy and paste.

Can I export without a paid plan?

It depends on the tool. Otter’s Basic plan can export TXT, which you can upload to Drive. Fathom’s copy buttons don’t list a plan limit in the pages we checked. Check current plan pages before you rely on it.

Does editing the Google Doc change the notes in the app?

No. The Doc is a copy. Otter also says edits made in Otter don’t update an exported Drive file until you click Update.
