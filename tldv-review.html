What official pages say tl;dv is

The official homepage FAQ (dateModified 13 November 2026) says tl;dv is “an AI meeting assistant and AI note taker. It records your meetings, writes the notes, transcript and action items, and sends them to the tools your team already uses, from your CRM to any LLM over MCP.” It also says you can start for free and keep using the free plan, and that “Your meetings are never used to train AI models.” I have not verified those claims in use.

How it records meetings (bot and no-bot)

Official pages describe both a meeting bot and a bot-free desktop recorder:

  • The homepage FAQ says: “The notetaker joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams, plus Slack huddles and WhatsApp calls. For in-person meetings there’s the mobile app, and the desktop app records without a bot in the call.”
  • The Google Meet page says you can install the Chrome extension and “a bot joins your calls automatically — recording, transcribing, and summarising on your behalf even when you’re not there,” or download the desktop app “to capture directly from your device, with no bot added to the call.” It adds that this is especially useful now that Google flags third-party notetaker bots as “potential risk” by default.
  • The desktop app page (dateModified 17 June 2026) is titled “Record Meetings Without a Bot.” It offers downloads for MacOS (M series and Intel) and Windows, and says: “Record Google Meet, MS Teams, Zoom, and all your favorite platforms without a bot.”
  • Help article Recording without a bot (dateModified 5 November 2026) says bot-free recording needs the latest desktop app on a Mac or Windows computer, that Mac users must grant Screen Recording and Microphone permission, and that “Depending on the platform, recording notifications may not appear automatically when using Bot-free recording. It is your responsibility to inform participants and follow applicable privacy laws.”

Whichever path you use, tell participants they are being recorded and follow the consent rules that apply to your calls.

Languages

Official pages do not use one number. The homepage says tl;dv “transcribes and writes notes in 100+ languages.” The official languages page says “over 30 languages,” and that the interface is in English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean, and German. The desktop app page prints a named “30+” translation list that includes Japanese. I have not tested transcription quality in any language, and I am not treating either “30+” or “100+” as a resolved count. See the disagreement notes on the Free plan page.

Pricing, in brief

Official structured data on the pricing page, checked 22 November 2026, lists Free at €0.00 per seat per month; Pro at €18 per seat per month billed annually or €29 monthly; and Business at €29 per seat per month billed annually or €39 monthly. Official blogs publish the same numbers in dollars and say the euro and dollar prices are the same number. The pricing page’s meta description on the same day included “40% off on every annual plan!” The structured data still showed those list prices, and I did not find an end date or a discounted amount there. Full citations are in tl;dv pricing.

FAQ

Does a tl;dv bot always join the call?

No official page I read on 22 November 2026 said a bot is the only option. The homepage, the Google Meet page, the desktop app page, and the “Recording without a bot” help article all describe a desktop app that records without a bot. The Google Meet page also describes a Chrome extension whose bot joins automatically.

Which platforms does tl;dv officially name?

The homepage FAQ names Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack huddles, and WhatsApp calls, plus a mobile app for in-person meetings and a desktop app. The integrations hub says it can “Record and transcribe meetings from Google Meet, Zoom and MS Teams.”

Is this a hands-on review?

No. It is a researched overview of official pages checked on 22 November 2026. I have not long-term tested tl;dv.
