Decision Log vs Action-Item Tracker

A decision log and an action-item tracker are both lists, which is why they get merged into one spreadsheet that then helps with neither job. A decision log records a question, the choice, who had to respond, when it closed, and who owns the next step. An action-item tracker records work: who will do what, by when, and whether it is done.

You can keep both in the same tool — a sheet, a Notion database, the comments under a memo — as long as the fields are not mixed. This page is a process guide. It is not a hands-on test of Notion, Google Sheets, or any AI notetaker. Meeting-note product comparisons are on the best AI meeting note takers guide and on reviews such as Fireflies.ai and Circleback.

Side by side

Decision log versus action-item tracker
Question Decision log Action-item tracker
One row is A choice that needed a recorded answer. A piece of work that needs to get done.
The key date The close date of the objection window, then the date it actually closed. The due date of the work.
The key people Who must respond (required reviewers), and who owns the next step after the choice. Who will do the work.
Useful statuses Draft, Open, Decided, Deferred, Meeting scheduled, Cancelled. Not started, In progress, Waiting, Done, Parked.
Done means The choice is written, with a date, in the memo and in the log. The work has been finished, or parked with a reason.
What a weekly review looks for Open rows past their close date: close them, extend them in writing, or convert them to a meeting. Overdue work, unclear owners, and tasks that are actually undecided questions.

A weekly async status can point to both lists. It should not be either list by itself. How a notes draft becomes actions or decisions is in turning AI meeting notes into action items and in AI meeting notes vs decision memos.

Worked example (fictional)

The shop, people, and rows below are invented for this page. They are not a client story and not a record of a real company decision.

Quilt & Co. is a seven-person shop considering a change to support hours. After a mid-week conversation, the team has a mix of real work and one actual choice. If everything is dropped into a single “action” list, it may look like this:

  • Change support hours?
  • Email the customers
  • Update the FAQ
  • Tell Leo
  • Look at Saturday coverage

The first and last bullets are not tasks yet. “Change support hours?” is a decision question with no options and no close date. “Look at Saturday coverage” is either information-gathering that needs an owner, or part of the same decision. A cleaner split:

Fictional decision-log row — Quilt & Co. support hours
Field Entry
ID DEC-018
Question Should Quilt & Co. move weekday support from 10:00–18:00 to 9:00–17:00 Asia/Tokyo, starting 8 December 2026?
Recommendation Yes, move to 9:00–17:00 on weekdays. Do not add Saturday coverage in this change.
Required reviewers Hannah Briggs (required), Leo Nguyen (required), Chris Adeyemi (optional).
Close date 26 November 2026, 17:00 Asia/Tokyo
Status Open (as of 20 November 2026)
Next step after the choice If yes: Chris updates the FAQ and Hannah sends the customer note, both before 5 December. If no: record that Saturday coverage was also declined, and park both.
Fictional action items that wait on, or follow, that decision
Action Owner Due Depends on
Write the two FAQ paragraphs (new hours, and a line that Saturday is unchanged) but do not publish Chris 25 November 2026 Can start while the decision is open; cannot publish until DEC-018 is decided
Draft the customer email; do not send Hannah 25 November 2026 Same
Tell Leo, in writing, that his review is required by 26 November, 17:00 Asia/Tokyo Hannah 21 November 2026 The open decision, not a separate “look at Saturday” task

Saturday coverage was named as option “do not add this time” inside the memo, not as a vague task called “look at Saturday.” If someone later wants to reopen Saturday hours, that is a new decision row, not an overdue action.

When one list is enough

A two-person project can often live with a decision log plus a Next section in the weekly status, and no separate task tracker. A larger queue of repeating work — support tickets, editorial production, implementation chores — usually needs an action list, and should still keep decisions out of it. If a row in the task list cannot be finished because nobody has answered a question, move the question to the decision log and leave the task marked waiting, with a link.

Review open decision rows once a week, at the same time you write your status. Anything past its close date should be closed, extended in writing, or turned into a meeting. That review is also the right moment to catch tasks that are secretly undecided questions.

FAQ

Can both live in the same spreadsheet?

Yes, if the fields stay different. A decision row needs a question, reviewers, a close date, and a status such as Open or Decided. A task row needs an owner, a due date, and a work status. Linking the two is useful. Merging the columns usually is not.

Do I need to log every weekly status?

No. Log decisions and proposals that are not yet decisions. A weekly status can point to the log; it does not need a row of its own.

What status values does a decision log need?

A small set is enough: Draft, Open, Decided, Deferred, Meeting scheduled, and Cancelled. Review Open rows once a week. Anything past its close date should be closed, extended in writing, or turned into a meeting.
