Who it is for, and who should skip it

Tactiq may fit a person or a small team that wants live transcripts and AI summaries of Google Meet, Zoom, or Microsoft Teams calls, and that does not want a notetaker bot in the participant list. Official pages describe a Chrome or Edge extension that transcribes in the browser, with no bot joining and no meeting audio stored. The Free plan is $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts a month, and 5 AI credits a month. Pro, at $8 per user per month billed annually on the pricing page, lifts the transcript cap to unlimited, but still lists 10 AI credits a month.

Skip it if you need a bot to attend a meeting you cannot join. Skip it if you join Microsoft Teams from the desktop app: a help article says the Microsoft Teams desktop application is not currently supported, and tells you to continue in the browser or the Teams progressive web app. Skip it if you need a documented iPhone or Android app: the download page I opened lists a Chrome extension and a Mac app, not a phone app. Skip it if you need unlimited AI summaries on the cheapest paid plan. Unlimited AI credits are listed on Team and above, not on Pro. Skip it if you cannot turn on the meeting’s captions. The install help article says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions to create the transcript, and tells you to keep captions turned on.

How the official pages say it works

The homepage headline calls Tactiq an AI note taker for Google Meet, Zoom, and Teams. It says you get live, in-meeting transcriptions and AI summaries. A section titled “Get live transcriptions without an AI bot joining the meeting” lists “No bot joins call,” speaker identification, and support for 60+ languages. The how-it-works page says Tactiq pops up when you start a meeting, that you can click a sentence to mark it as a key item, and that the full transcript and quotes are then ready to share.

The download page lists two products. The Chrome extension transcribes Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams, writes automatic AI summaries and action items, and lets you ask AI questions live inside the meeting. The Mac app is described as a lightweight native app that transcribes Zoom desktop calls without the browser and syncs with your Tactiq account. The same FAQ says the Mac app transcribes Zoom desktop calls only for now, and tells you to use the Chrome extension for Google Meet and Microsoft Teams.

A help article, How to install the Tactiq extension, says to use Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge on a computer, and that Tactiq works with Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom meetings that run in your browser. After you join a test meeting, it says to turn on captions and check that the Tactiq widget appears and begins showing the transcript. A note on that page says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions to create the transcript, and to keep captions turned on during the meeting.

A separate help article, How to use Tactiq with Microsoft Teams meetings, says Tactiq supports Microsoft Teams meetings in Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and the Microsoft Teams Progressive Web App (PWA). It also says the Microsoft Teams desktop application is not currently supported. If Teams asks whether to use the browser or the desktop app, the article says to select “Continue on this browser.”

The homepage FAQ says Tactiq does not record or store the audio from your meetings, and that the transcript happens in real time and is saved as text. The same FAQ says the transcription is only displayed for you, but you can notify everyone that you are transcribing during the meeting. This page is not legal advice. Recording and transcription rules vary by state and by country.

Plans and pricing

These are the list prices on Tactiq’s pricing page on 20 November 2026. The cards I saw show annual billing for the paid plans. Monthly prices were not on those cards. A help article, How to choose the right Tactiq plan and pricing, dated 30 June 2026, lists both annual and monthly figures. I quote both. The help article also says localized pricing may be shown at checkout, and names ARS, AUD, BRL, CAD, COP, EUR, GBP, JPY, MXN, and PEN when paying by card. It says PayPal checkout displays prices in USD. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

Features, as the official pages describe them

  • Live transcription for Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams in Chrome or Edge, with no bot in the call. The pricing comparison table also lists Google Companion Mode on every plan, including Free.
  • AI-generated insights, Ask Tactiq AI, saved AI questions, in-meeting AI, use of AI across multiple meetings, and a daily digest, all marked included on every plan in the comparison table.
  • Share and export transcripts, upload meeting files, edit transcripts, global meeting search, screenshots, auto-correct, labels, and meeting tags, also marked included on every plan.
  • An AI workflow builder and workflow integrations, marked included on every plan. AI Meeting Agent is marked as not included on Free, Pro, and Team, and included on Business and Enterprise.
  • The integrations page lists Google Docs, Confluence, Quip, Notion, Slack, Calendar, HubSpot, Asana, Monday, ClickUp, Jira, Whereby, Intercom, Evernote, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Zoho, and Jitsi, among others. I have not confirmed which of those names are meeting platforms, export destinations, or both, and I have not confirmed which plan includes each one. The pricing table’s collaboration row is labeled “Google Drive, Notion, and other integrations,” and is marked included on every plan.
  • Security rows on the pricing comparison table mark SOC 2 Type II attestation, GDPR compliance, ISO 27001 certification, and HIPAA compliance as included on every plan, including Free. The homepage says Tactiq keeps meeting data private and secure with ISO 27001 certification, a SOC 2 Type II attestation, and full GDPR and HIPAA compliance. The homepage FAQ says Tactiq uses OpenAI’s enterprise API, not ChatGPT, and that OpenAI does not use data submitted via API to train models. Visit Tactiq’s Trust Center and Privacy Center for the vendor’s own detail.

Two official pages do not describe Tactiq’s AI connectors the same way. The pricing cards list Tactiq MCP (beta) and the Tactiq Claude Connector (beta) on Business. A machine-readable official page at tactiq.io/llms.txt says searching and listing meetings work on any Tactiq plan, including Free, and that reading meeting details, summaries, and full transcripts requires Tactiq Team or higher. I am not choosing between those two statements. Check the current pricing page and Tactiq’s Claude or MCP pages before you rely on a connector.

Pros and cons from those pages

These are not scores. They are what the official pages say, and what they leave out.

Pros

  • Official pages describe live transcription without a bot joining the call, and say meeting audio is not recorded or stored.
  • The Free plan is $0, with no credit card and no time limit, including in-meeting AI and AI-generated insights, within the monthly transcript and credit caps.
  • Pro’s annual list price of $8 per user per month is the lowest yearly paid entry I have on this site among approved affiliates, as of 20 November 2026, if you only need unlimited transcripts and can work with 10 AI credits a month.
  • The homepage says Tactiq supports over 60 languages, and that 1,000,000+ users currently have the Chrome extension installed.
  • The pricing table marks SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, GDPR, and HIPAA as included on every plan, including Free.

Cons

  • Transcription depends on the meeting platform’s captions. If captions are off, or are poor, the official install article gives you no second capture method on that page.
  • There is no documented bot that can attend a meeting you skip.
  • The Microsoft Teams desktop app is not currently supported. The Mac app is limited to Zoom desktop calls. I did not find an iPhone or Android app on the download page.
  • Pro still caps AI credits at 10 per month. Unlimited AI credits start on Team, at $16.67 per user per month billed annually, or $20 monthly in the help article.
  • The Business annual figure is $29.17 on the pricing page and $29.16 in the help article. That is a one-cent disagreement, but it is still a disagreement.
  • Official pages do not agree on which plan is required for MCP or the Claude connector.
  • The transcript is only displayed for you unless you share it. You are the one who has to notify other people, if notice is required.

Questions, answered from the official pages

What is Tactiq?

Tactiq’s homepage describes it as an AI note taker for Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams. Official pages say it transcribes meetings in real time from a Chrome or Edge extension, without a bot joining the call, then generates AI summaries, action items, and insights. A download page also lists a Mac app for Zoom desktop calls.

How much does Tactiq cost?

As of 20 November 2026, the official pricing page listed Free at $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month. Pro was $8 per user per month billed annually. Team was $16.67 per user per month billed annually, for 1 to 20 users. Business was $29.17 per user per month billed annually, for 20 to 200 users. Enterprise is custom, for 200 or more users. A help article dated 30 June 2026 also lists monthly prices: Pro $12, Team $20, and Business $40 per user per month. The same help article lists Business annual as $29.16, one cent below the pricing-page figure of $29.17. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

Does Tactiq join the meeting as a bot?

No, according to official pages. The homepage says you get live transcriptions without an AI bot joining the meeting. The same FAQ says Tactiq does not record or store meeting audio, and that the transcript is saved as text. A help article on installing the extension says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions to create the transcript, and tells you to keep captions turned on.

Does Tactiq work on a phone?

The official download page I opened on 20 November 2026 lists a Chrome extension and a Mac app. I did not find an iPhone or Android app listed on that page. The install help article says to use Google Chrome or Microsoft Edge on a computer.
