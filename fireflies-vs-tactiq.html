Quick verdict by use case

There is no single winner. These are readings of the official pages, not test results.

  • No bot in the participant list, from the default setup. Tactiq’s homepage says no bot joins the call. Fireflies usually joins as a visible Notetaker. Fireflies does document bot-free paths, but they are not the default bot workflow: a Chrome extension for Google Meet, and a desktop Take Notes mode.
  • A notetaker that can join a meeting you do not attend. Fireflies’ joins FAQ says the bot can record without you if you invited fred@fireflies.ai and the platform allows guest bots. Tactiq’s official pages describe capture from your browser or, for Zoom desktop, from a Mac app, with you in the meeting. I did not find a documented Tactiq bot that attends a meeting you skip.
  • A cheaper annual entry seat, if you can work with a monthly AI-credit cap. Tactiq Pro is $8 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited transcripts and 10 AI credits a month. Fireflies Pro is $10 per seat per month billed annually, with unlimited transcription and unlimited AI summaries on the plan card, plus 20 AI credits that the credits guide describes as one-time, not a monthly allotment. These are different limits, not interchangeable seats.
  • A phone app, or stored meeting audio or video. Fireflies’ pricing page lists iOS and Android apps, a desktop app, and a Chrome extension. Tactiq’s download page lists a Chrome extension and a Mac app, and the homepage FAQ says Tactiq does not record or store meeting audio. Fireflies official pages do not describe video recording the same way: the Pro plan card lists video recording, while the joins FAQ says Fireflies records video on the Business plan or higher.
  • Live transcripts in the browser, without storing the call audio. That is the workflow Tactiq’s official pages describe, using the meeting platform’s captions. Fireflies’ usual product is a recorder that stores meeting content, with storage limits that rise by plan.

Bots, captions, and recording without a bot

Tactiq’s homepage says you get live transcriptions without an AI bot joining the meeting. The install help article says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions, and to keep captions turned on. A Microsoft Teams help article says the Teams desktop application is not currently supported, and tells you to continue in the browser or the Teams PWA.

Fireflies’ joins and records FAQ says the bot can record without you attending, if you invited fred@fireflies.ai and the platform allows guest bots. The same FAQ says you can record without the bot joining by using the Chrome extension on Google Meet only, or the mobile app for in-person recordings. The Fireflies review on this site also quotes a desktop Take Notes mode that captures system audio on Windows or Mac without the Notetaker joining, and that does not save a separate audio or video file. The Chrome extension product page describes recording, transcribing, and summarizing Google Meet calls without a bot joining the meeting.

I have not tested whether Tactiq’s caption-based transcript, or Fireflies’ visible Notetaker, Chrome extension, or Take Notes mode, is obvious to the other person on the call. Both vendors still describe notice in some form. This page is not legal advice.

Who might pick which, on the official pages alone

Pick Fireflies if you want a notetaker that can join when you are not in the meeting, a phone app, stored recordings, or the broader Fireflies feature set on Pro and Business (the plan cards list assistants, integrations, and, on Business, conversation intelligence and team analytics). Read the free-plan pages carefully, because they do not describe the limits the same way. Longer notes are in the Fireflies.ai review and Fireflies.ai pricing explained.

Pick Tactiq if you want a browser extension that official pages say does not join as a bot, you can keep captions on, and you are happy with text transcripts rather than stored audio. It also fits if you want a $0 plan with no card, or an $8 annual Pro seat with unlimited transcripts, and you can work within the published AI-credit caps. Longer notes are in the Tactiq review and Tactiq pricing explained.

Questions, answered from the official pages

Is Fireflies or Tactiq free?

Both publish a $0 plan. Tactiq Free is $0 with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month. Fireflies Free is called free forever, but official pages disagree on the limits: the pricing page marks transcripts and AI summaries as limited and lists 400 minutes of storage per team, while Free-plan help says 3 meetings are included and that auto-join allows unlimited transcriptions. Check both live pages before you rely on a free allowance.

How much do Fireflies and Tactiq cost?

As of 20 November 2026, Fireflies Pro was $10 per seat per month billed annually or $18 monthly, Business was $19 or $29, and Enterprise was $39 annual only. Tactiq Pro was $8 per user per month billed annually, or $12 monthly in a help article, Team was $16.67 or $20, and Business was $29.17 on the pricing page or $29.16 in that help article, and $40 monthly. These seats include different credit and storage rules. Check both live pages before you buy.

Does Fireflies or Tactiq join the call as a bot?

Tactiq’s official pages say no bot joins, and that transcription runs from a Chrome or Edge extension, using the meeting platform’s captions. Fireflies usually joins as a visible Notetaker and can attend a meeting you skip if the bot is invited. Fireflies also documents a Google Meet Chrome extension and a desktop Take Notes mode that work without the bot joining.
