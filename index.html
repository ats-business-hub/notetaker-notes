1. Does a bot join the call, or does it record from your device?

Fireflies usually joins as Fireflies.ai Notetaker. The Chrome extension can record Google Meet without a bot, but official help says it is audio only and does not work for Zoom or Teams. The desktop app can “Take Notes” from system audio; official help says that mode creates a transcript and summary but does not save separate audio or video files.

MeetGeek can send a Notetaker to Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams, or record without a bot. Official docs describe a Chrome extension (MeetGeek says it is invisible to other participants), a desktop app that records from the device — including Slack Huddles, which the bot cannot join — plus a mobile app and file uploads.

Krisp is built around bot-free capture from your device, with an optional Krisp Bot (Core plan and above) for Zoom, Meet, or Teams, including meetings you cannot attend. Noise cancellation on the desktop app runs on the device; AI transcription is processed in the cloud.

Whichever tool you use, tell participants they are being recorded and follow the consent rules that apply to your calls.

2. Can it send action items to the tools you already use?

The useful question is whether action items reach Slack, your CRM, or Notion without copy-and-paste.

Fireflies can send notes, summaries, transcripts, and action items to Notion (help says saving transcripts to Notion needs Pro or higher). It also documents Slack, HubSpot, and Salesforce integrations, and lists 240+ apps. Some advanced features use optional AI credits, billed separately from the plan.

MeetGeek can post summaries and highlights to Slack, save a page per meeting in Notion, and sync to HubSpot and Salesforce. Official pages also list ClickUp, Jira, Zapier, Make, and n8n. Automated workflows start on Pro.

Krisp can share meeting notes to HubSpot contacts, companies, or deals, and Zapier can trigger when action items are generated. Slack is listed officially (help focuses on notifications and action-item collaboration; the product page also describes sharing a summary to a channel). Notion appears on Krisp’s pricing and product pages, but I did not find a dedicated Notion setup guide.

I have not verified how reliable these syncs are in daily use.

3. Where are recordings stored, and can you delete them?

Fireflies stores and processes data by default in the United States on AWS and GCP. It says customers own their data and meeting content is not used to train AI models. Storage is 400 minutes per team on Free, 8,000 minutes per seat on Pro, and unlimited on Business and Enterprise. The meeting owner can delete a meeting permanently; bulk delete starts on Pro. Automatic retention and private storage are Enterprise features. Private Storage can keep data in a dedicated location, including the EU, but Fireflies says processing still happens in the US.

MeetGeek hosts data on AWS and offers EU or US residency. For European customers, it says production data and LLM analysis stay in the EEA, and that meeting content is not used to train external models. Retention is plan-based: Basic, transcripts 3 months and audio 1 month; Pro, transcripts 1 year and audio 6 months; Business, unlimited transcripts and 12 months of video; Enterprise, custom or zero retention. You can delete meetings in bulk. Account deletion with “erase all data” has a 7-day recovery window.

Krisp says noise cancellation runs locally, while cloud transcription processing data is deleted immediately afterward. Recordings are stored encrypted in the cloud. Storage is 10 GB per seat on Core and 60 GB on Advanced (not pooled). Meetings can sit in Trash for 30 days, then be restored or permanently deleted. Admins can enforce auto-deletion. Default data-center region was not confirmed; Enterprise lists a location choice.

Transcription languages, including Japanese

Japanese is a hard filter if you need it. Fireflies lists Japanese (ja) among 100+ languages, though its pricing FAQ still calls English the primary language and other languages beta. MeetGeek lists Japanese (Japan), ja-JP, among 100+ languages, with automatic detection. Krisp official help lists 16 languages and does not include Japanese. I have not tested accuracy.

Who each tool fits

Fireflies may fit a team that wants a calendar-connected notetaker, a wide integration list, and a free plan. The usual setup puts a bot in the meeting unless you use the Google Meet extension or desktop “Take Notes” mode. Free-plan help says 3 meetings are included and auto-join can unlock unlimited transcriptions. Custom retention is Enterprise, and default processing is in the United States. A researched overview is in the Fireflies.ai review for small businesses (2026). If the other choice is Otter, see Otter.ai vs Fireflies.ai for small businesses (2026).

MeetGeek may fit a team that wants both auto-join and bot-free capture, plan-based retention, and EU or US residency. The free plan is limited to 3 hours a month; automated workflows start on Pro.

Krisp may fit client-facing work where a bot is unwelcome, especially if you also want on-device noise cancellation. It is the weakest official fit for Japanese transcription, and there is no ongoing free plan — only a 7-day trial.
