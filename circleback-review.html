Who it is for, and who should skip it

Circleback may fit a small team that wants notes and action items on every meeting, including a free plan that the pricing FAQ says has unlimited meetings. The homepage documents two ways to record online calls: a bot, or a desktop app for macOS and Windows that does not join as a bot. Calendars named on the homepage are Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook. Platforms named there are Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack huddles, and GoTo, plus in-person recording and imports.

Skip it if 30 days of history is too short and you do not want to pay. That limit is on the Free card. Full integration automations, including the HubSpot and Salesforce line, are on the Pro card, not described as part of Free. Skip the desktop app if screen contents must stay out of the notes. Circleback says the desktop app records video by default and that what is on screen goes into the notes. The same is true of saved bot video. Skip it if you need a written monthly price before you toggle away from Yearly. The Monthly tab is on the page, and the HTML I fetched only contained the Yearly prices.

How the official pages say it works

The homepage describes notes, action items, automations, and search across meetings. It says speaker names are identified and that more than 100 languages are supported. It says Circleback does not use customer data to train models.

The bot path uses the calendar connection to detect meetings and join them. The desktop FAQ says you can record without a bot, that the app records meeting video by default, and that what is shown on screen makes it into the notes. A second FAQ says screen content from saved video, from the bot or the desktop app, goes into the notes so you can find it with Ask Circleback. In-person recording is on the mobile app, and the Free card also lists Apple Watch. Imports are described for past audio or video, and specifically from Fathom, Fireflies, Granola, and Read AI, or from other apps through the Circleback API.

I did not find a Circleback page that tells you how to announce recording to other people. The product does not, in the pages I read, say the bot is hidden. Pro lists a “personalized notetaker participant,” which implies a named bot on that plan. This page is not legal advice. Recording rules vary by state and by country.

Plans and pricing

On 19 November 2026 the pricing page had Yearly selected. The prices below are what that view showed, each labeled per user per month. The signup links on the paid cards used annual billing. A Monthly tab was present and not selected. Its prices were not in the page HTML, so I am not inventing a higher monthly rate. The FAQ’s 50-person annual totals match the Yearly figures times 12. Check the current pricing page, including the Monthly tab, before you buy.

  • Free: $0. AI meeting notes and action items, Ask Circleback, view and draft emails, online and in-person meetings, a bot or the bot-free desktop app, mobile and Apple Watch, speaker-labeled transcripts, automatic tagging and sharing, limited automations, 30-day meeting and recording history, API, MCP, and CLI access, and 2 teams.
  • Pro: $15 per user per month on the Yearly view. Unlimited meeting history, 1 year of recording storage, automations with all integrations (the card names Notion, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, webhooks, and more), custom AI insights, a personalized notetaker participant, and 5 teams.
  • Business: $25 per user per month on the Yearly view. Admin and member roles, unlimited free viewers, custom data retention, default and enforceable settings, 2 years of recording storage, a usage dashboard, and unlimited teams.
  • Enterprise: $35 per user per month on the Yearly view, plus a note that volume discounts apply automatically as you add users. The card lists everything in Business, then SSO (SAML or OIDC) and SCIM, invoice or PO billing, unlimited recording storage, priority support, HIPAA compliance, and enterprise-managed authorization. The page has a user-count field. It does not print a discount table in the HTML I fetched. The FAQ’s 50-user Enterprise total is $21,000 a year, which is $35 times 50 times 12, so that example does not show a discount at 50 users.

The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings. On annual billing, 50 users cost $9,000 a year on Pro, $15,000 on Business, and $21,000 on Enterprise. Business and Enterprise include unlimited free viewers. Viewers can read meetings shared with them and cannot use Circleback for their own meetings. You can change plans or seats from Settings, and Circleback says you are charged the prorated difference. Cancel is described as anytime, in one click. The FAQ does not say what happens to recordings after you cancel.

Nonprofit accounts can get 30% off: sign up with a nonprofit email, then contact support. Students can get 50% off. The FAQ says a school email applies it automatically, or you contact support with proof of being a student. I am repeating the FAQ. I have not confirmed how support applies either discount.

Five people who each need their own meetings, on the Yearly Pro price, are 5 times $15, or $75 a month before any discount. Viewers who only read notes are a paid seat until Business, where the FAQ says they are free. The Free plan’s “2 teams” and Pro’s “5 teams” are separate from that viewer rule.

Features and integrations, as the official pages describe them

  • Notes, action items, Ask Circleback, and search across meetings, on the homepage and on the Free card.
  • Automations. Free says “limited.” Pro says all integrations. The homepage lists Slack, Notion, Linear, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, Asana, Zoho, folk, Monday, Zapier, Make, Gmail, Outlook, Google Calendar, webhooks, API, MCP, and CLI, plus several others (PostHog, Ashby, Intercom, Plain). The homepage does not assign each one to a plan. I am not treating the homepage list as included on Free.
  • Languages. The homepage says over 100, and names English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Portuguese, Dutch, and Japanese. The meeting-languages page lists Japanese (日本語) in a long named list. Circleback detects the predominant language and transcribes the whole meeting in that language. Notes, action items, and insights default to the transcript language. Settings can force those outcomes into another language. The transcript stays in the language that was spoken.
  • Email drafting is on the Free card (“view and draft emails”). The homepage describes Gmail and Outlook connections. It does not say those connections send mail by themselves.

Pros and cons from those pages

Pros

  • Free includes unlimited meetings, notes, action items, a bot or the desktop app, and in-person capture, with a stated 30-day history rather than a meeting count.
  • You can avoid a bot by using the desktop app. The homepage also documents a bot if you want calendar auto-join.
  • Japanese is on both the homepage short list and the support-page list.
  • The homepage says customer data is not used to train models. The privacy policy says AI and transcription providers receive data on a transactional basis to provide the service.
  • The privacy policy states a location: services are hosted and operated in the United States. Business and Enterprise can add free viewers so people who only read notes are not paid seats.
  • The trust center lists SOC 2 Type 2, HIPAA, and the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework.

Cons

  • Free history is 30 days, recording storage on Pro is 1 year, and on Business it is 2 years. Unlimited recording storage is Enterprise.
  • CRM-style automations are written on the Pro card. Free only says limited automations.
  • Screen contents can enter the notes, from the desktop app by default and from bot video.
  • The Yearly prices are published. The Monthly prices were not in the HTML. I cannot say whether Monthly costs more.
  • HIPAA is an Enterprise pricing line and also a trust-center status. The trust center does not say HIPAA is limited to Enterprise. The pricing card does not say it is included earlier.
  • The cancellation FAQ does not say whether recordings are deleted, exported, or kept.
  • The privacy policy’s effective date is 15 July 2024. It is older than the pricing page I read, and it does not repeat the “does not train models” sentence from the homepage.

Privacy and security

The privacy policy is effective 15 July 2024. It lists meeting recordings, meeting transcripts, and meeting calendar event information as personal data Circleback may collect. It says Google API data follows Google’s Limited Use rules. AI model and transcription providers are listed as service providers, “strictly on a transactional basis for providing the Services.” Retention of personal data is for as long as the account is open, or as needed to provide the service, and longer when the law or a dispute requires it. The policy does not give a separate retention clock for meeting recordings. Plan cards do: 30 days on Free, 1 year of recording storage on Pro, 2 years on Business, custom retention on Business, and unlimited recording storage on Enterprise.

On location, the policy says: the services are hosted and operated in the United States through Circleback and its service providers, personal data will be hosted on U.S. servers, and possibly in other countries. The trust center lists SOC 2 Type 2, HIPAA, and the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework. The trust-center page I read does not name a data-center region. I am using the privacy policy for the location claim and the trust center for the audit claims. I am not merging them into a region the trust center did not state.

FAQ

Does Circleback put a bot in the meeting?

The homepage says you can use a bot or the bot-free desktop app for macOS and Windows. The desktop FAQ says the app records meeting video by default and that what is on screen goes into the notes. A separate answer says screen content from saved video, whether from the bot or the desktop app, can also go into the notes. This page has not tested that.

Is Circleback free?

The pricing page lists a Free plan at $0. Its card includes AI meeting notes and action items, Ask Circleback, online and in-person meetings, a bot or the desktop app, mobile and Apple Watch, speaker-labeled transcripts, limited automations, 30-day meeting and recording history, API, MCP, and CLI access, and 2 teams. The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings.

How much does Circleback cost?

With the Yearly tab selected on 19 November 2026, the pricing page listed Pro at $15, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month. Those cards' signup links use annual billing. The FAQ says 50 users on annual billing cost $9,000 a year on Pro, $15,000 on Business, and $21,000 on Enterprise, which matches those per-user rates times 50 times 12. A Monthly tab is on the page, but its prices were not in the HTML I fetched. Check the pricing page before you buy.

Does Circleback support Japanese?

The homepage says Circleback supports over 100 languages, including Japanese. The meeting-languages support page lists Japanese and says Circleback detects the predominant language and transcribes the whole meeting in that language. Notes and other AI outcomes default to the transcript language, and a setting can force those outcomes into another language. The transcript stays in the spoken language. This page has not tested accuracy.

Where does Circleback store meetings?

The privacy policy, effective 15 July 2024, says the services are hosted and operated in the United States and that personal data will be hosted on U.S. servers, and possibly in other countries. It lists meeting recordings and transcripts as personal data Circleback may collect. The trust center page I read lists SOC 2 Type 2, HIPAA, and the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework, and it does not name a storage region.

Which plan includes CRM automations?

The Free card says Limited automations. The Pro card says automations with all integrations, and names Notion, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, webhooks, and more. Unlimited free viewers, for people who only read shared notes, are listed on Business and Enterprise. The pricing FAQ says viewers cannot use Circleback for their own meetings.
