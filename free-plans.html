1. Is there a free plan, or only a trial?

Fireflies publishes a Free plan at $0. The pricing page calls it “Free forever.” Paid list prices on that page, as of November 2026, are Pro $10 per seat monthly when billed annually or $18 when billed monthly, Business $19 or $29 on the same pattern, and Enterprise $39 per seat monthly, annual billing only. I did not find a sentence that says a credit card is required, or not required, to open the Free plan.

MeetGeek publishes Basic as free forever. The pricing page’s short Basic list is 3 hours of transcription a month, 3 months of transcript storage, and 1 month of audio storage. The homepage’s yearly and monthly cards also call Basic free and say “3 hours of recording / month,” “Basic AI summaries,” and “Core integrations.” Paid list prices on the homepage, as of November 2026, are Pro $15.99 monthly or $9.99 yearly, and Business $27 monthly or $17 yearly. Enterprise is custom. As of 20 November 2026 (Japan time), the pricing page was also advertising a Black Friday 30% discount on new Pro and Business subscriptions for 12 months, code BLACKFRIDAY2026, valid for monthly and annual new subscriptions. A script on that page stores sale prices of Pro $11.19 monthly and $6.99 yearly, and Business $18.90 monthly and $11.90 yearly, and sets the offer window from 16 November 2026, 00:00 PST to 30 November 2026, 23:59 PST. Promotions change. Check the official page before you buy. I did not find a sentence that says a card is required, or not required, to open Basic. I also did not find a separate timed trial of MeetGeek.

Krisp pricing for Meeting AI leads with a free trial, not a $0 plan you keep. The trial card says $0, “Free for 7 days,” “No credit card required,” and “Test all premium features risk-free.” The features listed on that card are unlimited transcription, unlimited noise cancellation, unlimited audio and video recording, unlimited AI notes and action items, and limited AI accent conversion. After that, the page’s paid Meeting AI plans are Core at $16 per user monthly or $8 per user on the yearly price, Advanced at $30 or $15 on the same monthly/yearly pair, and Enterprise as a sales conversation. I did not find an ongoing free Meeting AI plan on that page.

Sonix is not in the snapshot table. Official pricing, checked 20 November 2026, describes a 30-minute free trial with no credit card required, not an ongoing free plan. Details are in the Sonix section below and in Sonix pricing explained.

2. What the free allowance includes

Fireflies Free

Help articles and the pricing page do not describe one set of Free limits. Here is what each page says. The disagreements are collected in the next section.

The article “Unlock Unlimited Transcriptions on the Fireflies Free Plan” says these limits apply to signups on or after 1 September 2026. Accounts that signed up earlier and already had unlimited free meetings are described as unaffected. With auto-join off, that article says you are limited to 3 meeting transcriptions. With auto-join on, and with a filter other than “Only when I invite fred@fireflies.ai,” it says there is no cap on the number of transcriptions. The transcription-credits article says the same split: by default, 1 transcription credit per meeting for 3 meetings; with auto-join on, credits are used for summarizing, not for transcribing. Signup transcription credits in the free-plan and transcription-credits articles depend on where the account is created: 3 from the website, 5 from the Chrome extension, 10 for a new mobile user, and 5 for an existing mobile user. Those articles say a credit is also used to transcribe an uploaded file or a dialer recording.

The pricing comparison marks Free transcripts and AI summaries as “Limited,” and the Free plan card says “Unlimited transcription*” with a tooltip that points at shared bot meetings, the Chrome extension, and the mobile app. The same comparison lists a 2-hour recording limit on Free and on Pro, 3 hours on Business, and 4 hours on Enterprise. It marks video capture as not on Free (720p starts on Pro). It marks download of transcripts and recordings as not on Free. It lists 3 public channels on Free. It lists Free API access as 30 requests a minute and 500 a day. It marks multi-language mode as not on Free or Pro. It marks auto-language detection on Free.

Storage is 400 minutes on every Fireflies page I checked, but not in the same unit. The pricing page says “400 mins of storage/team” and “400 mins/team.” The storage help article’s table says 400 mins per user, as a total stored minutes figure, not a monthly refill. The free-plan features article says 400 minutes of storage per seat for recordings, uploads, and dialer calls, and says older meetings must be deleted when the limit is reached. Uploads in the free-plan articles are MP3, MP4, WAV, or M4A, up to 100 MB and 150 minutes per file, using 1 transcription credit. The storage article also describes a monthly upload rate limit of 3,000 minutes per source for an individual, without a separate Free number in that section. It says live bot meetings, the Google Meet Chrome extension, and mobile recordings are not under that upload rate limit.

On AI credits, the AI-credits guide says every account gets one-time credits, Free included: 20 on sign-up, shared by the team, not increased when you add seats, and not expired. The free-plan features article says 20 free one-time AI credits per seat. The “What’s included in the Fireflies Free plan” article says 20 AI credits per month. The pricing comparison’s AI-credits row shows 20 credits on Pro, 30 on Business, and 50 on Enterprise, and the Free cell in that row is empty. Billing terms match the one-time table (Free 20, Pro 20, Business 30, Enterprise 50) and say plan credits do not expire. Purchased credit packs are a paid add-on. The AI-credits guide says transcription, note-taking, and recap emails are not charged in AI credits, while AskFred, custom summary sections, voice agents, and several other tools are. The free-plan features article says Voice Agents can be tried on Free if you have enough credits, and that Voice Agents is a paid-plan feature otherwise.

MeetGeek Basic

On the pricing page’s comparison, Basic is 3 hours of transcription a month, a 2-hour limit per meeting, transcripts stored 3 months, audio stored 1 month, and video storage shown as an em dash. Pro on that same grid is 20 hours a month, a 2-hour meeting limit, transcripts for 1 year, audio for 6 months, and video still an em dash, with $0.50 per extra hour on the Pro card. Business is unlimited transcription, a 3-hour meeting limit, unlimited transcript storage, 12 months of audio, and 12 months of video. Enterprise is unlimited transcription, a 4-hour meeting limit, unlimited transcripts, custom audio, and custom video. Download of assets and of transcripts is marked off for Basic and on from Pro. HD video is marked off for Basic and Pro. AI meeting summaries and AI next steps are marked on for Basic. AI chat is marked on for Basic and Pro and “Unlimited” for Business and Enterprise. The Integrations row is marked off for Basic and “Unlimited” from Pro. Zapier, Make, and n8n are marked off for Basic. API and MCP access, calendars, and the Slack assistant are marked on for Basic. “Transcribe in 100+ languages” is marked on for Basic.

The homepage does not repeat those retention numbers. Its Basic card says 3 hours of recording a month, basic AI summaries, and core integrations. Its Pro card, at both $15.99 and $9.99, says “Unlimited recording.” The manage-plans help article says Enterprise is “theoretically unlimited but capped at 200 hours monthly” unless MeetGeek extends it, and that if you still have at least one minute left, the next meeting is included even if it runs past the monthly quota. It also says pay-as-you-go continues recording beyond included hours at $0.50 per extra hour. The pricing page attaches that $0.50 rate to the Pro card.

Getting started says MeetGeek is freemium, with core features free on Basic, and that signup uses a Google or Microsoft account. The same article says email-summary controls start on Pro and are not on Basic, and that renaming the notetaker, editing its disclaimer, and the wrap-up reminder are Business and Enterprise. A September 2026 product update says Desktop App 2.0 is available from the Free plan on macOS and Windows.

Krisp trial, then paid storage

The trial card lists unlimited transcription and unlimited AI notes for 7 days. It does not state a storage cap for the trial. The pricing comparison says transcripts and summaries in “16+” languages. The language list on that page names 16: English, French, German, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Portuguese, Russian, Spanish, Hindi, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, Czech, Ukrainian, and Korean. The recording help article lists the same 16 and does not include Japanese. Core storage is 10 GB per seat and Advanced is 60 GB per seat. The storage help article says that allowance is not pooled across the team. A recording FAQ says a recording must last at least 30 seconds and that one recording can be up to 4 hours.

3. Where official pages disagree

I am not choosing a winner among these. The product may follow one of them. I have not tested which.

Fireflies transcription. The pricing table marks Free transcripts “Limited.” The Free card says “Unlimited transcription*” for shared bot meetings, the Chrome extension, and the mobile app. Help says the default is 3 meeting transcriptions, and that auto-join removes the cap for eligible meetings. The free-plan features article’s heading says “unlimited transcription and unlimited AI summaries,” while the table under that heading says summaries are generated with available AI credits. A separate unlimited-transcripts FAQ says AI summaries are not included in unlimited transcripts and depend on the AI-credit balance.

Fireflies storage unit. The pricing page says 400 minutes per team. The storage help table says 400 minutes per user. The free-plan features article says 400 minutes per seat.

Fireflies AI credits. One free-plan article says 20 credits per month. The AI-credits guide and the free-plan features article say 20 one-time credits that do not expire. Those two also disagree on whether the 20 are per seat or one pool for the team. The pricing table does not show a Free AI-credit number.

Fireflies integrations. The free-plan articles say integrations are available on Free, including a “50+ apps” line that mentions CRMs, and the free-plan features article has an integrations row. The pricing comparison marks Integrations blank on Free and “All” on Pro, Business, and Enterprise. The pricing FAQ says you get app and CRM integrations on the premium tiers, and that sending audio through Zapier starts on Pro.

Fireflies when credits run out. The AI-credits guide says free users see a paywall and are not auto-charged. The same guide’s FAQ answer describes an automatic $5-a-month subscription for 50 credits when default plan credits run out and you are not already on an AI-credits subscription, without repeating the free-user exception. Billing terms put the “no charge unless you upgrade” line under “If you’re a Free user,” and put the automatic $5 subscription under the paid-plan section.

MeetGeek Pro hours. The pricing page lists Pro at 20 transcription hours a month. The homepage says Pro includes unlimited recording.

MeetGeek Basic integrations and agents. The homepage says Basic includes core integrations. The pricing comparison marks the Integrations row, and Zapier, Make, and n8n, off for Basic, while marking API and MCP, calendars, and Slack assistant on. The Pro plan card lists “AI Agents and agentic workflows” under what Pro adds on top of Basic. The comparison grid marks “AI Agents & Workflows” on for Basic and Pro, and “Unlimited” for Business and Enterprise.

MeetGeek automatic language detection. The pricing comparison marks it on for Basic. The language-settings help says it is available for paid users. The getting-started help says the transcript language defaults to auto-detect. The language-settings article also says the account default starts as English (United States).

MeetGeek “unlimited.” The pricing page says Business and Enterprise transcription is unlimited. The manage-plans article says Enterprise is capped at 200 hours a month to prevent misuse, and that MeetGeek can extend that.

Krisp language count. The pricing comparison says “16+” languages. The language list on that page, and the recording help article, each name 16 languages and do not include Japanese.

4. What official pages say about stepping down

Fireflies. Billing terms say a downgrade takes effect on the next billing cycle, and you keep the current tier’s features until then. They say Enterprise can move to Business or Pro, and Business can move to Pro only. Cancelling the plan moves the account to Free at the end of the current cycle. Until then, billing terms say you keep paid features and access to your data. I did not find a page that says which meetings are deleted when the account is on Free and over the 400-minute storage figure. The free-plan features article says that once you are at the storage limit, older meetings must be deleted to free space. Cancelling an AI-credits add-on is separate and also waits until the end of that cycle. Upgrading replaces the old plan’s included AI credits; they do not stack.

MeetGeek. The homepage says you can switch any seat between tiers anytime, and that free and paid seats can be mixed. The manage-plans article describes buying licenses, assigning them, and upgrading. It also says that when a free user accepts a paid invite, the monthly reset date moves to the day they accepted. I did not find a page that says what happens to meetings already stored when a paid seat moves back to Basic. The pricing page is where retention by plan is listed. The FAQ says you can request deletion.

Krisp. The cancel article says you are not downgraded immediately. You keep the paid plan until the end of the current cycle, and payments are not refundable unless the terms say otherwise. On a team, it says teammates are downgraded and cannot upgrade themselves; an admin can re-subscribe. The recording FAQ says that if you downgrade, you can still listen to and share existing recordings, and you cannot make new recordings unless you buy more storage. I did not find a sentence that says what stops on day 8 of the 7-day trial if you do not choose a paid plan. A change-plan article says that if you are still in the trial and you switch the Core plan to yearly, you are not charged at that moment.

5. Japanese, as the official pages state it

Fireflies lists Japanese, code ja, in the supported-languages article for transcription and summaries, updated in the days before this check. That article also lists Japanese as a display language for the app interface, and says display language does not change transcription. The pricing FAQ still says English is the primary language and that Spanish, French, Portuguese, Italian, and 100+ other languages are in beta. I have not tested accuracy.

MeetGeek’s API language-code appendix lists Japanese (Japan), ja-JP. The FAQ says 100+ languages with automatic detection and points to the language-settings article for the full list. A September 2026 product-update note names Japanese among languages it says received a speech-to-text update. Whether automatic detection is on Basic is one of the disagreements above. I have not tested accuracy.

Krisp’s recording help lists 16 transcription languages and does not include Japanese. The pricing page’s language list matches that set. I have not tested accuracy.

6. Who should start where

This is a reading of the limits above, not a test of the products.

Fireflies may fit a team that wants a plan that stays at $0, with a 2-hour cap per meeting on the pricing page, if the team can accept that storage, credits, summaries, and integrations are described differently on different official pages. For signups on or after 1 September 2026, help says the default is 3 meeting transcriptions unless auto-join is turned on.

MeetGeek Basic may fit occasional use inside the pricing page’s 3 hours a month, with transcripts kept 3 months and audio kept 1 month, and with no video-storage duration shown for Basic. Use the pricing page rather than the homepage if you need the Pro hour cap: those two pages do not say the same thing. Downloads of transcripts are marked off for Basic.

Krisp may fit a team that wants 7 days on the paid note taker and does not want to enter a card, and that does not need Japanese. The pricing page does not list a free plan to fall back to. I did not find an official description of the account on day 8.

Noota Free, checked 20 November 2026

Noota is not in the snapshot table above, which stays with Fireflies, MeetGeek, and Krisp. It does publish an ongoing Free plan, so it belongs on this page as a short add-on. Details and conflicts are in the Noota review.

The pricing page lists Free at €0 per user per month, with no card, one month of storage, and web, mobile, and desktop apps. All Noota list prices are in euros. The comparison table marks Free as “Bot only.” Official pages do not describe the recording limit the same way. The pricing footnote says unlimited recording on Free requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. A help article says bot recordings are unlimited on Free, and that the 300-minute cap applies to live, desktop app, and Chrome extension recordings, renewed on the account anniversary. The same pricing page also advertised 40% off the first year of a new annual Pro or Business plan, until Monday, 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time, with no code and no discount on monthly plans. A separate help article describes a 7-day Pro or Business trial that requires a card and converts automatically unless you stop it.

A Supported Languages article dated 15 April 2024 lists Japanese. The current marketing feature page does not name Japanese in its short language list. I have not tested accuracy.
