What each product is

Krisp started as noise cancellation and now sells a Meeting AI product that adds a note taker on top. Its official pricing and marketing pages, checked 26 November 2026, list Core at $16 per month or $8 per month billed annually, and Advanced at $30 per month or $15 per month billed annually, plus a $0 Free Trial. The marketing page says “No bot joins your meeting” and describes a Mac and Windows install as a microphone and speaker. I do not rank noise cancellation or notes by quality; I have not tested either.

Otter is a meeting note taker that can join from your calendar as a bot. Official help on turning that auto-join off is on Stop Otter Notetaker from auto-joining meetings. Otter is not an affiliate on this site; links go to official pages only.

What it costs on list prices

Krisp Core at $8 a month billed annually is close to the $8.33 Otter Pro annual figure recorded on this site on 20 November 2026. Otter Basic at $0 is the cheaper start if a free plan covers your meetings. Krisp’s official pricing page did not list an ongoing free plan. These are list prices before tax or any discount. They are not quotes, and I have not verified checkout totals.

Krisp’s 55% annual-plan banner would change the Krisp numbers if it applies to you. I did not see complete terms, so I am not inventing a discounted price. Black Friday cleanup on this site is scheduled separately.

Bot or no bot

This is the clearest published difference. Krisp’s official pages say the product works as a microphone and speaker on your device, and that no bot joins the meeting. Otter can send a notetaker from your calendar. If you need to stop Otter from joining, use Otter’s official help, summarized on Stop Otter Notetaker from auto-joining meetings.

Bot-free does not mean consent-free. Tell people when you record, and follow your company’s and your country’s rules. I have not tested either capture method.

Languages, including Japanese

Otter’s official Supported Languages article, updated 10 November 2026, lists transcription in English (US and UK), French, Spanish, Japanese, Mandarin Chinese (Simplified), German, Italian, Cantonese, Korean, Portuguese (Brazil), Russian, Indonesian, Malay, Dutch, Turkish, Vietnamese, and Polish. The same article says transcription and translation are different, and that AI Chat can translate conversation content without changing the original transcript.

Krisp’s official Voice Translation help article, updated 8 November 2026, includes Japanese in a 60+ language list for that product. I am not treating that list as a meeting-note language guarantee. This site’s Krisp pricing page already notes that meeting-note language counts of 16 and 17 have not been reconciled, and that a homepage FAQ list did not include Japanese.

I do not rank either tool on Japanese or English accuracy. Accuracy depends on microphones, overlapping speech, and audio quality, and I have not tested it.
