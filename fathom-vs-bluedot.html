Fathom vs Bluedot: Quick Comparison

Fathom and Bluedot are AI meeting note takers that approach meeting documentation from different directions. Choosing between them requires more than comparing subscription prices. The way each product captures conversations, handles recording duration, and organizes meeting information can influence its suitability for your daily workflow.

Fathom is particularly worth considering if you want to document frequent meetings without immediately committing to a subscription. Bluedot deserves consideration if recording without a meeting bot, choosing between audio and video capture, or working across desktop and mobile environments is important to you.

What Is Fathom?

Fathom is an AI meeting assistant that captures conversations and produces transcripts and summaries. Its official feature documentation also describes meeting search, Highlights, Trackers, Ask Fathom, and tools for analyzing team conversations.

Official source: fathom.ai

The product is worth evaluating when the main objective is to reduce the amount of administrative work associated with regular meetings.

Consider a consultant who conducts discovery calls, project reviews, and client follow-ups throughout the working week. Rather than selecting a note taker based on the longest feature list, that consultant might prioritize access to previous discussions, convenient summaries, and predictable subscription costs.

Fathom is a reasonable starting point for that evaluation.

Its individual and organizational offerings should, however, be considered separately. A professional documenting personal meetings may not need the same functionality as a department that wants centralized oversight of customer conversations.

Meeting Capture and Recording Style

Capture style is one of the most important differences in this Fathom vs Bluedot comparison.

How Fathom captures meetings

Fathom's official feature documentation distinguishes its conventional meeting notetaker from its newer bot-free functionality.

According to that documentation, bot-free capture is available in beta through its Mac desktop application on every plan, including Free. A Fathom user needs to be in the meeting, and Windows support for that capture method is described as coming. The conventional Fathom Notetaker works on Mac and Windows.

The website identifies Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack Huddles as compatible with bot-free capture. Its conventional notetaker supports Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams.

Official source: fathom.ai

This makes operating-system and meeting-platform compatibility essential purchasing checks.

For example, someone who exclusively uses Windows should not assume that a feature available through Fathom's Mac application is also available through its Windows experience.

Similarly, platform compatibility for conventional capture should not automatically be interpreted as compatibility for bot-free capture.

How Bluedot captures meetings

Bluedot explicitly describes bot-free online meeting capture across Google Meet, Zoom, Teams, and Slack on Mac and Windows.

Its website also identifies optional video capture and mobile-based documentation of in-person conversations.

Official source: bluedothq.com

This approach makes Bluedot worth considering when avoiding an additional meeting participant is part of an organization's preferred workflow.

Before selecting either product, establish which capture method matches your devices and meeting platforms. A general compatibility statement is less useful than confirmation that the exact capture method you intend to use is supported.

Transcription, AI Summaries, and Meeting Notes

A meeting transcript and an AI-generated summary serve different purposes.

The transcript should be treated as a working record of the conversation. A summary is a condensed interpretation that can help identify topics, decisions, and follow-up work.

Neither should automatically be treated as a fully verified account.

Fathom's approach

Fathom describes searchable meeting records, AI summaries, and tools for retrieving information from previous conversations.

Official source: fathom.ai

These capabilities are relevant when users need to revisit discussions rather than simply receive a recap immediately after each meeting.

A project manager, for example, might want to review previous conversations before discussing a disputed requirement. A consultant might use meeting documentation to prepare an agenda for the next client call.

The purchasing question is whether the available documentation and retrieval tools fit that person's existing working habits.

Bluedot's approach

Bluedot describes transcription and summarization alongside AI-assisted querying across meetings.

Official source: bluedothq.com

Its suitability should be assessed according to the information users want to retrieve, the format in which they intend to retain it, and whether the original conversation needs to remain accessible.

Which produces better notes?

Neither vendor publishes a head-to-head accuracy comparison, and this article did not test either product.

The published features do not establish which product performs better with overlapping speakers, technical vocabulary, poor microphone quality, or unfamiliar accents.

Prospective users should evaluate both products using an authorized sample conversation that reflects their actual working conditions.

Useful evaluation criteria include:

  • Correct interpretation of names and terminology.
  • Whether decisions are distinguished from suggestions.
  • Whether summaries preserve important qualifications.
  • Whether follow-up tasks accurately reflect commitments.
  • How easily information can be checked against the original conversation.

Fathom's published allowance makes it an attractive candidate for ongoing individual documentation without a subscription.

Bluedot's introductory allowance is more appropriate for evaluating whether the product fits a particular workflow before committing to continued use.

Importantly, recording quantity and recording duration are different restrictions.

A subscription may permit repeated meetings while placing a restriction on the length of each individual recording. This matters for workshops, extended client consultations, research discussions, and training sessions.

Do not interpret unlimited recordings as confirmation that every related functionality is unrestricted.

Other considerations include retention periods, processing allowances, export permissions, and availability of advanced AI functions. If a restriction matters to you and isn't covered in the cited documentation, ask the vendor before buying.

Official source: bluedothq.com

Fathom's pricing documentation identifies subscription-dependent organizational security controls, including options for centralized administration and customized retention.

Official source: fathom.ai

These are vendor-published descriptions, not independent security assessments conducted for this comparison.

The suitability of either product for confidential material depends on the applicable contracts, administrative configuration, organizational policies, and circumstances of the conversation.

Before introducing an AI note taker, consider how participants will be informed, who should have access to the resulting documentation, and when information should be deleted.

Recording and consent rules vary by country and state, so check the rules that apply where you and the other participants are.

Which Tool Is Better for Individual Users?

Fathom is the stronger initial candidate for individuals who regularly document routine meetings and want to minimize subscription expenditure.

Bluedot is worth prioritizing when its particular desktop capture options or distinction between audio and video are more important than access to an ongoing no-cost allowance.

The decision may also depend on whether the person primarily attends scheduled video calls or works across different conversation environments.

For an independent consultant, the most important factor could be continuity of access to client documentation. For a freelance researcher, the choice could instead depend on capture flexibility and the ability to review sensitive material appropriately.

Neither professional should purchase functionality simply because it is marketed as an advanced capability.

The preferred subscription is the one that covers the actual workflow without unnecessary complexity.

Which Tool Is Better for Teams?

Fathom deserves consideration for organizations prioritizing shared meeting knowledge and conversation analysis.

Bluedot deserves consideration for organizations prioritizing its capture approach and administrative workflow requirements.

Rather than asking which vendor has more organizational features, identify what the team needs to accomplish.

A customer-facing department might emphasize consistent follow-up documentation. A project delivery team might prioritize retrieving historical decisions. An organization handling confidential information might prioritize access restrictions and retention policies.

An appropriate evaluation should include representatives from the intended users and the people responsible for information security.

The team should also establish who owns meeting documentation when an employee changes roles or leaves the organization.
