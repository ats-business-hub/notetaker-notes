What official pages say Sonix is

The homepage and pricing page present Sonix as a platform for converting audio and video to text, with translation, subtitles, an in-browser editor, and AI analysis. The homepage tagline I saved was “Upload once. Get everything.” The pricing page says all plans include the editor, 54+ languages, and what Sonix calls award-winning accuracy. Sonix’s own pages also repeat a “99% accuracy” claim. That is a vendor claim. I have not tested accuracy.

The features page lists audio formats including MP3, WAV, M4A, and FLAC, and video formats including MP4, MOV, MKV, and AVI. It also names an in-browser transcript editor, automated subtitles, and integrations including Zoom, Dropbox, Google Drive, a REST API, and webhooks.

Two official ways to get a transcript

The AI meeting recorder page describes two paths that, it says, end in the same Sonix editor.

Upload a recording. You record the meeting yourself, or export a file from another app, then upload it. The meeting-recorder page says this works on any platform that saves an audio or video file, including Webex and other apps that Sonix Agent does not join. It also says uploads are available on all plans and on the free trial, in 54+ languages. Words arrive after you upload the file, “in minutes,” according to that comparison table.

Sonix Agent in the call. The same page says Sonix Agent joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams, writes the transcript while people talk, names speakers from the call, and saves the meeting video with the transcript when you leave. You paste the invite link, admit the agent from a waiting room or lobby if the platform uses one, and allow recording if the platform asks. The page says Sonix Agent shows up in the participant list under that name, with the Sonix logo as its video tile, so everyone in the call can see it. If nobody admits it, “Sonix stops trying after about 10 minutes and that meeting costs nothing.” Live transcription is listed at 52 languages, plus bilingual Spanish and English, which is a narrower list than the 54+ languages quoted for uploads. The page says Sonix Agent is included with Pro and Enterprise plans, and that the free trial covers uploaded recordings.

Sonix Agent does not join Webex or other meeting apps, according to that page: you upload those recordings instead.

Languages, including Japanese

The pricing and features pages both say transcription and translation cover 54+ languages. The languages page names Japanese and says transcripts are returned in kanji, hiragana, and katakana. The meeting-recorder page lists 52 live languages for Sonix Agent, which is not the same figure as the 54+ list for uploads. I have not tested Japanese accuracy, dialects, or mixed-language audio.

Security notes from official pages

The features and pricing pages say Sonix is SOC 2 Type 2 audited, and they mention encryption at rest, TLS in transit, and two-factor authentication. HIPAA is described with a signed BAA, and SSO is named, in the security lines I saved; the pricing page’s Enterprise block is where HIPAA, a signed BAA, SSO/SAML, and SCIM are described in more detail. I have not opened gated trust-center reports.

Who might look at Sonix

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested the editor or the agent.

Look at Sonix if you mainly transcribe uploaded audio or video, want a 30-minute trial with no credit card, or need a published Japanese transcription path. Look at the Pro or Enterprise cards if you want Sonix Agent to join Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams live. If you want a meeting notetaker that official pages describe as recording without a bot, this site’s bot-free meeting recorders guide and the Tactiq, Bluedot, and Circleback reviews are the closer comparisons. Sonix is not interchangeable with those products.

FAQ

What is Sonix, according to its official pages?

Sonix describes itself as a speech-to-text platform for transcribing, translating, and subtitling uploaded audio and video. A separate page describes Sonix Agent, which joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams and writes a live transcript. This page has not tested either path.

Does Sonix have a free plan?

Sonix publishes a 30-minute free trial with no credit card required, not an ongoing free plan. The meeting-recorder page says the trial covers uploaded recordings, and that Sonix Agent is included with Pro and Enterprise.

How much does Sonix cost?

As of 21 November 2026, Pay As You Go was $10 per hour; Core was $25 a month or $275 a year; Advanced was $50 a month or $550 a year; Pro was $80 a month or $880 a year; and Enterprise was custom. Additional hours on subscription plans are $10 per hour. Extra seats on Core, Advanced, and Pro are $25 a month or $275 a year and do not add more hours. Check the live page before you buy.
