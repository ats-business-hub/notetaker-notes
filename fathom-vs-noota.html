Fathom vs Noota: pricing, recording methods, and plan limits

Fathom and Noota both turn meetings into notes and summaries, but they price in different currencies and record in different ways. This comparison uses each vendor’s official pricing and help pages, checked on 26 November 2026. It is not a hands-on test, and it does not rank accuracy. Recording methods

Fathom’s pricing page lists a choice of bot-free and bot capture. It marks bot-free capture as a beta feature for Mac. The Free plan lists unlimited recordings and transcriptions, and “sign up, free forever.”

Noota can join as a bot, or record from the computer with the desktop app. The pricing FAQ says bot-free (desktop) recording is on the Pro plan and above. Participants are notified that the meeting is recorded, according to that FAQ.

Unlimited recording on Noota’s Free plan is conditional. The pricing page states: it requires a connected calendar with “Join all meetings” enabled, and the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants. Otherwise, live recordings and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. Noota’s support article lists live, desktop-app and Chrome extension recordings as 300 minutes per month on Free, and unlimited on paid plans.

Storage and history

Fathom’s pricing page lists recordings, call storage, transcription, and call downloads as unlimited. It also says paid plans have unlimited lookback for “my calls,” while the team-calls lookback window depends on the plan.

Noota’s pricing and support pages list meeting history as 1 month on Free, 6 months on Pro, and unlimited on Business and Enterprise. The support article says meetings older than the plan’s history limit are archived (it does not say they are deleted).

Who each tool is for, on paper

Choose Fathom if you want a US-dollar free plan that the vendor describes as uncapped for recordings and transcriptions, and you are comfortable with bot-free capture still being a Mac beta. Choose Noota if you want a euro-priced free plan, EU hosting claims, and bot-free recording on Pro and above — and you accept the Free-plan “Join all meetings” sharing rule if you want unlimited recording. These are editorial positions based on the official pages above, not a test of quality.
