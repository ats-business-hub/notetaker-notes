Bluedot

The Bluedot review says the homepage calls it an invisible, bot-free note taker. The FAQ says it never invites a bot. Recording uses a Chrome extension or a desktop app on Mac or Windows, and you do not have to be the host. It captures from the browser or desktop, so the review’s FAQ lists Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack huddles, and phone calls. Video is optional. It does not announce itself the way a bot does, and the FAQ recommends telling participants. That review has not tested this.

The free card is 5 meetings for the life of the account, then view and share only. The same card says “Start free, forever,” and the homepage FAQ says Bluedot is not a free AI meeting note taker. Basic, the first paid plan, is $14 or $18 per user per month. The review reads the lower row as annual because that control is marked selected. Basic is audio only and caps a recording at 1 hour. The review says Bluedot charges only users who record.

The full write-up, including the calendar-access conflict and the 30-day versus 90-day backup lines, is the Bluedot review.

Circleback

The Circleback review says the homepage documents two ways to record online calls: a bot, or a desktop app for macOS and Windows that does not join as a bot. The desktop FAQ says the app records meeting video by default and that what is on screen goes into the notes. The same is true of saved bot video. Skip the desktop app if screen contents must stay out of the notes. Platforms named on the homepage include Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, Slack huddles, and GoTo. That review has not tested this.

Free is $0. The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings. The Free card limits history to 30 days and says automations are limited. The first paid plan on the Yearly view is Pro at $15 per user per month. The Monthly tab’s prices were not in the HTML the review fetched, so this page does not add a monthly rate. HubSpot and Salesforce automations are named on the Pro card, not on Free.

The full write-up is the Circleback review.

Laxis

The Laxis review says the homepage describes Laxis as bot-free. The help center is more specific. The desktop app captures the microphone and computer audio, and the desktop guide says no bot joins. A Chrome extension captures captions in Google Meet, and in Zoom and Teams in the browser, without a bot. Those extension meetings are text only. The same help center says a meeting bot can join Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams. The privacy policy says some options show other participants that a notetaker is present and some do not. That review has not tested this.

Basic is $0 with 300 transcription minutes a month, recordings up to 45 minutes, and 30 days of conversation history. New accounts get a 14-day Premium trial with no credit card, then move to Basic. Meetings older than 30 days are archived, not deleted. Premium, the first paid plan, is $15.99 a month, or $9.99 a month billed yearly ($119.88 a year).

Language is a disagreement inside the Laxis review, not a new finding here. The homepage and the pricing comparison table say 100+ languages. The meeting-assistant help page and the billing page say 60. The 60-language list is not published on the pages that review read, so it does not confirm Japanese transcription. Search inside meetings works in English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean, and the app interface includes Japanese.

The full write-up is the Laxis review.

Which should you pick

Pick Bluedot when the requirement is a product whose pages describe device capture and say it does not invite a bot. Accept 5 lifetime meetings on the free plan, and accept that Basic is audio only with a one-hour cap. The review also says you do not have to be the host.

Pick Circleback when you want unlimited meetings on the free plan and you will use the desktop app. The history limit on that free plan is 30 days. The desktop app records video by default, and screen content can enter the notes. A bot remains available if you turn it on.

Pick Laxis when you want an ongoing free allowance of 300 minutes a month and a desktop path that says no bot joins. Read the rest of that review before you treat “bot-free” as the only mode: help also documents a bot, and the language pages say both 60 and 100+. Premium at $9.99 a month billed yearly, or $15.99 billed monthly, is the first paid plan on that review.

Pick Noota when you want EU-hosted notes and can pay Pro for the desktop mode the pricing table calls “invisible.” Do not pick it for a bot-free free plan: Free is marked bot only.

Noota

The Noota review says the pricing comparison table marks Free as “Bot only,” and Pro, Business, and Enterprise as “Bot or invisible.” The pricing FAQ and a 21 October 2026 product update place recording without a bot on the desktop app, on the Pro plan and above. The desktop-app page says the app itself is included in every plan, for Mac and Windows, but those same pages say the Free plan uses the app with the Noota bot. Official pages do not agree on whether participants are notified when you record without a bot. That review has not tested this.

Free is €0 with no card and one month of storage. Official pages do not describe the Free recording limit the same way. A 300-minute monthly cap appears next to an unlimited bot path. The cheapest paid list price on 20 November 2026 was Pro at €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. Those prices are in euros. The full write-up, including the conflicts, is the Noota review.

Tactiq

Tactiq official pages say a Chrome or Edge extension transcribes Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams with no bot joining the call, and that meeting audio is not recorded or stored. Help says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions, and to keep captions turned on. The download page lists a Mac app for Zoom desktop calls only, and a help article says the Microsoft Teams desktop application is not currently supported. I did not find an iPhone or Android app on the download page. As of 20 November 2026, the official pricing page listed Free at $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month; Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited transcripts and 10 AI credits; Team at $16.67 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited AI credits; and Business at $29.17 per user per month billed annually. A help article dated 30 June 2026 also lists monthly prices of $12, $20, and $40 for Pro, Team, and Business. The full write-up is the Tactiq review.

FAQ

Which of these records without a bot?

The Bluedot review says the homepage says Bluedot does not invite a bot, and that it records with a Chrome extension or a desktop app. The Circleback review says the homepage documents a bot-free desktop app for macOS and Windows, and also a bot. The Laxis review says the homepage describes Laxis as bot-free, the desktop app says no bot joins, and the help center also documents a bot for Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams. The Noota review says bot-free (“invisible”) desktop recording starts on Pro, and that Free is bot only. Tactiq’s official pages say no bot joins, and that a Chrome or Edge extension transcribes from the meeting’s captions. This page has not tested any of them.

What does each free plan allow?

Bluedot's free card is 5 meetings per lifetime, then view and share. The card also says Start free, forever, and the homepage FAQ says Bluedot is not a free AI meeting note taker. Circleback's pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings, and the Free card limits history to 30 days. Laxis Basic is $0 with 300 minutes a month, recordings up to 45 minutes, and 30 days of history. New Laxis accounts get a 14-day Premium trial. Noota Free is €0 with no card and one month of storage; official pages disagree on the 300-minute cap versus an unlimited bot path, and that free plan is not the bot-free mode. Tactiq Free is $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts a month, and 5 AI credits a month.

What is the starting paid price?

On the reviews checked 19 November 2026, Bluedot Basic is $14 or $18 per user per month. The lower price sits with the Annual control. Circleback Pro is $15 per user per month on the Yearly view. Monthly prices were not in that HTML. Laxis Premium is $9.99 per month billed yearly, or $15.99 billed monthly. Noota Pro, checked 20 November 2026, is €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. That price is in euros. Tactiq Pro, checked the same day, is $8 per user per month billed annually, or $12 monthly in a help article dated 30 June 2026. This page does not convert currencies.

Which should you pick if you do not want a bot?

Pick Bluedot if you want the product whose pages describe device capture and say it does not invite a bot. Pick Circleback if you want unlimited meetings on the free plan and will use the desktop app, knowing it records video by default and screen content can enter the notes. Pick Laxis if you want 300 minutes a month on Basic and a desktop path that says no bot joins, and you have read that a bot is also documented and that the language pages disagree between 60 and 100+. Pick Noota if you want EU-hosted notes and will pay Pro for the desktop mode the pricing table calls “invisible.” Free is not that mode. Pick Tactiq if you want a bot-free Chrome or Edge extension, can keep captions on, and are happy with text transcripts rather than stored audio, including on a $0 plan or an $8 annual Pro seat.
