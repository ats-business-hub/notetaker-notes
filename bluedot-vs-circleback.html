How each one records

Bluedot’s homepage headline describes an “invisible, privacy-first AI note taker,” and a section titled “AI notetaker that doesn’t join your meetings” says Bluedot turns conversations into transcripts and meeting notes with no bots joining your calls. It says you can capture Meet, Zoom, Teams, and Slack on Mac or Windows, with video optional and no bots; take notes at in-person meetings with iPhone, Android, or Apple Watch; and make, record, and transcribe phone calls from Bluedot using your own phone number. A banner on the pricing page also said “Introducing Bluedot 2.2. Now with phone call recording.” The plan cards I saved do not say which plan includes phone call recording, so I am not assigning that feature to a tier.

Circleback’s homepage FAQ, “Can I use Circleback without a bot?”, says yes: you can download the desktop app for macOS and Windows to record meetings without a bot joining your calls. By default it records meeting video, and what is shown on screen makes it into the notes. Circleback can also send a bot. The pricing cards list “a bot or the bot-free desktop app for online meetings” and “mobile and Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings.” The homepage names Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, WebEx, Slack huddles, and GoTo, plus in-person meetings, and says Circleback connects with Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook Calendar to detect and automatically join meetings.

Both homepages say they do not use customer data to train models. Bluedot’s homepage says “Your data is never used to train or improve AI models.” Circleback’s homepage FAQ, “Does Circleback train AI models on our data?”, says no: Circleback does not use customer data to train models. I have not audited either practice.

Free plans

Bluedot Free is $0, labeled start free, forever, and lists 5 meetings per lifetime, a Chrome extension, desktop and mobile apps, and Slack, Notion, Zapier, and Make. That is a lifetime cap of five meetings, not a monthly allowance.

Circleback Free is $0. The card includes notes, action items, Ask Circleback, a bot or the desktop app, mobile and Apple Watch, speaker-labeled transcripts, limited automations, 30-day meeting and recording history, and API, MCP, and CLI access. The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings.

On those published caps, Circleback Free allows more meetings. Bluedot Free is easier to exhaust if you want an ongoing free option. The products are not otherwise equivalent.

Paid prices and seat rules, as of 21 November 2026

Bluedot’s Annual cards listed Basic at $14, Pro at $20, and Business at $32 per user per month. The Monthly cards listed Basic at $18, Pro at $25, and Business at $39. Basic is described as audio only, with unlimited meetings, unlimited storage, a one-hour cap per recording, webhook access, and MCP integration. Pro adds unlimited meetings with video, unlimited recording duration, custom note templates, and Zoom and Google Drive imports. Business adds CRM and ATS integrations, advanced admin controls, SSO and SCIM, custom data retention, org-wide MCP and webhooks, and unlimited audio/video imports. A flat-rate company plan is offered on request, with no public amount. A plan-by-plan walkthrough is in Bluedot pricing explained.

Circleback’s Yearly cards listed Pro at $15, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month. The same page’s billingPlanPrices list has Pro at $18 billed monthly or $180 a year, Business at $29 billed monthly or $300 a year, and Enterprise at $420 a year. The pricing FAQ says that on annual billing, 50 users cost $9,000 a year on Pro, $15,000 on Business, and $21,000 on Enterprise. A plan-by-plan walkthrough is in Circleback pricing explained.

The seat rules differ. Bluedot’s pricing FAQ says it only charges for users who record meetings. Circleback’s pricing FAQ says teammates who are not in meetings do not need a paid seat on Business and Enterprise, where you can add unlimited free viewers; that unlimited-viewer line is not on Free or Pro. I have not used either billing screen.

Who should pick which

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested accuracy, the extension, or the desktop app.

Look at Circleback if you want a stated unlimited-meeting free plan, a choice of bot or bot-free desktop capture, in-person recording on mobile or Apple Watch, and you can accept 30 days of history until you pay. Look at Bluedot if you want official pages that describe capture without a bot, in-person and phone-call recording, a pricing FAQ that charges only people who record, and you can accept a five-meeting lifetime free cap, or a one-hour audio cap until Pro. Japanese is named on both homepages’ language lists (Circleback lists it in prose; Bluedot shows 日本語 in the language row). I have not tested accuracy in either product.

FAQ

Do Bluedot and Circleback both record without a bot?

Bluedot’s homepage describes capture with no bots joining your calls. Circleback’s homepage FAQ says you can use the desktop app to record without a bot, and that Circleback can also send a bot. This page has not tested either product.

Which free plan records more meetings?

Circleback’s pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings, with 30-day history on the Free card. Bluedot Free allows 5 meetings for the lifetime of the account. On those published caps, Circleback Free allows more meetings.

Which costs less, Bluedot or Circleback?

On 21 November 2026, Bluedot Basic was $14 per user per month billed annually (audio only, one-hour cap). Bluedot Pro, which the cards describe as adding unlimited video, was $20 annually. Circleback Pro was $15 per user per month on the Yearly cards, or $18 billed monthly in the page-source list. A lower Basic sticker price is not the same product as Circleback Pro.
