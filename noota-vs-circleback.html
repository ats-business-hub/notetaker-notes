How each one records

Noota’s AI note-taker page says Noota takes notes of online meetings, phone calls, and in-person conversations, “with or without a bot.” The same page’s FAQ says Noota records, transcribes, and takes notes of Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, and Webex meetings, with a bot that joins the call or without one, from the desktop app, and that in-person meetings and phone calls are covered by the mobile app and the built-in recorder. The pricing FAQ says Noota can join as a bot with the name you choose, or record quietly from your computer with the desktop app on the Pro plan and above, and that participants are notified that the meeting is recorded. The pricing comparison table marks recording mode on Free as “Bot only,” and on Pro, Business, and Enterprise as “Bot or invisible.”

Circleback’s homepage FAQ, “Can I use Circleback without a bot?”, says yes: you can download the desktop app for macOS and Windows to record meetings without a bot joining your calls. By default it records meeting video, and what is shown on screen makes it into the notes. Circleback can also send a bot. The pricing cards list “a bot or the bot-free desktop app for online meetings” and “mobile and Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings.” The homepage names Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, WebEx, Slack huddles, and GoTo, plus in-person meetings, and says Circleback connects with Google Calendar and Microsoft Outlook Calendar to detect and automatically join meetings.

Free plans

Noota Free is €0, with no card needed. The card lists unlimited meeting notes with an asterisk, 1 month of storage, web, mobile, and desktop apps, voice dictation, MCP integration, a limited email agent, web search and deep research, and EU hosting, with no AI training on your data. The footnote, repeated in the FAQ “Is the Free plan really free?”, says unlimited recording on the Free plan requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants. Otherwise, live recordings and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month.

Circleback Free is $0. The card includes AI meeting notes and action items, Ask Circleback, a bot or the bot-free desktop app, mobile and Apple Watch apps, speaker-labeled transcripts, limited automations, 30-day meeting and recording history, and API, MCP, and CLI access. The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings.

The caps are not written the same way, so I am not declaring a single winner. Circleback’s FAQ states unlimited meetings, with 30-day history on the Free card. Noota’s unlimited line on Free depends on the calendar and sharing conditions in the footnote; otherwise the cap is 300 minutes a month.

Paid prices, as of 21 November 2026

Noota’s pricing page states that all prices are in euros (EUR). I have not converted them into US dollars. The Annual cards listed Pro at €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly, and Business at €39 annually, or €49 monthly. Enterprise is custom, for 50+ seats. The same page advertised 40% off annual Pro and Business plans for the first year, until Monday, 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time, with no code needed, for new customers, returning customers, and monthly subscribers who switch to annual: Pro €11.40 and Business €23.40 per user per month billed annually for that first year. The page says there is no discount on monthly plans. A plan-by-plan walkthrough is in Noota pricing explained.

Circleback’s pricing page had the Yearly tab selected. The visible cards listed Pro at $15, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month. The same HTML includes a billingPlanPrices list in US cents: Pro $18 billed monthly or $180 a year; Business $29 billed monthly or $300 a year; and Enterprise $420 a year. A plan-by-plan walkthrough is in Circleback pricing explained.

Seat rules differ. Noota’s pricing FAQ says seats are unlimited on every plan, and each person pays for the plan they need. Circleback’s pricing FAQ says unlimited free viewers start on Business and Enterprise; viewers can open shared meetings but cannot use Circleback for their own meetings.

Who should pick which

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested accuracy, the bot, or the desktop app.

Look at Circleback if you want a stated unlimited-meeting free plan, a bot or a bot-free desktop app on that free plan, and you can accept 30 days of history until you pay, plus U.S. hosting as described in the privacy policy. Look at Noota if you want prices and hosting described in European terms, a first-year Black Friday rate on new annual Pro or Business plans that is still on the page as of this check, and you can accept a bot-only Free plan, or pay for Pro when you want the desktop “invisible” mode. The AI note-taker page names transcription in English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Dutch, “and more.” Japanese is named on Circleback’s homepage language list. I did not find Japanese named on the Noota pages I opened today, so I am not making a Japanese-language claim for Noota here.

FAQ

Can I record without a bot?

Noota’s pricing table marks bot-free (“invisible”) recording on Pro and above; Free is marked bot-only. Circleback’s homepage FAQ says the desktop app can record without a bot, and the Free card lists a bot or the bot-free desktop app. This page has not tested either product.

Which free plan records more meetings?

Circleback’s pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings, with 30-day history on the Free card. Noota’s Free unlimited line depends on a connected calendar, Join all meetings, and auto-sharing the summary; otherwise the cap is 300 minutes a month. I am not declaring a single winner from those different conditions.

They publish prices in different currencies, and I have not converted them. Circleback Pro was $15 per user per month on the Yearly cards, or $18 billed monthly in the page-source list. Check both live pages before you buy.
