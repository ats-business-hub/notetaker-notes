What Fathom offers, from official pages

Fathom describes itself as an AI notetaker that summarizes meetings. The features page FAQ says the Fathom Notetaker “captures calls on Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams.” The integrations page says the Zoom integration “Lets Fathom join your Zoom meetings to create transcripts, summaries, and action items automatically,” and the same for Microsoft Teams. The features FAQ also asks whether Fathom can capture meetings without a bot, and answers yes: “Bot-free capture is in beta in the Fathom desktop app on Mac and is available on every plan, including Free.” It says a Fathom user needs to be in the meeting, that bot-free capture “works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack Huddles,” and that Windows support for the new desktop app is coming. The iOS app, that FAQ says, captures in-person meetings.

I am not quoting a single language count. The features page describes transcripts and summaries in seven languages, with translation into 29 more through Ask Fathom, while a help article on Free versus Premium says unlimited transcriptions in 38 languages. Official pages do not agree, so I am leaving the number out.

The features page FAQ, “Is Fathom free?”, says yes, and that the free plan includes unlimited meetings, transcription, and AI summaries, plus the Fathom Notetaker, bot-free capture (in beta on Mac), multi-language support, and the iOS app. It says team features like Highlights, Trackers, Deal View, and Scorecards are on paid plans.

A help article, Free vs. Premium for Individuals, adds detail that the pricing cards do not spell out as clearly. It says you have no obligation to upgrade at the end of a free preview, and that if you stay on Free you lose access to new Premium features once the preview ends, 30 days after sign-up. On summaries, it says Free includes “advanced summaries for your first 5 calls each month (after that, only the General/Enhanced template will be available),” while Premium includes unlimited advanced AI summaries. It also says both Free and Premium include CRM sync with HubSpot, Salesforce, and Close for up to 3 users per email domain, and a personal-use Zapier integration. Premium, that article says, can remove in-meeting banners and rename the meeting bot.

Who might look at an alternative

The reasons below come from Fathom’s own pages, not from a test of the product.

  • You want bot-free capture on Microsoft Teams, or on Windows. Fathom’s features FAQ says bot-free capture works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack Huddles, and that Windows support for the new desktop app is coming. Teams is named for the Notetaker bot, not for that bot-free list.
  • You need more than 5 advanced summaries a month and do not want to pay for Premium. The help article caps advanced summaries on Free at the first 5 calls each month.
  • You are a two-person team comparing paid seats. Fathom Team and Business have a 2-user minimum. Some of the tools below sell a single paid seat.
  • You want a vendor whose paid entry price is lower than Fathom Premium or Team. That comparison is in the next section. It is not automatic: Fathom Team annual, at $15 per user per month, is close to several alternatives, and Fathom’s free plan may already cover a light user.

FAQ

Is Fathom free?

Yes. As of 20 November 2026, Fathom’s pricing page listed a Free plan at $0, called free forever, with unlimited recordings and transcriptions, a choice of bot-free capture (in beta) or bot capture, and instant AI call summaries. The features page says the free plan includes unlimited meetings, transcription, and AI summaries, plus the Fathom Notetaker, bot-free capture in beta on Mac, multi-language support, and the iOS app. A help article says Free includes advanced summaries for the first 5 calls each month, and after that only the General/Enhanced template is available.

How much does Fathom cost?

As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page listed Free at $0; Premium for individuals at $16 per user per month billed annually or $20 billed monthly; Team at $15 or $19 per user per month, with a 2-user minimum; Business at $25 or $34, also with a 2-user minimum; and Enterprise at $35 per user per month with annual billing only. Paid plan cards mention a 90-day guarantee. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

Can Fathom record without a bot?

Yes, with limits Fathom publishes. The features page says bot-free capture is in beta in the Fathom desktop app on Mac, is available on every plan including Free, and that a Fathom user needs to be in the meeting. It says the Fathom Notetaker captures calls on Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, while bot-free capture works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack Huddles. Windows support for the new desktop app, it says, is coming.

What are good alternatives to Fathom?

It depends on the constraint you cannot bend. Fireflies documents a calendar bot plus a desktop path without a bot, with Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually. Noota documents bot and bot-free recording, with prices in euros. Bluedot is bot-free only, including on Teams and Windows, but its free plan is 5 lifetime meetings. Circleback documents a bot or a bot-free desktop app, including on a $0 plan with unlimited meetings and 30-day history. Laxis documents a bot, a desktop app, and a Chrome extension, with Premium at $9.99 a month billed yearly. Tactiq is a bot-free Chrome or Edge extension, with Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, and a Free plan of 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month. These tools are not all cheaper than Fathom Premium, and Fathom’s own free plan already includes unlimited recordings.
