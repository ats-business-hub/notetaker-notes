Best AI Note Takers for Recorded Files, Podcasts, and Interviews (2026)

I read official pricing, product, and FAQ pages on 21 November 2026 (Japan time) to see which AI note takers document a way to transcribe a file you already have — a podcast episode, an interview recording, or any other audio or video — and what that path costs to try. I have not uploaded a podcast or interview to these tools, so this is not a ranking of accuracy, speaker labels, or how well a transcript reads. “Best” here is a search phrase, not a test score. If you mainly need a meeting notetaker on Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams, start with Best AI meeting note takers for small teams. Related comparisons include Sonix vs Fireflies and Sonix vs Bluedot.

The four tools below are ones whose official pages describe file upload or import, and for which I have an approved affiliate URL. I also opened Noota and Laxis official pages the same day and did not find a clear file-upload line on the pages I read, so they are not given a section here.

How I compared the official pages

I looked for three things the official pages actually state: whether you can upload or import an existing audio or video file, whether interviews or podcasts are named as a use, and what you can try without paying. I did not convert currencies, and I did not repeat vendor accuracy or time-saved claims as if I had measured them.

Sonix

Sonix is built around files you already have. The homepage headline I saved is “Bring interviews, videos, audio, and documents together in one place.” The same page lists journalists, researchers, podcasters, and video producers under “For Professionals,” and says it is “Trusted by journalists, researchers, and content creators to transcribe, translate, and subtitle audio and video.” A journalists line describes “Interviews, press briefings, and recordings transformed into accurate transcripts.” I am repeating that official wording; I have not transcribed an interview in Sonix.

The homepage lists native-level transcription in 54+ languages, audio formats including MP3, WAV, M4A, and FLAC, and video formats including MP4, MOV, MKV, and AVI. A product line on the same page is “Upload once. Get everything.” The pricing page offers a 30-minute free trial, with no credit card required, and says you can cancel anytime. Pay as You Go is $10 an hour, with AI workspace usage not available, 5 GB of storage, and a single-user account. Core is $25 a month or $275 a year, with 5 hours a month or 60 hours a year of transcription and translation. Advanced is $50 a month or $550 a year. Pro is $80 a month or $880 a year. Extra hours on any subscription plan are $10 an hour. The AI meeting recorder page says Sonix Agent joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams and is included with Pro and Enterprise, while the free trial and Pay as You Go cover uploaded files, not the live agent. Longer write-ups are in the Sonix review and Sonix pricing.

Fireflies

Fireflies is primarily a meeting notetaker. On 21 November 2026, the pricing page listed “Upload audio/video file” among the key features on the Free card, which is $0, and the comparison listed “Upload and transcribe audio & video files.” The Free card also lists Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, transcription in 100+ languages, a desktop app, a mobile app, and a Chrome extension. The same page listed Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually, or $18 billed monthly, and Business at $19 billed annually. Official pages still disagree on some Free-plan limits; that conflict is walked through in Fireflies.ai pricing explained. I have not uploaded a podcast to Fireflies.

Bluedot

Bluedot is a bot-free meeting notetaker that also documents file imports. The homepage FAQ “Can I upload and transcribe existing files?” answers yes: you can upload MP4, MP3, M4A, and other common video or audio formats, and Bluedot will transcribe the file and generate a structured summary in any of its 100+ supported languages. The pricing FAQ “What does ‘import’ mean in Bluedot?” says these are existing recordings that you upload from your local computer, Google Drive, or other sources. The Annual cards I saved list “Zoom & Google Drive imports” on Pro, at $20 per user per month, and “Unlimited audio/video imports” on Business, at $32 per user per month. The Monthly cards list Pro at $25 and Business at $39. The Free card is $0, with 5 meetings for the life of the account; I am not inferring that the Free card includes the same imports as Pro or Business. Details are in the Bluedot review and Bluedot pricing.

Circleback

Circleback is a meeting notetaker whose homepage FAQ “Does Circleback work in-person?” says yes: you can record in-person meetings, “as well as import past audio or video meetings to get notes, action items, a searchable transcript and more.” That is the official import wording I found. The pricing page lists a $0 Free plan with unlimited meetings, a bot or the bot-free desktop app, and 30-day meeting and recording history. I have not imported a file into Circleback, and the FAQ does not spell out a format list like Sonix or Bluedot. Longer write-ups are in the Circleback review and Circleback pricing.

Who should look where

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested accuracy or any upload workflow.

Look at Sonix if you mainly have files to transcribe, want a 30-minute no-card trial of that upload path, or want Pay as You Go at $10 an hour instead of a monthly meeting seat, as the official pricing page states. Look at Fireflies if you already want a meeting notetaker and the official pricing page’s upload line is enough to try a file on the $0 card, after you have read the Free-plan limit notes in the Fireflies pricing guide. Look at Bluedot if you want a bot-free notetaker whose homepage FAQ documents MP4, MP3, and M4A uploads, and you will check whether the import you need sits on Pro or Business. Look at Circleback if you want a meeting notetaker that also says you can import past audio or video meetings, including on the way to its $0 Free plan with unlimited meetings and 30-day history.

FAQ

Which tools officially support uploading a recording?

As of 21 November 2026, Sonix’s official pages center on file uploads, including MP3, WAV, M4A, FLAC, MP4, MOV, MKV, and AVI. Fireflies’ pricing page lists “Upload audio/video file” among the Free card’s key features, and the comparison lists “Upload and transcribe audio & video files.” Bluedot’s homepage FAQ says you can upload MP4, MP3, M4A, and other common video or audio formats; the pricing FAQ defines an import as an existing recording uploaded from your computer, Google Drive, or other sources, and the plan cards list Zoom and Google Drive imports on Pro and unlimited audio or video imports on Business. Circleback’s homepage FAQ says you can import past audio or video meetings. I have not tested any upload path, and I did not find a clear file-upload line on the Noota or Laxis pages I read the same day.

Can I try transcription for $0?

Sonix offers a 30-minute free trial of uploaded recordings, with no credit card required. That trial does not include Sonix Agent, which is listed on Pro and Enterprise. Fireflies’ Free card is $0 and lists upload among its key features; official pages still disagree on some Free-plan limits, which is walked through in the Fireflies pricing guide on this site. I am not declaring a winner from those different $0 offers.

Is this a ranking of the best transcription tool?

No. “Best” here is a search phrase, not a test score. I compared only what official pages said about file uploads, interviews, and list prices on 21 November 2026. I have not transcribed a podcast or interview with these tools.
