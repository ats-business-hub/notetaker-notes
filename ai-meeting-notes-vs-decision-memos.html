AI Meeting Notes vs Decision Memos

AI meeting notes and a decision memo are easy to mix up because both are written records of work talk. They do different jobs. Notes help someone who missed the call, or you tomorrow, remember what was said. A decision memo records the question you are actually answering, the options you considered, the recommendation, who must respond, and — once it closes — the choice and the owner of the next step.

The difference in one pass

What each document is for
Question AI meeting notes (or any meeting notes) Decision memo
Job Capture what was said, and often a first-pass summary. Get a recorded answer to a stated question.
Typical shape A narrative, bullets, or a transcript-like recap, sometimes with suggested action items. One page: question, context, options, recommendation, objection window, and a space for the final choice.
When it is “done” When a person has checked names, dates, and figures, and the recap can be filed or sent. When required reviewers have answered, or the written window has ended, and the choice is written in the memo and in a log.
What it is weak at Turning a vague “we should maybe…” into an owner, a close date, and a yes or no. Replacing a conversation about tone, conflict, performance, or a live walkthrough.

Action items can sit in either document, but they are not the same as a decision. If the work is “send the revised invoice layout to Dana by Thursday,” that is an action. If the work is “should the default payment term stay at 14 days,” that is a decision and needs a memo, or at least a written question and a close date. The companion page decision log vs action-item tracker goes further on that split. For cleaning a notes draft into owners and dates, see how to turn AI meeting notes into action items.

When notes are the right record

Use notes, including a first draft from an AI notetaker, when the point of the writing is memory and share-out:

  • Someone missed the call and needs the discussion, not only the ending.
  • You need a recap of a client conversation, a walkthrough, or a negotiation that only makes sense as a sequence.
  • The next steps are already clear, and you only need owners and dates.

A person — not the draft itself — should still check names, dates, and figures before anything is forwarded. That is a process rule, not a claim about any one product’s accuracy.

When a decision memo is the right record

Write a memo when you need a choice to exist as a document, not only as a vibe at the end of a call:

  • The question can be written in one sentence (yes/no, or a short list of named options).
  • People can read the facts; you are not asking them to react only to tone or a demo that only works in the room.
  • The decision can wait for a written window — for example, one or two working days — with a close date and a timezone.
  • The topic is not a sensitive conversation about performance, conflict, health, or legal risk. Meet for those.

A useful test: if you cannot write the decision question in one sentence, you are not ready to send a memo, and you are probably not ready to book another meeting about it either.

Send the memo with a recommendation already in it. A page that only asks “thoughts?” usually becomes another meeting. Write the close date in the first message. If a required reviewer is out, extend the date in writing. Do not close a decision on a silent inbox if that person was marked required.

Worked example (fictional)

The studio, people, and notes below are invented for this page. They are not a client story, not a transcript from a real call, and not output I captured from a notetaker.

Harbor & Pine is a six-person design studio. After a 25-minute Monday check-in, this is the kind of recap that often gets pasted into chat:

Priya: Friday standups feel thin. Maybe we drop them in December?

Ken: We could try every other week.

Priya: Yeah, let’s think about it.

Alex: I’ll look at the calendar.

Also discussed: the Imai catalog draft is on track for Thursday, and Ken will send printer quotes.

The catalog work and the printer quotes are useful. The Friday-standup thread is not a decision. There is no stated question, no options written down, no close date, and “I’ll look at the calendar” is not an owner of a choice. Two weeks later, people may remember that “we were going to think about Fridays,” and they may book another meeting to have the same talk.

A one-page memo for the same question could look like this:

Fictional decision memo for Harbor & Pine — Friday standup in December
Field What to write
Decision question Should Harbor & Pine keep the Friday 16:30 standup through December, switch to every other week, or replace it with a written Friday status?
Why this, why now November Fridays have been short, and the December calendar is filling with client reviews. A default needs to be set before Priya sends the December schedule on 25 November.
Options A — Keep the weekly Friday standup. B — Move to every other Friday. C — Cancel the Friday standup for December and send a written Friday status instead. D — Do nothing this week and decide in January.
Recommendation C. The Monday check-in already covers live coordination. A written Friday status would keep a week-ending record without a standing call. Revisit in the first week of January.
Who must respond Priya (required), Ken and Alex (optional).
Objection window 24 November 2026, 17:00 Asia/Tokyo. If no required reviewer objects, proceed as recommended.
Decision (filled when it closes) Left blank until 24 November. Then: the choice, the date, and who owns the first written Friday status.

The meeting notes can still be filed. They explain how the question came up. The memo is what later answers “what did we decide, and who had to agree.”

If you already use an AI notetaker

A first draft from a notetaker can feed either document. It does not choose which one you need. Treat suggested action items as a starting list, then split them: work that already has an owner and a date, and questions that still need a written decision.

When you should still meet

A memo is the wrong tool when the decision is already blocked on a disagreement you have written down once, when someone will be surprised or put at risk, or when you need a whiteboard, a walkthrough, or a negotiation. In those cases, keep the notes, schedule the meeting, and write down that you converted the memo into a call, with a time. An async-first weekly update can still be the default for status; it does not have to be the default for every hard conversation.

FAQ

Are AI meeting notes enough to record a decision?

Not by themselves. Notes can show that a topic came up. A decision is recorded when the question, the options, the recommendation, the close date, and the final choice are written down, with an owner for the next step. A first draft from an AI notetaker can feed that memo; it does not replace it.

When should I skip the memo and just keep the notes?

When the next steps are already clear and you only need owners and dates, or when the point of the writing is to remember a discussion, a walkthrough, or a client conversation. Have a person check names, dates, and figures before you send the recap.
