Best AI Note Takers for In-Person Meetings (2026)

I read official pricing, product, and FAQ pages on 21 November 2026 (Japan time) to see which AI note takers document a way to capture a meeting that is not on Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams — a room, a coffee catchup, or another in-person conversation — and what you can try at $0 or €0. I have not recorded an in-person meeting with these tools, so this is not a ranking of audio quality, consent prompts, or how well a transcript holds up in a noisy room. “Best” here is a search phrase, not a test score. If you do not want a bot on a video call, see Bot-free AI meeting recorders. Related comparisons include Bluedot vs Laxis and Laxis vs Circleback.

The four tools below are ones whose official pages describe in-person capture, and for which I have an approved affiliate URL. Sonix’s official recorder page describes a live agent that joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams, or an upload path for a recording you already have; I did not find a dedicated in-person recorder on the Sonix pages I read, so Sonix is not given a section here.

How I compared the official pages

I looked for an explicit in-person, mobile, or Apple Watch capture line, whether the product is described as bot-free or offers a bot-free path, and the $0 or €0 starting point. I have not tested room audio, and I am not giving legal advice about consent. Several official pages still recommend telling people that a meeting is being recorded.

Bluedot

Bluedot’s homepage title is “Bluedot AI Note Taker | Bot-Free & Privacy-First.” The page says it “captures, transcribes, and summarises every meeting, online or in-person,” and, under “In-person meetings,” “Take notes at meetings or coffee catchups with iPhone, Android or Apple Watch.” The FAQ “Can I record in-person meetings?” answers yes, using the Bluedot mobile app on iOS or Android. The same homepage says your data is never used to train or improve AI models. The pricing page lists Free at $0 per user per month, with 5 meetings / lifetime, plus a Chrome extension and desktop and mobile apps. Annual cards list Basic at $14 (audio only), Pro at $20, and Business at $32 per user per month; Monthly cards list $18, $25, and $39. I have not used the mobile app or the Apple Watch app.

See Bluedot plans

Laxis

Laxis’s homepage says it transcribes every meeting into polished AI notes, tasks, and reports — “bot-free, in 100+ languages.” The same page describes a recorder for Apple Watch: “Record in-person conversations from your wrist, while your phone stays in your pocket.” The pricing page lists “Record online & in-person meetings” on the Basic card, which is $0, with 300 minutes of transcription a month and 30 days of conversation history. The comparison table lists a 45-minute cap per recording on Basic. The pricing FAQ says every new account starts with a 14-day free trial of Premium, with no credit card required, then moves to Basic. On the Yearly cards I saved, Premium is $9.99 per month and Business is $19.99 per month. The official billing help article also lists monthly rates of $15.99 and $29.99, and yearly totals of $119.88 and $239.88. I have not used the Apple Watch recorder.

See Laxis plans

Circleback

Circleback’s homepage FAQ “Does Circleback work in-person?” answers yes: you can record in-person meetings with Circleback, and you can also import past audio or video meetings. The pricing cards list “Online and in-person meetings,” “A bot or the bot-free desktop app for online meetings,” and “Mobile and Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings.” Those lines appear with the $0 Free plan, which also lists 30-day meeting and recording history. The pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings. Yearly cards list Pro at $15, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month; the page’s source list also has monthly amounts of $18 and $29 for Pro and Business. The desktop app is the bot-free path for online calls, not the in-person path. I have not used the mobile or Apple Watch app.

See Circleback plans

Noota

Noota’s AI note-taker page says Noota takes notes of online meetings, phone calls, and in-person conversations, “with or without a bot.” The same page’s FAQ says in-person meetings and phone calls are covered by the mobile app and the built-in recorder, and that Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, and Webex can be recorded with a bot or from the desktop app. The pricing table marks recording mode on Free as “Bot only,” and on Pro and above as “Bot or invisible.” Free is €0, with no card needed. The footnote says unlimited recording on Free requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. Pro is €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. A Black Friday offer of 40% off any new annual Pro or Business plan was still on the page through Monday, November 30, 11:59 pm (Paris time). Prices are in euros; I have not converted them. I have not used the mobile in-person recorder.

Who should look where

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested room audio, a watch app, or a mobile recorder.

Look at Bluedot if you want a product whose official homepage calls itself bot-free and names iPhone, Android, and Apple Watch for in-person meetings or coffee catchups, and you can accept a 5-meeting lifetime cap on Free. Look at Laxis if you want a bot-free official homepage, a 14-day no-card Premium trial, and a $0 Basic plan that lists in-person recording against a monthly minute pool. Look at Circleback if you want a $0 Free plan with unlimited meetings and 30-day history, plus official wording for mobile and Apple Watch in-person capture, or a bot-free desktop app when you are back on a video call. Look at Noota if you want European pricing and hosting language, a mobile in-person path on the official note-taker page, and you can accept a bot-only Free plan for some online meetings, or pay for Pro when you want the desktop “invisible” mode.

FAQ

Which tools officially record in-person meetings?

As of 21 November 2026, Bluedot’s homepage says it captures meetings online or in-person, and that you can take notes at meetings or coffee catchups with iPhone, Android, or Apple Watch. Laxis’s pricing page lists “Record online & in-person meetings,” and the homepage describes an Apple Watch recorder. Circleback’s homepage FAQ says yes, you can record in-person meetings, and the pricing cards list mobile and Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings. Noota’s AI note-taker page says it takes notes of online meetings, phone calls, and in-person conversations, with in-person meetings and phone calls covered by the mobile app and the built-in recorder. I have not recorded an in-person meeting with these tools.

Can I record in person without a meeting bot?

Bluedot’s homepage calls the product bot-free and says no bot joins your call. Laxis’s homepage presents the product as bot-free. Circleback’s official pages offer a bot or a bot-free desktop app for online meetings, and mobile or Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings. Noota’s pricing table marks Free as bot-only and places bot-free (“invisible”) desktop recording on Pro and above; the in-person path it describes is the mobile app and the built-in recorder. This page has not tested whether anyone else in the room is notified.

Which free plan covers in-person recording?

Circleback Free is $0 with unlimited meetings and 30-day history, and the pricing cards list mobile and Apple Watch apps for in-person meetings. Laxis Basic is $0, with 300 minutes a month, a 45-minute cap per recording, and 30 days of history, and the card lists recording of online and in-person meetings; new accounts also get a 14-day Premium trial with no credit card. Bluedot Free is $0 with a 5-meeting lifetime cap, and the Free card lists desktop and mobile apps. Noota Free is €0, with a footnote on when recording is unlimited. I am not declaring a winner from those different caps.
