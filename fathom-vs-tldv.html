Quick verdict

Fathom is worth shortlisting if you mainly want individual meeting capture, a $0 “Free forever” plan with unlimited recordings and transcriptions, and paid individual or team tiers listed in US dollars. tl;dv is worth shortlisting if you want a standing free plan with unlimited recordings and transcripts, plus published features for searching and analysing more than one meeting — with tighter free-tier caps on AI notes and storage. These are recommendations based on published product pages, not a comparison of summary quality.

What each product is

Fathom is an AI meeting notetaker. Its pricing and product pages describe meeting recording, transcription, instant AI call summaries, clips, playlists, and search across calls. The integrations menu lists Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Slack Huddles. A longer breakdown of the list prices is in the Fathom pricing guide.

tl;dv is an AI meeting recorder and notes platform. Official product pages describe recording and transcription, AI notes and summaries, multi-meeting insights, and integrations with video conferencing, CRMs, and automation tools. For a closer look at official claims, see the tl;dv review and tl;dv pricing.

Choosing between them is less about finding a universally better assistant and more about what happens after a call ends: a summary you can check against the transcript, or a searchable archive with team workflows — and which of those features are included in the tier you can pay for.

Recording, platforms, and summaries

Fathom’s integrations menu and product copy list Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Slack Huddles. The Free card lists a choice of bot-free (in beta) or bot capture; the feature grid describes bot-free capture as a beta feature for Mac. I have not tested bot-free capture, and I am not stating that it works on Windows.

tl;dv’s integrations hub says it can “Record and transcribe meetings from Google Meet, Zoom and MS Teams.” The official desktop app page is titled for recording without a bot. Official help, “Recording without a bot,” says the desktop app captures system audio and your microphone, not video, screen sharing, or chat, and that it can work with conferencing tools that output audio, including Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams. That article says you should inform participants, because recording notifications may not appear automatically in that mode.

Both products generate AI meeting summaries. Fathom distinguishes instant AI call summaries on Free from advanced summaries, which the help article caps at the first 5 calls each month on Free. tl;dv’s help article caps full automatic AI notes at the first 10 meetings on Free. AI summaries can omit context or misstate a tentative comment as a decision; important commitments should be checked against the transcript. I have not measured accuracy for either product.

Fathom’s help article says both Free and Premium include CRM sync with HubSpot, Salesforce, and Close for up to 3 users per email domain, plus a personal-use Zapier integration. Business-tier pricing copy describes unlimited CRM-connected users and automatic field updates. tl;dv’s integrations hub lists CRM logging, calendar capture, collaboration tools, project management, Zapier, and an API; the same page and footer also refer to 5,000+ or 6,000+ tools. Official help says the Free plan includes 20 integration credits for the lifetime of the account. Do not assume every integration is included on the free tier.

Languages

Fathom’s Free vs. Premium help article, read 25 November 2026, says unlimited transcriptions in 38 languages on both Free and Premium.

Official tl;dv pages still do not use one language count. The homepage, checked the same day, says it “transcribes and writes notes in 100+ languages.” The official languages page says transcriptions, notes, and reports are supported in “over 30 languages,” and that the interface is localized in multiple languages, including English, Spanish, French, Portuguese, Japanese, Korean, and German. The desktop app page also refers to “30+” languages. I am not treating either figure as a resolved count, and I have not tested transcription quality.

Privacy, security, and recording consent

Meeting recordings can include confidential business information. Both vendors publish privacy and security material: Fathom’s privacy policy and help center, and tl;dv’s privacy policy and a product-page claim of encrypted, GDPR-compliant services. I am not restating hosting regions or training-data rules from memory. If those details matter, read the current policies and any data-processing terms yourself.

Tell participants when a meeting is being recorded, especially if you use a bot-free mode that may not display an automatic notice. Applicable law and your own company or client rules can add further requirements.

Which tool may fit

Freelancers and independent consultants

Consider Fathom first if your priority is capturing individual client meetings, reading a summary, and preparing a follow-up, and if the Free or Premium individual cards match what you need. tl;dv may still fit if you often need to revisit discussions across many clients and can live with the Free plan’s lifetime AI-note cap, or you will pay for Pro.

Remote team leads

Consider tl;dv first if official multi-meeting insights, shared records, and team reporting are the point of buying a tool. Fathom Team and Business also document shared workspaces, team search, Ask Fathom, and, on Business, coaching metrics — compare the actual entitlements and the seat minimum before you choose.

Sales and customer-facing teams

Look past basic transcription. Compare CRM sync rules, team reporting, and admin controls on the tier you would actually buy. No performance winner can be established from product documentation alone.

Small businesses watching the bill

Start with the minimum features you need. If retaining notes and reducing manual typing is enough, try the official free tier first, and confirm the summary and storage caps. If you need cross-meeting analysis or CRM automation, add up the annual seat cost — including Fathom’s 2-user minimum on Team and Business — before you upgrade.

Common mistakes

Do not treat “unlimited recording” as unlimited advanced AI. Fathom’s free advanced-summary cap and tl;dv’s first-10-meetings AI-notes cap are easy to miss. Do not compare a dollar price with a euro price without checking checkout currency. Do not assume every advertised integration is on the entry-level plan. And do not skip a human check of decisions and customer commitments.

Final verdict

Shortlist Fathom if you want individual meeting capture, a documented free forever plan with unlimited recordings and transcriptions, and paid plans listed in US dollars, with team options from $15 per user per month billed annually (2-user minimum).

Shortlist tl;dv if you want published multi-meeting workflows and a free plan with unlimited recordings and transcripts, and you can accept lifetime AI-note and storage caps on Free, or you will pay Pro or Business at the euro list prices above.

Neither product should be described as categorically better without a comparison on your own meetings. If Fathom is in the running but you want other options, see Fathom alternatives, Fireflies vs Fathom, and Otter vs Fathom. If tl;dv is in the running, see tl;dv vs Fireflies and tl;dv vs Otter.

FAQ

Is Fathom or tl;dv cheaper?

As of 25 November 2026, Fathom lists prices in US dollars (Premium $16 per user per month billed annually, or $20 monthly) and tl;dv lists prices in euros (Pro €18 per seat per month billed annually, or €29 monthly). The currencies differ, so compare the live checkout currency and any promotion before calling one cheaper. Both publish a $0 or €0 free plan, with different usage caps.

Does Fathom or tl;dv have a free plan?

Yes. Fathom lists an individual Free plan at $0, “Free forever,” with unlimited recordings and transcriptions and instant AI call summaries. A help article says advanced summaries are limited to the first 5 calls each month on Free. tl;dv’s official structured data lists Free at €0.00 per seat per month. Official help says recordings and transcripts are unlimited, storage is 3 months, and full automatic AI notes cover the first 10 meetings only — a lifetime allowance, not a monthly renewal.

Do Fathom and tl;dv need a bot in the meeting?

Fathom’s Free card lists a choice of bot-free (in beta) or bot capture, and the feature grid describes bot-free capture as a beta feature for Mac. tl;dv officially records Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams with a bot, and publishes a desktop app for bot-free, audio-only capture. Official help says you should inform participants when using bot-free recording.
