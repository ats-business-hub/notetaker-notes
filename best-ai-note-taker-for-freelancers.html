Best AI Note Takers for Freelancers (2026)

I read official pricing, product, and FAQ pages on 21 November 2026 (Japan time) to see what a solo freelancer can try without buying a team plan: a standing $0 or €0 plan, a trial that does not require a credit card, or pay-as-you-go hours. I have not used these tools on client work, so this is not a ranking of accuracy, how a client sees a bot, or invoice-ready workflow. “Best” here is a search phrase, not a test score. If you only want the lowest published list price, see Cheapest AI meeting note takers in 2026. A wider catalog of $0 starting points is in Free plans and trials.

The six tools below are ones I can also link with an approved affiliate URL. I did not invent a “freelancer plan” where a vendor does not use that word. I used each official page’s own free, trial, or pay-as-you-go wording instead.

How I compared the official pages

Freelancers usually need a way to start alone, without buying seats for a team they do not have. I used each vendor’s own wording for the free plan or trial, any per-user or single-user note, and the first paid price a solo user would see. I did not convert Noota’s euro prices into US dollars, and I did not treat a lifetime 5-meeting cap as if it were the same as a monthly minute pool.

Circleback

Circleback’s pricing page lists Free at $0 per user per month, with AI meeting notes and action items, a bot or the bot-free desktop app, and 30-day meeting and recording history. The FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings. Yearly cards list Pro at $15, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35 per user per month; the same page’s source list also has monthly amounts of $18 and $29 for Pro and Business. I have not used Circleback on a client call. Longer write-ups are in the Circleback review and Circleback pricing.

Sonix

Sonix’s homepage says it delivers transcription “From solo creators to Fortune 500 teams.” The pricing page offers a 30-minute free trial, with no credit card required, and lists Pay as You Go at $10 an hour “For occasional projects,” with a single-user account, 5 GB of storage, and no AI workspace usage. Core is $25 a month or $275 a year, with 1 user included and extra seats billed separately, and the page says extra seats do not add more hours. Advanced is $50 a month or $550 a year, and Pro is $80 a month or $880 a year. Extra hours on a subscription are $10 an hour. The AI meeting recorder is included with Pro and Enterprise; the free trial covers uploaded files, not the live agent. This is a different starting point from a standing free meeting plan. Details are in the Sonix review, Sonix pricing, and Best AI note takers for recorded files, podcasts, and interviews.

See Sonix plans

Laxis

Laxis’s homepage says “Free to start · No credit card required,” and presents the product as bot-free, in 100+ languages. The pricing FAQ says every new account starts with a 14-day free trial of Premium, with no credit card, and that when the trial ends you move to the free Basic plan and keep all your notes. Basic is $0, with 300 minutes a month, 30 days of history, and recording of online and in-person meetings; the comparison table lists a 45-minute cap per recording. Yearly cards list Premium at $9.99 and Business at $19.99 per month; the billing help article also lists $15.99 and $29.99 billed monthly. Website purchases are billed in USD through Stripe, the pricing FAQ says. I have not used Laxis on freelance calls.

See Laxis plans

Noota

Noota’s pricing page lists Free at €0, with no card needed, one month of storage, and unlimited meeting notes with an asterisk. The footnote, repeated in the FAQ, says unlimited recording on Free requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. The pricing table marks Free as “Bot only,” and places bot-free (“invisible”) desktop recording on Pro and above. Pro is €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. A Black Friday offer of 40% off any new annual Pro or Business plan was still on the page through Monday, November 30, 11:59 pm (Paris time). All prices are in euros; I have not converted them. Details are in the Noota review and Noota pricing.

See Noota plans

Bluedot

Bluedot’s homepage says the product is built “From solo founders to Fortune 500.” Official pages present it as bot-free. The Free card is $0 per user per month, with 5 meetings / lifetime, and lists a Chrome extension plus desktop and mobile apps. The homepage FAQ calls that a lifetime trial of core features for the first 5 meetings, after which you can continue as a free member to view and share recordings, or upgrade. Annual cards list Basic at $14 (audio only), Pro at $20, and Business at $32 per user per month; Monthly cards list $18, $25, and $39. A 5-meeting lifetime cap is not the same as a monthly minute pool. Details are in the Bluedot review and Bluedot pricing.

Fireflies

Fireflies’ pricing page, on 21 November 2026, listed a Free card at $0, “For individuals starting out,” with upload of an audio or video file among the key features, plus Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, and transcription in 100+ languages. Pro was $10 per seat per month billed annually, or $18 billed monthly, and Business was $19 billed annually. Official pages still disagree on some Free-plan limits; that conflict is walked through in Fireflies.ai pricing explained. I have not used Fireflies as a freelancer. Who should look where

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested a freelance client call on any of these tools.

Look at Circleback if you want a standing $0 plan with unlimited meetings and a 30-day history cap, and official wording for a bot or a bot-free desktop app. Look at Sonix if your work is files more than live meetings, and you want a 30-minute no-card upload trial or Pay as You Go at $10 an hour. Look at Laxis if you want a 14-day no-card Premium trial, then a $0 Basic plan with a monthly minute pool, including in-person recording as the official card states. Look at Noota if you want a €0 starting plan and European pricing, and you can live with the Free-plan footnote and a bot-only Free recording mode. Look at Bluedot if you want a bot-free official homepage and can accept a 5-meeting lifetime cap on Free. FAQ

What can a freelancer try without a credit card?

As of 21 November 2026, Circleback Free is $0, with unlimited meetings and 30-day history. Laxis starts new accounts on a 14-day Premium trial with no credit card, then moves you to Basic at $0, with 300 minutes a month, a 45-minute cap per recording, and 30 days of history. Noota Free is €0 with no card; unlimited recording on that plan requires a connected calendar, Join all meetings, and auto-sharing the summary, otherwise the cap is 300 minutes a month. Bluedot Free is $0, with 5 meetings for the life of the account. Fireflies’ Free card is $0; official pages still disagree on some Free-plan limits. Sonix offers a 30-minute free trial of uploaded recordings, with no credit card, not a standing free monthly plan. I am not declaring a winner from those different offers.

Which option fits occasional work rather than a monthly seat?

Sonix’s pricing page lists Pay as You Go at $10 an hour, with a single-user account, for occasional projects, plus Core at $25 a month or $275 a year with a 5-hour monthly allowance. The other tools on this page are priced mainly as monthly or yearly seats or plans. I have not turned Sonix hours into meeting seats, and I have not tested a freelancer workload on any of them.

It is not a ranking of the lowest price, and it is not a hands-on test.
