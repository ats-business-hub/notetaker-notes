Weekly Async Status Update Template

A weekly async status is a short written update, sent on a fixed day, that a teammate can read in a few minutes — including on a phone. It is not meeting notes, and it is not a decision memo. Notes remember a conversation. A memo records a choice. A status says what got done, what is next, and which decisions are still open.

Below is a free template you can copy, plus a filled fictional example. This page is a process guide, not a hands-on test of Slack, Teams, Notion, or any AI notetaker. If your week still includes calls you want captured, the tool comparisons live on pages such as the best AI meeting note takers guide and the Fireflies.ai review.

The shape that stays readable

A reliable weekly status has a period, an author, a goal for the period, a Done list, a Next list, a Decisions needed section, risks or waits only when they change the plan, and links to the longer doc, ticket, or memo. Keep decisions in their own section. Do not bury a yes/no inside a status bullet.

Write outcomes (“invoice layout sent to Dana”) rather than effort (“worked on invoices”). If nothing important happened, say so in one line. An empty update is better than a padded one. Send it even when you are the only person on the project; future you is a reader.

Put a timezone on the sent time, and on every decision due date. That matters more once two people are not in the same place. For a fuller cadence — when to send, when the objection window closes, and when you still meet — see how to run an async-first weekly team update.

Filled example (fictional)

The company, people, and figures below are invented for layout only. They are not a client story and not a record of work that was done.

Fictional weekly status — Cedar Route Logistics, dispatch cleanup
Field Example
Team / project Cedar Route Logistics — dispatch board cleanup
Author Riley Cho
Period 17–21 November 2026
Sent 21 November 2026, 16:00 Asia/Tokyo
Goal for this period Get the “on-time / delayed / waiting on dock” labels agreed so Dana can use them on the live board on 24 November.
Done
  • Drafted a three-label set and sent it to Dana and Mateo
  • Listed 9 routes from last week where the old “pending” label hid a dock wait
Next
  • Dana confirms or amends the labels — Dana, 24 November
  • Riley loads the agreed labels onto the board — Riley, 25 November
Decisions needed Do we treat a dock wait over 45 minutes as “delayed” or keep a separate “waiting on dock” label? Needed from Dana (required), Mateo (optional). By 24 November 2026, 15:00 Asia/Tokyo. Memo to follow if Dana wants the options written out.
Risks or waits Two Friday routes still use a driver-only spreadsheet. If those are not moved by 25 November, the new labels will cover the main board only; say so in the next status rather than delaying the whole change.
Links Label draft; list of 9 routes.

What this example is doing: the goal is one outcome, Done is written as results, Next has owners and dates, and the open choice is not hidden inside “worked on labels.” If Dana needs options and a recommendation on the 45-minute rule, that is a decision memo, not another status bullet.

How to send it

  • Pick one channel and one day. Changing both every week is how people stop reading.
  • Write the status before you offer a meeting about the same period. If a decision is blocked, say so in Decisions needed, then schedule a call. Do not replace the written update with “we’ll talk Friday.”
  • Have a person check names, dates, and figures before you hit send.
  • If you also record meetings, keep the status short and link out. The status is not a place to paste a transcript. For turning a notes draft into owners, see turning AI meeting notes into action items.

FAQ

Can I use the template on this page for free?

Yes. The blank template and the fictional filled example are free to copy. The paid Async Status & Decision Memo Pack is my own product; it adds a decision memo, message templates, and a decision tracker. You do not need the pack to use this page.

How long should a weekly async status be?

Short enough to read on a phone. If you need a long narrative, link out to the document and keep the status as outcomes, next steps, and open decisions.

What if I have no decisions this week?

Write “None” in Decisions needed. Keep the section. Skipping it is how a yes/no gets buried in a Done bullet the following week.
