AI Note Takers for Mixed Japanese–English Meetings (2026)

Last checked: 26 November 2026, against official vendor help and product pages. The question this page tries to answer is narrower than “does it support Japanese?” It is whether the official docs describe a meeting in which people switch between Japanese and English, or only a single transcription language per recording. I have not used these products, so this is not a test of accuracy or of how often a mixed meeting is recognised correctly.

Free allowances, trials, and paid list prices are in Free plans and trials: Fireflies vs MeetGeek vs Krisp and in each product’s pricing page on this site. Bot-free capture is in Bot-free AI meeting note takers.

What the official pages actually describe

  • Fireflies: Official help describes Multi-Language (Auto-Detect) mode, marked Beta, for multiple spoken languages in the same meeting. Ordinary Auto-Detect transcribes the dominant language only. Japanese is listed for transcription and summaries, code ja.

    See Fireflies plans

  • Otter: Japanese is a supported transcription language. Official help says to select the language spoken in the audio before recording. Translation of an existing transcript, including a Japanese summary of an English meeting, is described as an Otter AI Chat feature, not as mixed-language transcription.

    See Otter’s official site

How to read this table

“Japanese transcription listed” only means the vendor’s official page includes Japanese on a language list. It is not a quality score. A bilingual meeting can still be transcribed poorly in a supported language, and a single-language setting can still be the wrong setting if speakers switch mid-call.

Where a vendor documents auto-detect, read the wording. “Dominant language” or “language spoken at the start of the meeting” is not the same as transcribing two languages in one transcript.

Fireflies

The Fireflies supported-languages article, checked 26 November 2026, says Fireflies supports transcription and summaries in 100+ languages, including Japanese (ja). It also offers:

  • Auto-Detect — transcribes the dominant language automatically.
  • Multi-Language (Auto-Detect) (Beta) — “detects and transcribes multiple languages in the same meeting,” described as useful when speakers switch languages.

Display language for the app is separate and “does not affect meeting transcription or summary languages.” I have not tested Multi-Language mode, and this page does not claim a plan name or a quality level for the beta. Plan limits are in Fireflies Free plan limits and Fireflies pricing.

See Fireflies plans

Otter

Otter’s supported-languages article was updated 10 November 2026 and rechecked 26 November 2026. Japanese is on the transcription list, together with English (US and UK) and a number of other languages. Official help says: select the language spoken in the original audio before recording or importing a file, and you can reprocess an existing conversation in a different transcription language on desktop or web.

The same article is explicit that transcription and translation are different. Choosing Japanese tells Otter that Japanese is spoken in the audio. It does not, by itself, translate English speech into Japanese. For translation, Otter documents AI Chat: for example, translating an English transcript, or asking in Japanese for a summary of an English conversation, without changing the original transcript language.

I did not find a Fireflies-style mixed-language transcription mode on that Otter article. Free-plan limits are in Otter Free plan limits.

MeetGeek and Circleback

MeetGeek’s language-settings article, checked 26 November 2026, lists Japanese (Japan) and describes automatic language detection as identifying the language spoken at the start of the meeting. You can also set a default language or a per-meeting language. MeetGeek is not an approved affiliate on this site, so product links stay on the official domain.

See MeetGeek’s official site

Circleback’s meeting-languages help, checked the same day, lists Japanese (日本語) and says Circleback “automatically detects the predominantly-spoken language and transcribes the entire meeting in that language.” You can choose a language for AI notes and action items in settings; “the meeting transcript will always be in the language that was spoken during the meeting.” That is a predominant-language model, not a documented mixed transcript.

See Circleback plans

Fathom and Krisp

Fathom’s languages help says it supports transcripts in 38 languages, including Japanese and English. Bold type on that page marks languages that also get translated summaries for Premium and Team Edition users. Automatic translated summaries are described when calls are detected to be in Spanish, Portuguese, German, French, Italian, or Dutch. Japanese is not in that automatic-translation list. I have not tested Fathom on a mixed Japanese–English call. See Fathom pricing and free plan.

Krisp should not be chosen for Japanese meeting transcripts on the basis of the AI Note Taker language list. The official recording help, checked 26 November 2026, lists 16 transcription languages and does not include Japanese. Japanese does appear on the Krisp AI Voice Translation language list, which is a separate, conversation-translation feature. See the Krisp review and free plans.

See Krisp plans

tl;dv and Noota

tl;dv’s official languages page, checked 26 November 2026, says the platform is localized in several languages including Japanese, and that transcriptions, notes, and reports are supported in over 30 languages, “from Portuguese to Mexican, Japanese, Bulgarian, and beyond.” I did not find a sentence that one meeting is transcribed in Japanese and English together. Free-plan limits are in tl;dv Free plan limits.

Noota’s supported-languages article lists Japanese. The copy I fetched on 26 November 2026 still showed a 15 April 2024 date, so treat it as official but possibly stale, and recheck before you rely on it. I did not find a mixed-meeting mode there. See the Noota review.
