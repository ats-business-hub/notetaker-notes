2. Cut costs by starting with a free plan, if the limits fit

The lowest cost is $0, when the published free allowance covers how often you meet and how long you need to keep notes. The details live on the free plans guide. The cost-cutting points from pages checked today are:

  • Circleback Free publishes unlimited meetings at $0, with 30-day meeting and recording history. If 30 days of history is enough, the official page does not require a paid seat for meeting volume alone. Every plan, including Free, is described as having unlimited meetings.
  • Tactiq Free is $0, free forever, with no credit card, 10 transcripts a month, and 5 AI credits a month. Tactiq’s help article says unused credits do not roll over, and that AI features pause when you hit the credit limit until the next month or an upgrade, while existing transcripts stay accessible.
  • Laxis Basic is $0, with 300 minutes a month, 30-day conversation history, and a 45-minute cap per recording. The FAQ says meetings are archived, not deleted, after 30 days, and that upgrading unlocks them again. New accounts start with a 14-day Premium trial and no credit card, then move to Basic.
  • MeetGeek Basic is free forever, with no credit card, 3 hours of transcription a month, 3 months of transcript storage, 1 month of audio storage, and recordings of up to 2 hours. The pricing page also says you can combine free and paid licenses on one team.
  • Fireflies Free is listed at $0, “Free forever.” The pricing page I checked today lists unlimited transcription with an asterisk (the tooltip says this is for shared bot meetings, the Chrome extension, and the mobile app), 400 minutes of storage per team, and limited AI summaries, without a number next to that summary cap on the pricing table. Other official Fireflies pages have described Free-plan limits differently. I am not choosing one figure here; see the free plans guide and Fireflies pricing explained.
  • Noota Free is described as free to start, with no card, one month of storage, and unlimited meeting notes, plus a footnote: unlimited recording on Free requires a connected calendar with “Join all meetings” enabled, and the summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes a month.
  • Bluedot Free is $0, but only 5 meetings for the lifetime of the account, not an ongoing monthly allowance. That is easy to exhaust if you want a long-term free option.
  • Krisp publishes a 7-day free trial at $0 with no credit card, not an ongoing free plan.
  • Sonix publishes a 30-minute free trial with no credit card, not an ongoing free plan. The FAQ says remaining free minutes are applied to your first upload. For occasional files, Pay As You Go at $10 per hour can be cheaper than a monthly seat; for steady volume, compare it with Core at $25 a month or $275 a year for 5 hours a month. More detail is on Sonix pricing explained.

3. Annual versus monthly billing

Where a vendor lists both cycles, annual billing is the lower monthly equivalent. The savings below are the official figures, not amounts I calculated from a discount percentage alone.

  • Fireflies Pro: $10 per seat per month billed annually, or $18 billed monthly.
  • Tactiq Pro: $8 per user per month billed annually, or $12 billed monthly ($96 versus a monthly rate; the help article calls this a 33% saving). Team is $16.67 annually ($200 a year) or $20 monthly (a 17% saving, per the same article). The public pricing page highlights the annual rates.
  • Bluedot: Basic $14 annually or $18 monthly; Pro $20 or $25; Business $32 or $39.
  • Noota: Pro €19 annually or €29 monthly; Business €39 or €49. The page says there is no discount on monthly plans for the current first-year annual promotion.
  • MeetGeek: Pro $9.99 billed yearly or $15.99 monthly; Business $17 yearly or $27 monthly. The page says paying yearly saves up to 40%.
  • Krisp: Core $8 annually or $16 monthly; Advanced $15 annually or $30 monthly. The page labels this “Annually Save 50%.”
  • Circleback: Pro, Business, and Enterprise cards showed $15, $25, and $35 per user per month. The FAQ’s 50-user example is calculated on annual billing. I did not find a different monthly list price on that page.
  • Laxis: The public page and its schema describe Premium as $9.99 per month billed yearly and Business as $19.99 per month billed yearly, with a control labeled “Save up to 38%” on yearly billing. I am not publishing a monthly list price I did not see printed.
  • Sonix: Core is $25 a month or $275 a year, Advanced $50 or $550, Pro $80 or $880. Those yearly totals are the figures on the pricing page, not conversions I made.

Annual billing only saves money if you keep the tool for the year. Official cancellation language varies; several pages say you can cancel, but I have not tested the checkout flow.

5. Seats, viewers, and published discounts

Per-user pricing adds up quickly on a team. A few official pages describe ways to avoid paying for every teammate, or list a discount category. I have not applied for these discounts, and I am not adding amounts a vendor did not publish.

  • Fireflies bills per seat for teammates added to the workspace, and says you can share individual recap links without adding those people as paid seats.
  • Circleback says Business and Enterprise include unlimited free viewers, so teammates who only read shared notes do not need a paid seat. The FAQ also lists a 30% non-profit discount (contact support after signing up with a non-profit email) and a 50% education discount for students (school email, or contact support with proof).
  • MeetGeek says you can combine free and paid licenses on one team, and that startups, non-profits, and educational organizations get a 30% lifetime discount.
  • Tactiq points students and teachers to a separate education-plan application. I am not publishing a discount percentage the public pricing page did not state.
  • Laxis says it offers specialized pricing for non-profits and educational institutions, and asks those groups to contact its partnership team.
  • Sonix says it has special discounted pricing for students, teachers or faculty, and non-profits, and separately mentions discounted pricing for a 300+ word online article about Sonix. It does not print those amounts on the public pricing page, so I am not inventing them.
  • Noota says seats are unlimited on every plan and each person pays for the plan they need, with volume pricing for 50+ seats on Enterprise.

6. A simple way to choose without over-buying

  1. Count how many meetings you need to capture in a typical month, and how long you need to keep the notes or recordings. Match that to a free plan first.
  2. If a free plan is too tight, compare the first paid tier on annual billing, and read what that tier still limits (AI credits, audio-only recording, storage length, or hours).
  3. Pay monthly only if you expect to cancel inside a few months. The official annual rates above are lower, but they assume you keep the subscription.
  4. If you mostly transcribe uploaded files, compare Sonix Pay As You Go with a monthly meeting-notetaker seat, using the hours you actually upload.
  5. Recheck the official page on the day you buy. The figures on this page are from 21 November 2026.

For product differences beyond price — bots versus bot-free capture, Zoom or Google Meet fit, or leaving Otter — start with Best AI meeting note takers for small teams, bot-free recorders, or Otter.ai alternatives.

FAQ

What is the cheapest paid AI meeting note taker?

Plans are not equivalent, so there is no single cheapest product. Among first paid tiers on official pages I checked on 21 November 2026, Tactiq Pro and Krisp Core were both $8 per user per month on annual billing. MeetGeek Pro was $9.99 billed yearly, Laxis Premium was $9.99 billed yearly, and Fireflies Pro was $10 billed annually. Those are list prices, not a ranking of quality. I have not hands-on tested the tools.

Can I use an AI meeting note taker for free?

Yes, on several ongoing free plans, if the published limits fit. Circleback Free, Tactiq Free, Laxis Basic, MeetGeek Basic, Fireflies Free, and Noota Free are all listed at $0 or as free to start. Bluedot Free is only 5 meetings for the lifetime of the account. Sonix and Krisp offer trials (30 minutes and 7 days), not ongoing free plans. See section 2 and the free plans guide.

Is annual billing cheaper than monthly?

Where official pages list both, yes. Examples from 21 November 2026 include Fireflies Pro at $10 versus $18, Tactiq Pro at $8 versus $12, MeetGeek Pro at $9.99 versus $15.99, and Krisp Core at $8 versus $16. I did not publish a monthly Circleback or Laxis price I could not see clearly on the public page.

Are these prices in US dollars?

Most of the pages I checked list USD. Noota states that all prices are in euros. I have not converted euros into dollars.

Do current promotions change which plan is cheapest?

They can, for a limited time. On 21 November 2026, Noota listed 40% off annual Pro and Business for the first year, through 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time. MeetGeek listed 30% off Pro and Business for 12 months with the code BLACKFRIDAY2026, in a scripted window through 30 November 2026, 23:59 PST. Promotions expire. Check the live page before you buy.

Have you tested these tools yourself?

No. This is a researched summary of official vendor pages checked on 21 November 2026. It is not a hands-on test of accuracy, apps, or how a free-plan limit behaves in daily use.
