Noota vs Sonix for Small Teams (2026)

How each one records

Noota’s AI note-taker page says Noota takes notes of online meetings, phone calls, and in-person conversations, “with or without a bot.” The same page’s FAQ says Noota records Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, and Webex meetings, with a bot that joins the call or without one from the desktop app, and that in-person meetings and phone calls are covered by the mobile app and the built-in recorder. The pricing FAQ says Noota can join as a bot with the name you choose, or record quietly from your computer with the desktop app on the Pro plan and above, and that participants are notified that the meeting is recorded. The pricing comparison table marks recording mode on Free as “Bot only,” and on Pro, Business, and Enterprise as “Bot or invisible.” I have not watched either capture path on a call.

Sonix’s AI meeting recorder page says Sonix Agent joins your Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams call and writes the transcript while people talk. It says speakers are named from the call, 52 languages work live, and the agent is included with Pro and Enterprise plans, while the free trial covers uploaded recordings. A comparison on that page lists “Sonix Agent in the call” on Pro and Enterprise, and “Upload the recording” on all plans and the free trial. The FAQ says the agent does not join Webex or other meeting apps, so those recordings should be uploaded instead, and that the free trial and Pay as You Go cover uploaded files, not the live agent.

Free plans and trials

Noota’s Free card is €0, with no card needed, and lists unlimited meeting notes with an asterisk. The footnote, repeated in the FAQ, says unlimited recording on the Free plan requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live recordings and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. The Free card also lists one month of storage.

Sonix’s pricing page offers a 30-minute free trial, with no credit card required, and says you can cancel anytime. It does not present a standing $0 monthly plan like Noota Free. The AI meeting recorder FAQ, “Is the AI meeting recorder part of the free trial?”, answers no: the meeting recorder comes with Pro and Enterprise, and the free trial and Pay as You Go cover uploaded files. The two $0 starting points are not the same kind of offer, and I am not declaring a winner.

Paid prices, as of 21 November 2026

Noota’s pricing page says all prices are in euros (EUR). Pro is €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. Business is €39 billed annually, or €49 billed monthly. A banner and the plan cards described Black Friday as 40% off any new annual Pro or Business plan until Monday, November 30, 11:59 pm (Paris time), which brought the first-year annual rates on the cards I saved to €11.40 per user per month (€136.80 for the first year instead of €228) and €23.40 (€280.80 instead of €468). The page says there is no discount on the monthly plan. I have not converted euros to dollars.

Sonix’s pricing page lists Pay as You Go at $10 an hour, with AI workspace usage not available, 5 GB of storage, and a single-user account. Core is $25 a month or $275 a year, with 5 hours a month or 60 hours a year of transcription and translation, plus a separate 5 hours a month or 60 hours a year of AI workspace usage, and 25 GB of storage. Advanced is $50 a month or $550 a year, with 20 / 240 transcription hours, 25 / 300 AI workspace hours, and 50 GB of storage. Pro is $80 a month or $880 a year, with 40 / 480 transcription hours, 100 / 1,200 AI workspace hours, and 100 GB of storage. Extra hours on any subscription plan are $10 an hour. Extra seats are $25 a month or $275 a year and, the page says, do not add more hours. The AI meeting recorder page says the recorder is part of Pro at $80 a month and of every Enterprise plan. I have not turned Sonix hours into Noota seats.

Who should pick which

These suggestions follow only what the official pages say. I have not tested accuracy, the bot, the desktop recorder, or Sonix Agent.

Look at Noota if you want a meeting notetaker whose official pages offer a bot or, on Pro and above, a bot-free (“invisible”) desktop app, prices and hosting described in European terms (the pricing and AI note-taker pages say meeting data is hosted in Europe, with a France option, and that data is not used to train AI models; the pricing page also lists ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II), a first-year Black Friday annual rate that was still on the page as of this check, and you can accept a bot-only Free plan, or pay for Pro when you want the desktop “invisible” mode. Look at Sonix if you mainly upload audio or video, want a 30-minute no-card trial of that upload path, and can move to Pro when you want Sonix Agent in Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams, as the official recorder page states.

FAQ

Can I record a live meeting?

Noota’s official pages offer a bot, or bot-free (“invisible”) desktop recording on Pro and above; Free is marked bot-only. Sonix Agent joins Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams and is included with Pro and Enterprise; the free trial covers uploads, not the agent. This page has not tested either product.

What can I try for $0 or €0?

Noota Free is a standing €0 plan. Unlimited recording on that plan requires a connected calendar, Join all meetings, and auto-sharing the summary; otherwise the cap is 300 minutes a month, with one month of storage. Sonix offers a 30-minute free trial of uploaded recordings, not a standing free monthly plan and not the live agent. I am not declaring a winner from those different offers.

Which costs less, Noota or Sonix?

They publish prices in different currencies, and I have not converted them. As of 21 November 2026, Noota Pro was €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly, with a first-year Black Friday annual rate of €11.40 still on the page through the dated offer ending Monday, November 30. Sonix Core was $25 a month or $275 a year, and Sonix Pro, which includes the agent, was $80 a month or $880 a year. Check both live pages before you buy.
