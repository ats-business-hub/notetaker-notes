Fathom vs Notta at a glance

Fathom and Notta both record meetings, transcribe them, and produce AI summaries. Their official pages (checked 26 November 2026) show different priorities. This comparison is built from those pages, not from hands-on testing. Fathom's free tier has no limit on meetings and puts most of its paid features into team, sales, and CRM workflows. Notta meters usage by minutes and summaries, supports far more transcription languages, and accepts uploaded audio, video, and YouTube links.

Pricing compared

Fathom

Fathom's pricing page has a Monthly/Annually toggle, with annual billing advertised as saving 25%+. Each paid plan shows two different per-user monthly prices depending on billing period:

  • Free: $0, "Free forever"
  • Premium (individuals): $16 per user per month billed annually, $20 billed monthly
  • Team: $15 annually / $19 monthly per user per month, 2-user minimum
  • Business: $25 annually / $34 monthly per user per month, 2-user minimum
  • Enterprise: annual billing only. The page text shows $35 per user per month (a $34 figure also appears next to it), so confirm the quote with Fathom.

Check the toggle on the pricing page to confirm the rate for your billing period before you buy. The page also shows a 90-day guarantee next to each plan.

Fathom lists some programs separately: startups backed by select VCs and accelerators can get up to 2 years of Fathom Team for free, nonprofits can apply for 10 free seats, and teams switching from Gong ("or something similar") can get Fathom Business free through their contract, plus data migration.

Notta

In the text of Notta's English pricing page, Pro and Business show only annual-billing prices. There is a Monthly toggle, but no monthly rate for either plan appeared in the page text we checked, so confirm it at checkout. Pro is listed as "$8.17 USD/month Total $97.99 · Billed annually," and Business as "$16.67 USD/month Total $199.99 · billed annually." Enterprise has custom pricing, "Starting from 51 seats." Every plan, including Free, gets up to 4 free uses a month of bilingual transcription and translation and up to 2 of monolingual real-time translation. Heavier translation use and extra Notta Brain credits are paid add-ons for Pro and above. For details, see Notta pricing (2026).

How the pricing models differ

The two vendors charge in different ways.

  • Fathom charges per user. Team and Business require at least 2 users. On the pricing page, recordings, call storage, transcription, and call downloads are marked as unlimited.
  • Notta charges per plan with usage quotas. Pro is a single seat with 1,800 transcription minutes, 100 AI summaries, and 100 file transcriptions per month. Business makes transcription minutes unlimited but keeps quotas on AI summaries (200) and file transcriptions (200).

For a single person, Notta Pro's published annual price is lower than Fathom Premium's. Fathom's Free plan, however, has no minute cap. Which is cheaper in practice depends on how many meetings you record and which paid features you need.

For the full Fathom breakdown, see Fathom Pricing and Free Plan (2026).

Free plans compared

This is the biggest difference between the two tools.

Fathom Free: The features page lists unlimited meetings, transcription, and AI summaries, along with the Fathom Notetaker, bot-free capture (Mac beta), multi-language support, and the iOS app. The pricing page adds clips, playlists, and search across calls. Some features in Fathom's comparison table are marked "Limited use," including AI-generated action items and account-wide Ask Fathom. The table does not clearly show which plan each marker applies to.

Notta Free: The transcription quota (no carryover) is "120 minutes per month," and the Free plan card says "Up to 3 minutes per conversation." You also get 10 AI summaries and 50 file transcriptions per month.

In practice, Fathom Free can record full-length meetings on an ongoing basis. Notta Free is better suited to testing transcription quality on short clips before you pay. For more on free tiers, see Free plans and trials: Fireflies vs MeetGeek vs Krisp (November 2026).

Capture style: bot, bot-free, mobile, and uploads

Fathom

Fathom offers both bot and bot-free capture. The features page describes three options: send the Fathom Notetaker, capture bot-free on Mac, or use your phone for in-person meetings.

  • Notetaker (bot): Works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, on Mac and Windows.
  • Bot-free capture: Records calls from the Fathom desktop app on Mac with no notetaker in the meeting. It is in beta and available on every plan, including Free. A Fathom user must be in the meeting. Bot-free capture works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack Huddles. Windows support is listed as coming.
  • Desktop app requirements: Mac only, macOS 14.2 or later.
  • iOS app: Captures in-person meetings and lets you review calls on an iPhone.
  • In-meeting experience: Live summaries and a private Scratchpad during the meeting.

Notta

Notta also offers both. Its homepage describes an online meeting recorder that records "via a bot that joins your call," and a bot-free recorder that runs on your device. Other capture options:

  • A Chrome extension that captures from any browser tab
  • iOS and Android apps
  • A desktop app for Mac and Windows, a web app, and screen recording
  • Transcription of audio files, video files, or YouTube links
  • Notta Memo, a separate hardware device that attaches to a phone

Notta lists Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex. Its FAQ calls the Zoom, Teams, and Google Meet integrations native.

Capture style takeaway

If you use Windows or Android, or need Webex, Notta's official pages list more options. Fathom's bot-free mode is currently Mac-only and in beta. Notta also accepts recordings made outside meetings, such as uploaded files and YouTube links. Fathom's pages reviewed here don't list file upload.

Fathom's bot-free capture is available on its Free plan. Both vendors let you keep a bot out of the call. For a broader roundup, see Bot-Free AI Meeting Note Takers: Bluedot, Circleback, Laxis, and Tactiq (2026).

Languages and translation

Fathom: The features page lists transcripts and summaries in seven languages: English, Spanish, French, German, Italian, Dutch, and Portuguese. Ask Fathom can translate transcripts into 29 more languages.

Notta: The homepage says it supports "58 different languages for transcription and translation." It also offers real-time translation in meetings and bilingual transcription. Every plan, including Free, includes up to 4 bilingual uses and 2 monolingual real-time translation uses a month. Transcript translation is listed from Pro up. For heavier use, the Monolingual Translation and Bilingual Transcription & Translation add-ons cost $6 and $9 per month billed annually ($72 and $108 a year), or $10 and $15 a month on a monthly plan.

If your meetings are in Japanese, Chinese, Korean, or another language outside Fathom's seven, Notta is the only one of the two whose official pages list transcription support for it.

AI summaries and asking questions across meetings

Fathom says AI summaries are ready right after your call. Free includes instant AI call summaries. Premium adds advanced summaries, AI-generated action items, and a conversational meeting assistant. Ask Fathom lets you chat with a single call or across your team's calls. The pricing page says paid plans have unlimited lookback for "my calls," and the lookback window for team calls depends on plan tier. The pricing page also lists more than 15 meeting templates, including BANT and Sandler.

Notta offers AI Notes with summaries and action items, counted against a monthly quota: 10 on Free, 100 on Pro, 200 on Business, and unlimited on Enterprise. Notta Brain adds cross-meeting Q&A, infographics, document building, and follow-up drafts, paid for with AI credits. The comparison table lists 1,000 credits a month on every plan, and the Notta Brain add-on costs $93.59 USD/year for 8,000 AI Credits/month.

Fathom's pricing page describes summaries as unlimited. On Notta, AI summaries are a quota on every plan below Enterprise.

Team, sales, and CRM features

Fathom's upper tiers are built around sales teams:

  • Team: shared workspace with folders, comments, mentions, and playlists; Trackers and Ask Fathom across team calls; team security controls and HIPAA compliance with a standard BAA
  • Business: CRM field sync that updates records automatically after meetings, Deal View, coaching metrics, and AI scorecards
  • Enterprise: Launch Assist implementation help, custom-built summaries, SSO and SCIM provisioning, custom data retention, and priority support SLAs

Notta's team features start at Business. Business adds web meeting video recording, advanced data security controls, a usage report, and CRM and Zapier integration. Enterprise adds SAML SSO, audit logs, and full data access control.

On both tools, automatic CRM sync is a Business-tier feature. Fathom also offers deal-level and coaching features that Notta's pricing page does not list.

Integrations

Fathom's integrations page (checked 26 November 2026) includes:

  • CRM: Salesforce, HubSpot, Planhat
  • Sales tools: Pace, GetAccept
  • Collaboration: Slack, Superhuman Go, Glean, Pickle, Slashy
  • Automation and AI: ChatGPT, Claude, Zapier, Make, Dust, Composio
  • Project management: Asana
  • Product: Twine
  • Video conferencing: Google Meet, Zoom, Microsoft Teams

The page says Claude and ChatGPT are available on every Fathom plan. Fathom also offers an API and an MCP server. Fathom's site also says it is now part of Superhuman.

Notta's integrations page lists Google Calendar and Outlook Calendar; seven CRMs (Freshsales, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesflare, Salesforce, Zendesk Sell, Zoho CRM); Zapier; Slack; ClickUp, Google Docs, Microsoft OneNote, and Notion; and Box, Dropbox, Google Drive, and Microsoft OneDrive.

Notta lists a wider range of CRMs and cloud storage services. Fathom lists more AI assistant and sales-tool integrations.

Security and compliance

Fathom's homepage lists SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, HIPAA Compliant, and SSO/SCIM. On pricing, a standard BAA comes with Team, and SSO and SCIM provisioning come with Enterprise.

Notta's security page says it "has successfully obtained SOC 2 Type II certification through an independent audit." It also says Notta follows ISO 27001 guidelines, complies with GDPR and CCPA, and follows HIPAA guidelines. Data in transit is encrypted with TLS 1.2 and data at rest with AES-256, and Notta is hosted on AWS. SAML SSO is an Enterprise feature.

If your organization needs a signed BAA, Fathom's pricing page names one at the Team tier. Notta's pages reviewed here describe following HIPAA guidelines but don't mention a BAA, so ask Notta's sales team directly.

Which should you choose?

Based only on what each vendor publishes:

Fathom may fit better if you: - Want a free plan for full-length meetings with no minute cap - Meet mainly in English or one of Fathom's other six transcription languages - Run a sales team that needs CRM field sync, Deal View, scorecards, or coaching - Use a Mac and want bot-free capture on the free plan - Want meeting data inside ChatGPT or Claude through MCP

Notta may fit better if you: - Need transcription in languages beyond Fathom's seven (Notta lists 58) - Want to transcribe uploaded audio, video, or YouTube links as well as live meetings - Use Webex, Windows, Android, or a Chrome-based workflow - Want the lowest published annual price for a single paid seat - Need bilingual transcription or translation (a few free uses a month on every plan, more as paid add-ons)

FAQ

Is Fathom or Notta cheaper? For one paid seat, Notta Pro's published price ($8.17/month, billed annually at $97.99) is lower than Fathom Premium ($16 per user per month billed annually, $20 monthly). Notta's monthly-billing price for Pro wasn't shown in the page text we checked. Fathom's Free plan, however, has unlimited recordings, transcription, and AI summaries, while Notta Free is capped at 120 minutes a month and 3 minutes per conversation.

Do Fathom and Notta both work without a bot? Yes. Fathom offers bot-free capture through its Mac desktop app (beta, on every plan including Free; a Fathom user needs to be in the meeting). Notta offers an on-device recorder with no bot, plus a Chrome extension that captures from browser tabs.

Which supports more languages? Notta. It lists 58 languages for transcription and translation. Fathom lists seven languages for transcripts and summaries, and Ask Fathom can translate transcripts into 29 more.

Which meeting platforms does each support? Fathom's Notetaker supports Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, and its bot-free mode works with Zoom, Google Meet, and Slack Huddles. Notta lists Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex.

Do both sync meeting notes to a CRM? Yes, on Business tiers. Fathom Business includes CRM field sync that updates records after meetings. Notta Business adds CRM and Zapier integration, with Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, Zoho CRM, and others on its integrations page.
