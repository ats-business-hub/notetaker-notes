How these tools record Microsoft Teams

Official pages describe two broad methods. A meeting bot joins as a participant, which other people can see, and which may wait in a lobby or appear under a security label. A device or browser recorder captures audio, captions, or both from your computer, often without adding a guest. Some vendors offer both, and Noota also documents a path that uses Teams’ own native recording. I have not tested whether a “bot-free” mode is obvious to the other person, and several help articles still tell you to get consent.

Fireflies

Fireflies documents Teams in several official places. The supported platforms article says Fireflies can “automatically join, record, and transcribe meetings on Microsoft Teams.” A separate Teams invite article says Fireflies joins Microsoft Teams meetings as a guest, and that guest access is disabled by default on Microsoft Teams. It also says you may need to allow guests to bypass the lobby and turn off a verification check such as CAPTCHA, or Fireflies “won’t be able to join and record the meeting.”

The same article describes a Microsoft security feature that can show Fireflies under “Suspected threats” with the label “Unverified” while waiting to join. Fireflies says this is Microsoft’s way of identifying external meeting assistants, and that the organizer can click Admit, “just like any other guest participant.” Most of those settings, the article says, require Microsoft Teams Administrator permissions.

If you do not want a bot in the call, the desktop Take Notes article lists Microsoft Teams among conferencing platforms the desktop app can detect, and says this mode “captures meetings using system audio, so no Fireflies.ai Notetaker joins the meeting.” A different Fireflies article, How to integrate Microsoft Teams with Fireflies, is about posting notes to Teams channels after the meeting, not about the bot joining the call.

As of 20 November 2026, list prices on the pricing page were Free at $0, Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually or $18 monthly, Business at $19 or $29, and Enterprise at $39 annual only. Official pages still disagree on Free-plan limits; that conflict is walked through in Fireflies.ai pricing explained. See also the Fireflies review.

Noota

Noota publishes a dedicated Microsoft Teams integration page. It says you can “record, transcribe and take notes of your Teams meetings, with a bot, from the desktop app, or with Teams’ own recording.” After you connect Google Calendar or Outlook, Noota “sees your upcoming Microsoft Teams meetings, and you choose meeting by meeting which ones to record.” The desktop path, that page says, records “with no one added to the participant list.”

The same page also describes a “Noota App for Teams”: install it from the Microsoft Teams marketplace, and “the Noota side panel opens directly inside your Teams meetings.” A third path, “Teams recording,” “uses Microsoft Teams’ native recording feature: Teams records the meeting itself, then the recording file is automatically sent to Noota.” The page also says Noota can “automatically post the meeting report into the Microsoft Teams meeting chat as soon as it is ready.” The pricing comparison table still marks Free as “Bot only,” and “Bot or invisible” as a Pro, Business, and Enterprise feature.

As of 20 November 2026, list prices were in euros: Free €0, Pro €19 per user per month billed annually or €29 monthly, and Business €39 or €49. The same pricing page still advertised 40% off the first year of a new annual Pro or Business plan, until 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time, with no code, and said there is no discount on monthly plans. The discounted first-year annual figures shown were €11.40 per user per month for Pro (€136.80 for the first year instead of €228) and €23.40 for Business (€280.80 instead of €468). Microsoft’s own recap: Teams Premium and Copilot

Microsoft is not an affiliate on this site. This section uses only facts from microsoft.com pages fetched on 20 November 2026.

The Microsoft Teams Premium page listed the add-on at $10.00 per user per month, paid yearly, and said it “requires a Teams license.” The page says Teams Premium makes meetings and calls “more protected, engaging, and intelligent,” and describes “AI-powered Intelligent recap.” In the “more intelligent” section it says you can “stay focused on the discussion with real-time notes and action items, and quickly catch up on anything you missed with detailed summaries.” An FAQ on the same page says you need to be licensed for a paid version of Teams to use Teams Premium.

Microsoft Learn says Intelligent recap is automatically available to users who have a Microsoft 365 subscription that includes Teams (or a Teams license) and a Teams Premium license or a Microsoft Copilot license. It says audio recap requires a Microsoft Copilot license. After a meeting, it lists AI meeting notes, AI recommended tasks, personalized timeline markers, speaker timeline markers, meeting chapters and topics, audio recap, and video recap. It also notes that for scheduled meetings, intelligent recap is available without saving meeting transcripts, and that this experience requires a Microsoft Copilot license.

Microsoft Support says recaps are available after Teams events, including meetings, town halls, webinars, and calls, that were recorded or transcribed. In a recap you can find the recording, transcript, shared files, notes, a custom summary, the agenda, and follow-up tasks. It says Intelligent recap experiences are available as part of Teams Premium, “an add-on license,” and “are also available as part of the Microsoft Copilot license.” The same article warns that AI-generated content “might be inaccurate, incomplete, or inappropriate.”

I am not quoting a Microsoft 365 Copilot list price here, because the Copilot product page I fetched did not clearly attach the figure I saw to Teams Intelligent recap. Check Microsoft’s current licensing pages before you buy.

  • Need a no-bot path on Teams. Bluedot’s official answer is that it never joins as a bot.   • Want the limits written in one place. Fireflies’ Free pages still disagree, which I walk through in the pricing explainer. Noota’s comparison table marks bot-free recording as paid. FAQ

Which AI note takers work with Microsoft Teams?

Official pages checked on 20 November 2026 describe Microsoft Teams capture for Fireflies, Noota, Bluedot, Circleback, Laxis, and Tactiq, and also for Otter, MeetGeek, Krisp, tl;dv, Granola, Fathom, Sembly, and Read.ai. Microsoft’s own Teams Premium and Copilot licenses can add Intelligent recap inside Teams. The way each third-party tool records differs: some send a bot into the call, some record from a Chrome extension or a desktop app, and some offer both.

Can I record a Teams meeting without a bot joining the call?

Yes, several official pages describe a no-bot path. Fireflies documents a desktop Take Notes mode that captures system audio, including Microsoft Teams, without the Fireflies.ai Notetaker joining as a participant. Noota’s Teams page says you can record from the desktop app with no bot in the participant list, or use Teams’ own native recording, which is then sent to Noota. Bluedot’s FAQ says it captures audio or video from your browser or desktop rather than joining as a bot, including on Microsoft Teams. Circleback documents a bot-free desktop app for macOS and Windows that can record Teams. Laxis help says a desktop app and a Chrome extension can capture Teams in the browser. Tactiq’s official pages say no bot joins, and describe a Chrome or Edge extension or the Teams PWA; they also say the Teams desktop application is not currently supported. I have not tested whether any of those paths is obvious to the other person on the call.

Does Microsoft Teams have its own AI recap?

Yes. As of 20 November 2026, Microsoft’s Teams Premium page listed the add-on at $10.00 per user per month, paid yearly, and said it requires a Teams license. Microsoft Learn says Intelligent recap is available with a Teams Premium license or a Microsoft Copilot license, and that audio recap requires a Copilot license. Microsoft Support says recaps are available after Teams events that were recorded or transcribed, and warns that AI-generated content might be inaccurate.

Which AI note taker is best for Microsoft Teams?

Official pages do not support a single winner, and this page is not a hands-on test. If you already pay for Teams Premium or Microsoft Copilot, Microsoft’s own Intelligent recap is the built-in option. Fireflies documents guest join for Teams meetings, including lobby and “Unverified” labels that may need an admit. Noota publishes a dedicated Teams page, a Teams marketplace app, and a native Teams recording path. Bluedot is the clearest bot-free-only option among the tools with an approved affiliate link. Circleback and Laxis document both a bot and a no-bot path. Tactiq is a bot-free Chrome or Edge extension, or Teams PWA, with a $0 plan and an $8 annual Pro seat, if you can keep captions on and are not relying on the Teams desktop app.
