Krisp vs Fathom for Small Teams (2026)

Last checked: 25 November 2026 (Japan time). I read the official pricing pages of Krisp and Fathom, plus Krisp’s AI Meeting Assistant page, Fathom’s features page and Fathom’s Free vs. Premium help article, that day. I have not run both tools on the same meetings, so this compares published plans and features, not transcript accuracy or summary quality. Prices change; check both pages before you buy.

Quick pick

  • Krisp if background noise on your own calls is a real problem and you want notes without a bot by default. No ongoing free plan (7-day trial), then Core at $8 per user per month billed annually or $16 monthly, with noise cancellation included.

  • Fathom if you want a free plan you can keep. Free is $0 with unlimited recordings and transcriptions; Premium is $16 per month billed annually ($20 monthly) for one person, and Team is $15 per user per month billed annually with a 2-user minimum.

What each product is

Krisp started as noise cancellation and now sells a Meeting AI product that adds a note taker on top. Its pricing page lists transcription, audio and video recording, AI notes and action items, in-person meeting notes, AI chat and accent conversion, with noise cancellation built into every plan. Krisp says it captures meetings at the audio level on your device, so no bot has to join. It also lists a “Record with bot” option for meetings you cannot attend.

Fathom is a meeting note taker. Its pricing and features pages list recordings, transcripts, AI summaries, AI action items, Ask Fathom (chat with your meetings), clips and playlists, and, on team plans, shared folders, Trackers, Deal View and coaching scorecards. Capture is through the Fathom Notetaker bot, bot-free capture in the Mac desktop app (beta), or the iOS app for in-person meetings.

What it costs on list prices

One person. Krisp Core is $8 a month billed annually, or $16 monthly. Fathom Free is $0. Fathom Premium is $16 a month billed annually, or $20 monthly. If Fathom Free covers your meetings, it is the cheaper start. If you also want noise cancellation, Krisp Core includes it at a lower paid price than Fathom Premium.

Five seats. Krisp Core annual: 5 × $8 = $40 a month ($80 on monthly billing). Fathom Team annual: 5 × $15 = $75 a month ($95 on monthly billing). Krisp Advanced annual: 5 × $15 = $75 a month. These are list prices before tax or any discount. They are not quotes.

Krisp’s 55% Black Friday banner would change the Krisp numbers if it applies to you. I did not see the terms, so check the checkout before you count on it.

Bot or no bot

This is the clearest practical difference. Krisp’s FAQ says you do not need a bot in your meeting because it works at the audio level on your device. You select Krisp Speaker and Krisp Microphone in your calling app and turn on the note taker. Because it sits under the calling app, Krisp says it works with Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Slack Huddles and other apps.

Fathom gives you a choice. The Fathom Notetaker joins Zoom, Google Meet and Microsoft Teams calls as a bot. Bot-free capture runs in the Fathom desktop app on Mac and is labelled beta, works with Zoom, Google Meet and Slack Huddles, and needs a Fathom user in the meeting. Fathom says it is available on every plan, including Free, and that Windows support is coming. If you are on Windows and want no bot, Fathom does not offer that yet.

Bot-free does not mean consent-free. Tell people when you record, and follow your company’s and your country’s rules.

Noise cancellation and accent conversion

Krisp includes unlimited noise cancellation on every Meeting AI plan. Its FAQ says there is no separate noise-only plan: you can switch off the Meeting Assistant if you only want clean audio. Accent conversion is also included, with daily limits on Core (1 hour a day each side) and higher limits on Advanced. Fathom’s pricing and features pages do not list either feature. If your problem is a noisy room, only Krisp addresses it in these two products.

I have not measured whether Krisp’s audio processing changes transcript accuracy. Accuracy depends on microphones, overlapping speech and language, so I do not rank either tool on it.

Notes, summaries, and search

Both produce transcripts, AI summaries and action items. The differences on paper:

  • Fathom Free gives advanced summaries for your first 5 calls each month, then only the General/Enhanced template, according to its help article. AI action items, follow-up emails, custom summaries and full Ask Fathom need Premium or above.
  • Fathom team plans add shared folders, comments, Trackers that flag topics across future calls, Ask Fathom across team calls, and, on Business, Deal View, CRM field sync and coaching scorecards.
  • Krisp Core lists unlimited AI notes and action items, AI chat, multilingual transcripts, MCP integration and 10 GB of storage. Advanced adds a manager view, company deal grouping, trimming and commenting, and 60 GB.

Storage is the one limit to watch. Krisp states storage per plan (10 GB on Core). Fathom lists unlimited recordings and call storage. I did not find a stated maximum meeting length for either product, so none is given here.

Languages

If you hold meetings in Japanese, neither tool is a safe pick on the pages I read. Krisp’s FAQ lists English on-device and 15 more languages on its servers (Spanish, French, German, Russian, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Portuguese, Hindi, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian and others). Japanese is not among them. Fathom’s features page lists seven languages, with translation into 29 more through Ask Fathom. A Fathom help article (edited 4 June 2026) says “unlimited transcriptions in 38 languages,” so Fathom’s own pages do not agree. Check with Fathom before you rely on a language outside the seven. For Japanese, Circleback lists Japanese among 100+ languages.

Privacy and security

Krisp’s pricing FAQ says data is encrypted at rest (AES-256) and in transit (TLS 1.3), that it is certified for SOC 2 Type II, GDPR and HIPAA, and that meeting data is never used to train Krisp’s AI models. Its comparison table puts SOC 2 report access and BAA signing on Enterprise (BAA for teams of 100+ seats). Fathom’s pricing page lists HIPAA compliance with a standard BAA on Team and Business, and SSO, SCIM and custom data retention on Enterprise. For anything else, read Fathom’s trust center and Krisp’s security pages yourself. I have not audited either.

FAQ

Is Krisp or Fathom cheaper?

It depends on whether you want a free plan. Fathom has a Free plan at $0 with unlimited recordings and transcriptions; Krisp has a 7-day free trial and no ongoing free plan that I found. On paid list prices as of 25 November 2026, Krisp Core is $8 per user per month billed annually ($16 monthly). Fathom Premium for individuals is $16 per month billed annually ($20 monthly), and Fathom Team is $15 per user per month billed annually ($19 monthly) with a 2-user minimum.

Can Krisp and Fathom record without a bot?

Yes, with limits. Krisp says it works at the audio level on your device without a bot, and it can also send a bot. Fathom offers its Notetaker bot or bot-free capture, but Fathom describes bot-free capture as a beta feature in its Mac desktop app (macOS 14.2 or later), with Windows coming.

Does Krisp or Fathom support Japanese?

Neither lists Japanese in the language lists I read on 25 November 2026. Krisp names English plus 15 other languages for transcription. Fathom’s features page names seven languages for transcripts and summaries.

Does Krisp have noise cancellation and Fathom not?

Krisp builds noise cancellation into every Meeting AI plan and lets you turn off the Meeting Assistant if you only want clean audio. Fathom’s pricing and features pages do not list noise cancellation as a feature.
