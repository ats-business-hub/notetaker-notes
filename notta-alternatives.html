What Notta offers, from official pages

Notta sells web and mobile access on one account, and the pricing navigation describes an “Online Meeting Recorder” as “Record via a bot that joins your call,” and a “Bot-free Meeting Recorder” as “Record on your device — no bot joins.” The Notta Bot page says the bot can join Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex when the meeting URL is on your calendar. The bot-free product page describes Notta Desktop, which “records directly from your device. No bot joins. No extra name in the participant list.” It lists Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack, Webex, Discord, WhatsApp Desktop, “and 33+ more,” says the app is available for Mac and Windows, and says Desktop is included for all Notta users at no additional cost. The same page calls Desktop a beta, says macOS 13 (Ventura) or later is required for bot-free recording with system audio, and that macOS 11–12 can run the app but cannot capture system audio. It also says the page transcribes in real time across 58 languages, with bilingual support for 23 language pairs, and that you are still responsible for informing participants about recording.

A help article, What is Bot-Free Recording?, says the feature is available on all plans in the Notta Desktop app, captures microphone and system audio as separate channels, and works with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Webex, and browser-based meetings. It lists system-audio support on macOS 13.0 and later and on Windows 10 or later.

Who might look at an alternative

The reasons below come from Notta’s own pages, not from a test of the product.

  • You want to try a real client call on a $0 plan. Free is limited to 3 minutes per conversation, in addition to 120 minutes a month. That is a much tighter per-call cap than several $0 plans below, including Fathom’s unlimited recordings and Otter Basic’s 30 minutes per conversation.
  • You need more than 1,800 transcription minutes a month and do not want Business. Pro’s monthly cap is 1,800 minutes, with no carryover, according to the pricing comparison table. Business is listed as unlimited transcription.
  • You want a vendor whose official pages I can also link with an approved affiliate URL. Notta is not an approved affiliate on this site. The next section leads with tools that are.
  • You are comparing list prices, not only features. Notta Pro’s $8.17 annual figure is already low next to several alternatives. A switch is not automatically a way to pay less; it may be about the free allowance, the recording method, or the currency the vendor bills in.

FAQ

Is Notta free?

Yes, with tight limits. As of 20 November 2026, Notta’s pricing page listed a Free plan at $0, with no credit card required, 1 seat, 120 transcription minutes per month, up to 3 minutes per conversation, 50 file uploads per month, and 10 AI summaries per month. A help article on choosing a plan, updated 18 September 2026, repeats those Free caps and notes that a recording longer than 3 minutes cannot be fully transcribed on Free, even if monthly minutes remain.

How much does Notta cost?

As of 20 November 2026, the Notta pricing page showed the annual tab: Pro at $8.17 per month, billed $97.99 annually, and Business at $16.67 per month per seat, billed $199.99 annually. Enterprise is custom, starting from 51 seats. A help article on plan pricing, updated 5 September 2026, also lists monthly billing: Pro at $13.49 per month and Business at $27.99 per seat per month, both in USD. The pricing page has a Monthly / Annual toggle labeled 40% off on Annual. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

Can Notta record without a bot?

Yes. Notta’s bot-free product page and help articles describe Notta Desktop, which captures microphone and system audio on the device so no Notta Bot appears in the participant list. The product page says it works on Mac and Windows, lists Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack, Webex, Discord, and WhatsApp Desktop among supported apps, and calls Desktop a beta. It also says macOS 13 or later is required for bot-free recording with system audio, and that Desktop is available to all Notta users at no additional cost. Notta Bot, a separate official page says, can join Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex from the calendar.

What are good alternatives to Notta?

It depends on the constraint you cannot bend. Fireflies documents a calendar bot plus a desktop path without a bot, with Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually. Noota documents bot and bot-free recording, with prices in euros. Bluedot is bot-free only. Circleback documents a bot or a bot-free desktop app, including on a $0 plan. Laxis lists Basic at $0 with 300 transcription minutes per month and Premium at $9.99 a month billed yearly. Tactiq is a bot-free Chrome or Edge extension, with Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, and a Free plan of 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month. Fathom’s free plan lists unlimited recordings, which is a very different $0 shape from Notta Free’s 3-minute per-conversation cap. If you mainly transcribe uploaded audio or video, official Sonix pages list Pay As You Go at $10 per hour, a 30-minute free trial, and Japanese among 54+ languages. These tools are not all cheaper than Notta Pro’s $8.17 annual figure, and they have different free allowances.
