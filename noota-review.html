Who it is for, and who should skip it

Noota may fit a small team, consultant, recruiter, or remote team that wants meeting notes hosted in the European Union, with a published promise that conversations are not used to train AI models, including on the Free plan. The official pages describe two ways to record online meetings: a bot that joins Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, or Webex, or recording from your computer with the desktop app. The pricing table marks that second mode, which it calls “invisible,” as a Pro, Business, and Enterprise feature. The same pages also describe in-person recording from a mobile app, and phone calls on Business and Enterprise.

Skip it if you need recording without a bot on a free plan. The pricing comparison table marks Free as “Bot only.” Skip it if you need a CRM or ATS connection without paying: the pricing table and the knowledge base put work-tool sync on Pro and above. Skip it if you need Zapier, the API, or Noota’s own phone-call recorder on Pro: those rows are marked as Business and Enterprise. Skip it if you need Noota itself to hold a HIPAA certification. The security page says HDS and HIPAA are held by its cloud providers, not by Noota. Skip it if you need prices in US dollars. Every list price I saw is in euros.

How the official pages say it works

The AI note-taker page says Noota takes notes of online meetings, phone calls, and in-person conversations. A knowledge-base article, “What is Noota?”, also lists uploaded audio and video files. The 21 October 2026 product update says a new version has been live since that date, with a desktop app for Mac and Windows, a redesigned mobile app, a prep brief before meetings, live transcription and live Ask Noota during a call, then a report, a follow-up email, and tasks afterward.

The pages describe these ways to capture a conversation:

  • A meeting bot, also called the meeting assistant, that joins Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Zoom, or Webex. The pricing FAQ says the bot can join with the name you choose. A help article says the assistant appears as a participant and posts a consent message.
  • Recording from your computer with the desktop app, which the pricing table calls “invisible.” The product update and the pricing FAQ place this mode on the Pro plan and above. The desktop-app marketing page says the app itself is included in every plan, for Mac (Apple silicon and Intel) and Windows.
  • A Chrome extension, named in the pricing table and in a help article about participant notifications.
  • The mobile app, which the product update says can record in-person meetings, including offline, with the transcript made when you are back online.
  • Phone calls, on the Business and Enterprise plans, according to the knowledge base and the pricing table. The integrations page also names Aircall, Ringover, and 3CX.

Official pages do not agree on whether other participants are notified when you record without a bot. The pricing FAQ, in the same answer that describes the bot and the desktop app, says “Participants are notified that the meeting is recorded.” A help article last dated 18 November 2025 says the Chrome extension and the desktop app do not automatically notify other participants, and that you should inform them yourself. The same article says the meeting assistant is always visible and always notifies participants. The product update also tells you to ask for consent before recording. This page has not tested what participants actually see.

Where official pages disagree about the Free plan

The Free plan is real on the pricing page: €0, no card, one month of storage, and transcription and summaries. After that, the limits are not described the same way on every official page.

  • Recording minutes. The pricing footnote says unlimited recording on Free requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants. Otherwise, live recordings and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. A help article, “When Are The Credits Renewed?”, says bot recordings are unlimited on Free, and that the 300-minute cap applies to live recordings, desktop-app recordings, and Chrome extension recordings, renewed each month on the anniversary of the account, not on the first of the calendar month. A related plans article shows the same 300-minute split. Those help articles do not repeat the calendar and auto-share conditions from the pricing footnote.
  • AI credits. The pricing comparison table lists 10 monthly AI credits per user on Free and 100 on Pro, and it marks follow-up email drafts and the email agent as “Uses credits” on those plans. The same credits help article says, of current plans, that only Free has a monthly minute limit and that paid plans have no minutes or credits to renew. Those two statements do not line up for Pro.
  • Voice dictation, meeting prep, and live Ask Noota. The pricing table marks voice dictation, the prepare-meeting assistant, web search and deep research, and live transcription and live Ask Noota as included on Free. The 21 October 2026 product-update plan table lists the brief, Ask Noota, live transcription, live Ask Noota, AI rewrite of reports, and voice dictation under Pro. The help-center plans article also marks voice dictation and “meeting prep, web research, document generation” as starting on Pro, not on Free. Use the pricing page and that help article together, and expect a gap.
  • Clips, templates, mailbox, and team settings. The pricing table marks Magic Clip and Scorecard as included on Free. The help-center plans article marks those as starting on Pro. The pricing table marks Gmail and Outlook sync as included on every plan; the help article marks mailbox sync as starting on Pro. The pricing table marks groups and team settings as starting on Business; the help article marks them as included on every plan. Custom templates line up: both the pricing table and the help article put them on Business and Enterprise.
  • How long Free meetings are kept. The pricing page and the plans help article say 1 month of storage or meeting history, and that meetings older than the plan’s history limit are archived. The privacy policy, last updated October 2026, says meeting content on the Free plan is archived after 30 days. Those can sit together. For paid plans, the same policy says meeting content is kept as long as the account is active, or for a period set by the admin (1 day to 3 years on Enterprise). That “as long as the account is active” line is broader than the pricing table’s 6-month storage on Pro.

Integrations and plan gates

The integrations page says you connect a tool in one click, choose whether the report, transcript, tasks, or recording is sent, and can review before a CRM or ATS record is saved. Official pages do not agree on how many tools that is. The page title says “46+” tools. A directory filter on the same page is labeled “All 53.”

The pricing FAQ names 16 ATS connections: Bullhorn, Teamtailor, Workable, Recruitee, SmartRecruiters, Flatchr, Tool4Staffing, AD-MEN, Boond, MySolution, Hiresweet, Nicoka, Zoho Recruit, Zoho People, Loxo, and Beetween, plus Zapier and the API for others. The integrations directory labels an ATS group of 20 and also names Greenhouse, BambooHR, ICMS, and Intuition Software, among others. I have not verified each connection.

Plan gates on the pricing comparison table, as of 20 November 2026, include:

  • Work tools (CRM and ATS): Pro and above. The knowledge base names Salesforce, HubSpot, and Pipedrive on Pro and above.
  • Download to PDF, Word, or TXT, summary translation, and rewrite summaries with AI: Pro and above.
  • Automations, auto-complete fields, API access, and the Teams app and Zapier: Business and above. The knowledge base also puts Zapier on Business and Enterprise.
  • VOIP, click-to-call, and bringing your own number: Business and above.
  • MCP integration: included on every plan, including Free, on that table.

Security and privacy

The security page says Noota is ISO 27001 certified (it names a July 2026 audit) and SOC 2 Type II certified, with reports in a trust center at trust.noota.io. The same page’s certifications FAQ names ISO/IEC 27001 and GDPR compliance, and says DORA alignment and EU AI Act work are in progress. It also says HDS and HIPAA are held by cloud providers, not by Noota itself. The pricing page repeats ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II, plus encryption at rest and in transit and EU data residency.

The privacy policy, last updated October 2026, says Noota is operated by NOOTA SAS in Toulouse, France. For meeting content, Noota says it acts as a processor under a data processing agreement, and that the customer is responsible for a legal basis to record and for informing participants. It says recordings, transcripts, and reports are never used to train Noota’s or a third party’s models, on any plan including Free. Deleted meetings are described as erased from production and backups in under 24 hours. The security page uses the same 24-hour window. The feature-page FAQ says a deleted recording is permanently removed within 24 hours, and that you can keep only audio without video, or only the written transcript.

Hosting is described as two environments. The standard environment runs on Google Cloud in Belgium and the Netherlands, with frontier models reached through Vertex AI in the EU. The sovereign environment is described as French providers: Scaleway in Paris, transcription by Gladia, and Mistral models, with Bleu trusted cloud “by the end of 2026.” The pricing table marks sovereign infrastructure as optional on Enterprise. The security page says the choice is made per workspace and can be changed later. The privacy policy says to contact sales to activate the sovereign environment.

The privacy policy is more precise than the pricing page’s “no transfer outside the EU” line for the standard environment. It lists an exception: in the standard environment, the optional feature that records calls from your own phone number may route those calls through Twilio, which may process call data in the United States. It also names payment and support providers that may process limited account or contact data outside the EU. It says meeting content is stored in the EU, and that the sovereign environment does not transfer data outside the EU.

FAQ

Does Noota put a bot in the meeting?

Yes, it can. The pricing comparison table marks the Free plan as “Bot only.” Pro, Business, and Enterprise are marked “Bot or invisible.” The pricing FAQ says Noota can join as a bot with the name you choose, or record quietly from your computer with the desktop app on the Pro plan and above. This page has not tested that.

Is Noota free?

The pricing page lists a Free plan at €0 per user per month, with no card needed, one month of storage, and web, mobile, and desktop apps. Official pages do not describe the Free recording limit the same way. The pricing footnote says unlimited recording on Free requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled, and that the meeting summary is automatically shared with all participants; otherwise live and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month. A help article says bot recordings are unlimited on Free, and that the 300-minute cap applies to live, desktop app, and Chrome extension recordings.

How much does Noota cost?

On 20 November 2026 the pricing page listed all prices in euros. Pro was €19 per user per month billed annually, or €29 billed monthly. Business was €39 billed annually, or €49 billed monthly. Enterprise is custom, for 50 or more seats. A Black Friday offer of 40% off the first year of a new annual Pro or Business plan was on that page, until Monday, 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time, with no code and no discount on monthly plans. Check the official pricing page before you buy.

Does Noota support Japanese?

A Supported Languages help article on support.noota.io, dated 15 April 2024, lists Japanese among Asian languages. The current AI note-taker feature page names English, French, Spanish, German, Italian, Dutch, and more, and its FAQ also names Portuguese. A separate knowledge-base article, “What is Noota?”, says 80+ supported languages. This page has not tested accuracy, and it has not confirmed that the 2024 list is still complete.

Where does Noota store meetings?

The security page and the privacy policy, last updated October 2026, say meeting data is hosted in the European Union. The standard environment runs on Google Cloud in Belgium and the Netherlands. A sovereign option is hosted in France. The privacy policy also describes one exception in the standard environment: optional recording of calls from your own phone number may route through Twilio, which may process call data in the United States.

Does a Free account get bot-free recording?

Not according to the pricing comparison table, which marks Free as “Bot only,” and not according to the pricing FAQ and the 21 October 2026 product update, which place recording without a bot on the Pro plan and above. The desktop app itself is described as included in every plan, but those same pages say the Free plan uses the app to record with the Noota bot.
