How these tools record Google Meet

Official pages describe two broad methods. A meeting bot joins as a participant, which other people can see, and which may wait in a lobby. A device or browser recorder captures audio, captions, or both from your computer, often without adding a guest. Some vendors offer both. I have not tested whether a “bot-free” or “invisible” mode is obvious to the other person, and several help articles still tell you to get consent.

Fireflies.ai

Fireflies is the most detailed official write-up of Google Meet among these six. The pricing page names GMeet on the Free card and, in the comparison table, “Record Zoom, Gmeet, MS teams, 10+.” Help says the bot appears as Fireflies.ai Notetaker, may need to be admitted, and posts in the meeting chat. A joins article also explains Google Meet’s “potential risk” label: Fireflies says this is expected behavior from a Google security update, and that you may need to admit the notetaker from the “With potential risks” section. It will wait up to 10 minutes in a Google Meet lobby, versus 15 minutes on other platforms.

If you do not want a bot in the call, two official paths are documented. The Chrome extension “only works with Google Meet in Chrome.” It records audio, not video; for video, the same article says to invite the notetaker bot. Auto-capture can start when a Meet call is detected. The desktop Take Notes article lists Google Meet among platforms the desktop app can detect, and says this mode captures system audio and does not save a separate audio or video file.

As of 20 November 2026, list prices were Free at $0, Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually or $18 monthly, Business at $19 or $29, and Enterprise at $39 annual only. Official pages disagree on Free limits. Details are in Fireflies.ai pricing explained and the Fireflies.ai review.

Noota

Noota publishes a dedicated Google Meet integration page. It says you can “record, transcribe and take notes of your Google Meet calls, with a bot that joins the call or without one, from the desktop app.” After you connect Google Calendar or Outlook, Noota “sees your upcoming Google Meet meetings, and you choose meeting by meeting which ones to record.” The desktop path, that page says, adds “no one” to the participant list. The pricing comparison table still marks Free as “Bot only,” and “Bot or invisible” as a Pro, Business, and Enterprise feature.

As of 20 November 2026, list prices were in euros: Free €0, Pro €19 per user per month billed annually or €29 monthly, and Business €39 or €49. The same pricing page still advertised 40% off the first year of a new annual Pro or Business plan, until 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time, with no code. See the Noota review, Noota vs Fireflies, and the Black Friday deals page. Offers change.

Bluedot

Bluedot is the clearest bot-free-only option in this group. The homepage says it can “capture meetings on Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, and in-person,” and its FAQ answers “Does Bluedot work with Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams?” with yes: it “captures audio/video from your browser or desktop rather than joining as a bot,” and is “compatible with any platform, including Zoom, Google Meet, MS Teams, Webex, and even Slack huddles or phone calls.” As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page listed Free at $0 with 5 lifetime meetings; Basic at $14 per user per month billed annually or $18 monthly; Pro at $20 or $25; and Business at $32 or $39. See the Bluedot review.

Circleback

Circleback’s homepage FAQ asks which platforms it works with and answers: “in-person meetings and all meeting platforms, including: Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, WebEx, Slack huddles.” The pricing page describes “a bot or the bot-free desktop app for online meetings.” As of 20 November 2026, the yearly cards showed Free at $0, Pro at $15 per user per month, Business at $25, and Enterprise at $35. Price data in that same page lists Pro at $18 billed monthly or $180 a year, and Business at $29 monthly or $300 a year. The FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings, with 30-day history on Free. See the Circleback review.

Laxis

Laxis names Google Meet in homepage structured data (“Works with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex”) and in AI Meeting Assistant help. That help page describes two relevant paths: “Send the Laxis meeting bot — the bot joins Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams calls as a participant,” and “Use the Chrome extension — the Chrome Extension captures captions in Google Meet, and in Zoom and Teams in the browser, without a bot.” As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page showed Basic at $0 (300 minutes a month, 45 minutes per recording), Premium at $9.99 per month billed yearly, and Business at $19.99 per month billed yearly. Billing help lists monthly prices of $15.99 and $29.99 for Premium and Business. See the Laxis review.

Tactiq

Tactiq describes itself as an AI note taker for Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams. The homepage says you get live transcriptions without an AI bot joining the meeting, and that Tactiq supports over 60 languages. A help article on installing the extension says Tactiq works with Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Zoom meetings that run in Chrome or Edge, and that Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions to create the transcript. Keep captions turned on, that article says. The pricing comparison table also lists Google Companion Mode on every plan, including Free.

As of 20 November 2026, the pricing page listed Free at $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month; Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited transcripts and 10 AI credits; Team at $16.67 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited AI credits; and Business at $29.17 per user per month billed annually. A help article dated 30 June 2026 also lists monthly prices of $12, $20, and $40 for Pro, Team, and Business. See the Tactiq review, Tactiq vs Otter.ai, and Tactiq alternatives.

FAQ

Which AI note takers work with Google Meet?

Official pages checked on 20 November 2026 describe Google Meet capture for Fireflies, Noota, Bluedot, Circleback, Laxis, and Tactiq, and also for Otter, MeetGeek, Krisp, tl;dv, Granola, Fathom, and Sembly. The way each tool records differs: some send a bot into the call, some record from a Chrome extension or a desktop app, and some offer both.

Can I record Google Meet without a bot joining the call?

Yes, several official pages describe a no-bot path. Fireflies documents a Chrome extension that records Google Meet audio only, and a desktop Take Notes mode that captures system audio, including Google Meet, without the bot joining as a participant. Noota’s Google Meet page says you can record from the desktop app with no bot in the participant list; the pricing table marks that mode as Pro and above. Bluedot’s FAQ says it captures audio or video from your browser or desktop rather than joining as a bot, including on Google Meet. Circleback documents a bot-free desktop app and lists Google Meet among supported platforms. Laxis help says the Chrome extension captures captions in Google Meet without a bot. Tactiq’s homepage says no bot joins, and install help says the Chrome or Edge extension transcribes Google Meet from the meeting’s captions.

Is there a free AI note taker for Google Meet?

Several vendors publish a $0 or €0 plan and also document Google Meet support. Fireflies Free is $0 and called free forever, but official pages disagree on the limits. Noota Free is €0, with Free marked bot-only on the pricing table. Bluedot Free is 5 meetings for the life of the account. Circleback’s pricing FAQ says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings, with 30-day history on Free. Laxis Basic is $0 with 300 minutes a month and a 45-minute cap per recording. Tactiq Free is $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts a month, and 5 AI credits a month. Check each vendor’s current pricing page before you rely on a free allowance.

Which AI note taker is best for Google Meet?

Official pages do not support a single winner, and this page is not a hands-on test. Fireflies has the most detailed Google Meet help, including a Meet-only Chrome extension and a waiting-room note for Google’s potential-risk label. Noota publishes a dedicated Google Meet integration page and marks bot-free desktop recording as a paid-plan feature. Bluedot is the clearest bot-free-only option among the tools with an approved affiliate link. Circleback and Laxis document both a bot and a no-bot path, and both list Google Meet among supported platforms. Tactiq is a bot-free Chrome or Edge extension, with a $0 plan and an $8 annual Pro seat, if you can keep captions on.
