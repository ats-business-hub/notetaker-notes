Quick verdict by use case

There is no single winner. These are readings of the official pages, not test results.

  • No bot in the participant list, from the default setup. Tactiq’s homepage says no bot joins the call. Noota can join as a bot, or record quietly from the desktop app, but quiet recording is placed on Pro and above, not on Free.
  • A phone app, desktop apps on Mac and Windows, or stored meeting media. Noota’s Free card lists web, mobile, and desktop apps, and the product records meetings, with storage that rises from one month on Free to six months on Pro and unlimited on Business. Tactiq’s download page lists a Chrome extension and a Mac app, and the homepage FAQ says Tactiq does not record or store meeting audio.
  • CRM sync, phone-call recording, or EU hosting as a published default. Noota’s Pro card lists CRM and work-tool sync. Phone-call recording is on the Business card. The pricing page says data is hosted in Europe, with ISO 27001 and SOC 2 Type II, and that data is not used to train AI models. Tactiq’s homepage mentions sharing to Slack, Notion, Linear, HubSpot, and more, and the pricing table marks workflow integrations as included, but I did not treat that as a documented CRM two-way sync the way Noota’s Pro card describes it.
  • A $0 plan with no card, or a low USD annual Pro seat, if you can work with a monthly AI-credit cap and captions. Tactiq Free is $0 with 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month. Tactiq Pro is $8 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited transcripts and 10 AI credits a month. Noota Free is €0, with the 300-minute footnote quoted below. Noota Pro is €19 per user per month billed annually, or €11.40 in the first year on the advertised Black Friday offer, with six months of storage and bot-free desktop recording.
  • A notetaker that can join a meeting you do not attend. Noota can join as a bot. Tactiq’s official pages describe capture from your browser or, for Zoom desktop, from a Mac app, with you in the meeting.

Bots, captions, and recording without a bot

Tactiq’s homepage says you get live transcriptions without an AI bot joining the meeting. The install help article says Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions, and to keep captions turned on. A Microsoft Teams help article says the Teams desktop application is not currently supported, and tells you to continue in the browser or the Teams PWA.

Noota’s pricing FAQ “Do other meeting participants see a bot during the call?” says: “Only if you want. Noota can join as a bot with the name you choose, or record quietly from your computer with the desktop app on the Pro plan and above. Participants are notified that the meeting is recorded.” Quiet desktop recording is therefore described as a Pro, Business, and Enterprise feature, not a Free-plan feature.

I have not tested whether Tactiq’s caption-based transcript, or Noota’s bot or quiet desktop recording, is obvious to the other person on the call. Both vendors still describe notice in some form. This page is not legal advice.

Questions, answered from the official pages

Is Noota or Tactiq free?

Both publish a $0 or €0 plan. Tactiq Free is $0 with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month. Noota Free is €0 with no card, one month of storage, and a footnote that unlimited recording requires a connected calendar with Join all meetings enabled and the summary shared with all participants; otherwise live and standard recordings are limited to 300 minutes per month.

How much do Noota and Tactiq cost?

They are not priced in the same currency, and this page does not convert them. As of 20 November 2026, Noota Pro was €19 per user per month billed annually or €29 monthly, and Business was €39 or €49, plus the first-year 40% annual offer advertised until 30 November 2026, 11:59 pm Paris time. Tactiq Pro was $8 per user per month billed annually, or $12 monthly in a help article, Team was $16.67 or $20, and Business was $29.17 on the pricing page or $29.16 in that help article, and $40 monthly. Check both live pages before you buy.

Can Noota or Tactiq record without a bot?

Tactiq’s official pages say no bot joins, and that transcription runs from a Chrome or Edge extension, using the meeting platform’s captions. Noota can join as a bot, or record quietly from the desktop app on Pro and above. Quiet desktop recording is not described as a Free-plan feature.
