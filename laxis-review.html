Who it is for, and who should skip it

Laxis may fit a small sales team that wants meeting notes and a voice keyboard on one subscription, and that can accept a choice between recording on the device and sending a bot. The help center says the desktop app records Zoom, Teams, Google Meet, Webex, speakerphone, and in-person audio without a bot. A bot can still join Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams. Premium is where the billing page puts AI chat and the 40+ templates, including sales frameworks such as MEDDIC and BANT. HubSpot, Salesforce, and Wealthbox are on Business.

Skip it if 300 minutes a month and a 45-minute cap are too small and you do not want to pay. Those are the Basic limits. Meeting transcription, Voice Keyboard dictation, and live translation share that pool. Skip it if you need a CRM connection on Premium. The integrations article says a CRM requires Business, even though the homepage structured data lists HubSpot and Salesforce on Premium. Skip it if you need a published Japanese transcription language. The pages I read say 60 languages and do not name Japanese in that list. Skip it if you need data residency before Enterprise. That feature is listed only there.

How the official pages say it works

The homepage calls Laxis a bot-free note taker in 100+ languages, plus a voice keyboard. The help center is more specific. It lists three ways to capture a meeting:

  • On your device. The desktop app captures the microphone and computer audio, and the desktop guide says no bot joins. The mobile app records in-person conversations. The web app records from the browser microphone.
  • A meeting bot. It joins Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams as a participant. You paste a link, or the calendar sends it. Auto-join can be all calendar meetings, only meetings you host, or only meetings you select. Google Calendar and Outlook connect one at a time.
  • The Chrome extension. It captures captions in Google Meet, and in Zoom and Teams in the browser, without a bot. The web guide says those meetings are text only. Bot meetings show a video player. Other recordings show audio controls.

You can also upload audio. The desktop guide lists m4a, mp3, wav, and flac, up to 200 MB. The homepage also describes Apple Watch recording, AI earbuds, and a 360-degree meeting camera. I did not use the hardware product pages for prices.

The privacy policy, last updated 10 November 2026, says some recording options show other participants that a notetaker is present and some do not. It says you are responsible for notifying people and getting any consent the law requires. This page is not legal advice.

One subscription covers the Meeting Assistant and the Voice Keyboard, and they draw from the same monthly minutes. The web app also has a separate Sales Agent product for prospect research and an AI SDR. That product has its own subscription. I cover those prices below so they are not mixed into the meeting plan.

Plans and pricing

These figures are from the Laxis billing help page on 19 November 2026. It prints both monthly and yearly USD prices for purchases on the web and the desktop app. The pricing page had the Yearly control selected and showed Premium at $9.99 per month and Business at $19.99 per month. Those match the yearly column. The Monthly dollar amounts were not in the pricing page HTML I fetched. The billing page is the source for the monthly column. App Store and Google Play charges are in local currency and can differ. Check both pages before you buy.

  • Basic: $0. 300 transcription minutes a month. Longest recording 45 minutes. Conversation history 30 days. Record online and in person, summaries, search, export, share, and folders. Voice Keyboard voice-to-text, with filler-word and repetition cleanup. The billing page says transcription in 60 languages. The pricing comparison table says 100+ languages on every plan, including Basic.
  • Premium: $15.99 a month, or $9.99 a month billed yearly ($119.88 a year). 2,000 minutes a month. Longest recording 2 hours. Unlimited conversation storage. AI chat, 40+ templates, live translation, an advanced AI model, keyword tracking and conversation insight on the pricing card, MCP, and up to 10 screenshots or photos per meeting. Voice Keyboard adds translate, agent mode, and writing that can use your meetings.
  • Business: $29.99 a month, or $19.99 a month billed yearly ($239.88 a year). Unlimited transcription and unlimited dictation. Longest recording 4 hours. Personalized meeting-assistant name, video recording replay, CRM automation, 7,000+ Zapier apps, team admin, up to 20 screenshots or photos per meeting, and priority support.
  • Enterprise: custom. The billing page says custom transcription volume, SSO/SAML, full API, custom deployment and data residency, MDM for the Voice Keyboard, a dedicated account manager, and onboarding. The pricing comparison table marks Enterprise transcription Unlimited, while the Enterprise card on that same page says custom transcription volume. I am not picking one.

The billing page says yearly billing saves up to 38 percent, and it breaks that into 37.5 percent on Premium and 33 percent on Business. New accounts get 14 days of Premium with no credit card, then move to Basic and keep their notes. A referral link gives 30 days of Premium. There is no self-serve Business trial. Contact sales for a team trial.

When monthly minutes run out, recording, dictation, and live translation pause until the next billing month or an upgrade. Notes already recorded stay. On Basic, a meeting can be locked if it was saved after the free minutes ran out, or if it is older than 30 days. Locked notes stay hidden until you upgrade. Archived meetings are not deleted. Upgrading unlocks them.

You can change plans on the web or desktop immediately, with the price difference prorated. Canceling keeps the paid features until the end of the current period. App Store and Google Play follow Apple’s and Google’s rules, and a downgrade there usually waits until the next renewal. You can change or cancel a subscription only in the store where you bought it.

Business seats are bought by the billing owner. Joining a workspace is free, and a workspace can mix Basic, Premium, and Business members. A Business seat is what upgrades a member. Five people who each have a Business seat, at the yearly web price, are 5 times $19.99 a month before tax. People without that seat stay on their own plan.

Nonprofit and education pricing exists. The pricing FAQ says to contact the partnership team. The billing page says to email support@laxis.tech. Neither page publishes a percent.

Prospect Research, under Sales Agent, is a separate subscription. The billing page lists Basic at $0 with 100 contact unlocks, Premium at $59 a month or $49 a month billed yearly with 5,000 unlocks and CRM integration, and Business at $119 a month or $99 a month billed yearly with 10,000 unlocks. AI SDR plans are arranged with sales. Those prices are not the meeting-assistant prices above.

Features, as the official pages describe them

  • Live transcript with speaker labels, notes written during the meeting, a summary, and a to-do list. Premium adds chat across meetings and 40+ templates, including follow-up email and sales frameworks.
  • Apps named in help: Mac, Windows, web, iPhone, Android, and Chrome. Meetings sync across them. Apple Watch is on the homepage.
  • Calendars: Google or Outlook, one at a time, with prep notes and optional bot auto-join.
  • Integrations: HubSpot, Salesforce, and Wealthbox on Business, one CRM at a time. Zapier on Business. MCP for Claude, ChatGPT, and other tools on Premium and Business, read-only to your own meetings. Zoom and Webex have their own sign-in apps.
  • Dictionary of up to 200 terms. Interface languages on the what’s-new page include English, Chinese, French, Spanish, Japanese, German, Portuguese, Korean, and Italian.
  • Voice recognition is off by default. If you turn it on, Laxis stores a voiceprint. The privacy policy says voice characteristics may count as biometric information in some places, and that you are responsible for consent. Turning it off deletes enrolled voices.

Pros and cons from those pages

Pros

  • Basic is an ongoing free plan with 300 minutes a month, not a handful of lifetime meetings. The 14-day Premium trial does not ask for a card, and notes remain after it ends.
  • Help documents a no-bot desktop path and a bot path, and says which platforms the bot joins.
  • The billing page publishes monthly and yearly USD prices, the shared minute pool, and what happens when minutes run out.
  • Business names the CRMs: HubSpot, Salesforce, and Wealthbox. Premium names the sales templates and AI chat.
  • The privacy policy says Laxis does not sell personal information. SOC 2 Type I is stated on the homepage, the privacy policy, and the security help page. They spell it Type I and Type 1. Encryption in transit and at rest is stated. Self-serve deletion is described as permanent.

Cons

  • The homepage says bot-free. Help and the privacy policy also describe a bot that joins as a participant.
  • The homepage and the pricing table say 100+ languages. The meeting-assistant help page and the billing page say 60. The 60-language list is not published on the pages I read.
  • The homepage says meeting and dictation data are never used to train models. The privacy policy says not without consent, and that training would be opt-in.
  • CRM is Business in the help center and Premium in the homepage structured data.
  • Enterprise transcription is Unlimited in the pricing comparison table and custom on the Enterprise card and the billing page.
  • The web guide puts a personalized bot name under paid-plan settings. The billing page puts that name, and video replay, on Business.
  • The homepage says data is stored on Google Cloud. The privacy policy does not name the provider or a region. Data residency is an Enterprise line.
  • Basic can archive or lock meetings. Dictation and live translation spend the same minutes as meetings. A Business team trial is not self-serve.

Privacy and security, including the conflicts

The privacy policy is last updated 10 November 2026. Cloud apps capture audio on the device and send it to Laxis servers for transcription, summary, and storage. The policy also describes on-device apps that transcribe on the device and do not send that content to Laxis. It does not name which app is the on-device one. The help pages I read for desktop, web, and mobile do not say those apps keep the transcript only on the device.

Training is the clearest conflict. The homepage says: “We never use your meeting or dictation data to train AI models.” Section 3 of the privacy policy says Laxis does not use meeting recordings, transcripts, notes, or dictation content to train models without your consent. It says training is never the default and never a condition of using the service, and that any program would be opt-in. I am leaving both sentences in place.

Location is incomplete. The homepage says conversations are encrypted and stored on Google Cloud. The privacy policy describes servers and subprocessors, and says a subprocessor list is available by emailing support@laxis.tech. It does not name Google Cloud or a country. The billing page lists data residency on Enterprise.

Deletion is described two ways that can sit together. The privacy policy says that when you delete content or the account, Laxis deletes that content from its systems immediately, including from backups, except where the law requires retention. The security help page says an email deletion request to support@laxis.tech gets a response within 30 days, and that deleting the account in the app is permanent. Basic’s 30-day rule is archive, not delete: the billing page says older meetings stay in the list and return when you upgrade.

Meetings are private until you share them. A setting can auto-share a summary with participants. The policy says to turn that off if you do not want it. Laxis says it is the controller of account and usage data and a processor of meeting and dictation content where GDPR applies. A data processing addendum is available for business customers. I did not find a HIPAA claim on the pages I read.

FAQ

Does Laxis put a bot in the meeting?

The homepage describes Laxis as bot-free. The help center describes three ways to record. The desktop app captures your microphone and computer audio and says no bot joins. A meeting bot can join Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams as a participant, from a link or from your calendar. The Chrome extension captures captions in the browser without a bot. The privacy policy says some options show other participants that a notetaker is present and some do not, and that you are responsible for notifying people. This page has not tested that.

Is Laxis free?

The billing page lists Basic at $0 with 300 transcription minutes a month, recordings up to 45 minutes, and 30 days of conversation history. New accounts get a 14-day Premium trial with no credit card. When the trial ends, the account moves to Basic and keeps its notes. Meetings older than 30 days are archived, not deleted, until you upgrade. A referral signup gets 30 days of Premium instead of 14.

How much does Laxis cost?

The billing help page, checked 19 November 2026, lists Premium at $15.99 a month, or $9.99 a month billed yearly ($119.88 a year), and Business at $29.99 a month, or $19.99 a month billed yearly ($239.88 a year). Enterprise is custom. The pricing page had Yearly selected and showed $9.99 and $19.99 per month. Its Monthly prices were not in the page HTML. The billing page says yearly billing saves up to 38 percent. App Store and Google Play prices can differ. Check the current pages before you buy.

Does Laxis support Japanese?

The help center says live transcription is available in 60 languages and does not publish that list on the pages I read, so I cannot confirm Japanese transcription from them. Search inside meetings works in English, Chinese, Japanese, and Korean. The apps' interface languages include Japanese. The homepage and the pricing comparison table say 100+ languages. The billing page says transcription in 60 languages. This page has not tested accuracy.

Does Laxis use meetings to train AI models?

The homepage says Laxis never uses meeting or dictation data to train AI models. The privacy policy, last updated 10 November 2026, says Laxis does not use that content to train models without your consent, that training is never the default, and that any training program would be opt-in. Those two statements are not the same.

Which plan syncs a CRM, and where are meetings stored?

The help center says connecting HubSpot, Salesforce, or Wealthbox requires the Business plan, one CRM at a time. The homepage structured data says Premium includes HubSpot and Salesforce sync. The homepage says data is encrypted and stored on Google Cloud. The privacy policy says cloud apps send audio to Laxis servers, and it does not name a cloud provider or a region. Data residency is listed as an Enterprise feature.
