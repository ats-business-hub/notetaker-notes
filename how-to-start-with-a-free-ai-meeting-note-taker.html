How to start with a free AI meeting note taker (2026)

A free AI meeting note taker records or transcribes your calls and turns them into notes you can search later. It is a low-risk way to see whether automated notes fit how you work, but every free plan caps something.

This guide is a starting path, not another catalog of every $0 plan on the site. For the wider list, including tools not covered here, see the free plans and trials hub. Plan-by-plan limits for Fireflies, tl;dv, and Tactiq are in the individual guides linked below. I read each vendor’s official pricing page and the official help articles cited here on 24 November 2026 (Japan time). I have not used these tools on client calls, so this is not a report of how they behave in a meeting. Pricing pages change often, so check the official page before you sign up or buy.

The six plans below are Fireflies, tl;dv, Otter, MeetGeek, Tactiq, and Bluedot. All plan names, limits, and prices come from those official pages and are stated as of 24 November 2026.

What free plans usually limit

The six free plans cap different things, so it helps to know what to look for before you create an account:

  • Storage or total minutes. Fireflies caps stored meeting minutes (fireflies.ai/pricing).
  • Hours, transcripts, or credits per month. MeetGeek gives 3 hours of transcription a month (meetgeek.ai/pricing). Tactiq gives 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month (tactiq.io/buy).
  • Lifetime caps. Bluedot’s free plan allows 5 meetings in total (bluedothq.com/pricing). tl;dv help says its free-plan limits apply for the lifetime of the account and do not renew monthly (tl;dv help).
  • AI-note caps. tl;dv’s AI notes cover whole meetings only for your first 10 meetings.
  • Retention. tl;dv and MeetGeek both delete free-plan material after set periods.
  • Meeting length. Caps range from 30 minutes per conversation (Otter Basic) to 3 hours (tl;dv).
  • Uploads and imports. Otter Basic includes 3 lifetime audio/video file imports (otter.ai/pricing).

The six free plans, one by one

Fireflies

Fireflies lists its Free plan at $0, “Free forever” (fireflies.ai/pricing). The pricing page shows “Unlimited transcription*”, “Limited AI summaries”, 400 mins of storage, and 20 AI credits. The comparison table caps recording at 2 hours per meeting.

A few points need care. Storage is a running total, not a monthly allowance: Fireflies’ storage article says the figure is the total minutes of meetings you can store, not per month, and the free-tier help article says older meetings must be deleted to free up space once you hit the limit (Fireflies help). Who the 400 minutes belong to is unclear: the pricing page says “400 mins of storage/team”, while the help articles say per seat or per user. On transcriptions, the pricing page says “Unlimited transcription*”, and the free-tier help article says you get 3 free transcriptions by default, with Auto Join unlocking unlimited transcriptions for supported meetings. Check the official page before you rely on either description. AI credits are 20 once, at signup (AI credits article), shared within a team; when you upgrade, your new plan’s credits replace the old ones rather than adding to them.

More detail is in the Fireflies free plan guide, and paid tiers are covered in Fireflies pricing.

tl;dv

tl;dv’s help center says the free plan includes unlimited recordings, transcripts, and manually timestamped notes (tl;dv help). It also includes 10 AI prompts, 10 AI Reports, 20 integration credits, and 4 clips. Clips can’t be downloaded. Recordings are stored for 3 months, and you can’t record meetings that overlap. Every plan has a 3-hour maximum per meeting.

Caution: the official help articles disagree on uploads. One says the free plan includes 5 uploaded files, and a newer uploads article says up to 3. Check the official page. See the tl;dv free plan guide for more.

Otter

Otter’s free tier is called Basic (otter.ai/pricing). It includes 300 monthly transcription minutes and 3 lifetime audio/video file imports. The comparison table allows up to 30 minutes per conversation and up to 5 workspace members. It also lists a monthly limit for imported files of 300 minutes per user. That appears as a separate line from the 300 monthly transcription minutes, so check the official page for how the two relate. The Otter vs Fireflies page compares the two side by side, and Otter pricing covers paid tiers. This site does not currently use an Otter affiliate link; the links above go to official pages.

MeetGeek

MeetGeek’s Basic plan is “Free forever” (meetgeek.ai/pricing). It includes 3 hours of transcription a month and recordings of up to 2 hours each. Transcripts are stored for 3 months and audio for 1 month. MeetGeek’s FAQ says Basic stops recording and analyzing meetings once you’ve used the 3 hours. This site does not currently use a MeetGeek affiliate link; the pricing link above goes to the official page.

Tactiq

Tactiq’s Free plan is $0, “Free forever”, with no credit card required, and the pricing page labels it “For 1 user” (tactiq.io/buy). It includes 10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month. One AI credit covers a single AI action, such as a meeting summary. According to the FAQ, AI features pause when you reach a limit and come back when the limit resets at the start of the next month. Existing transcripts stay accessible. Read the Tactiq free plan guide for more.

How to pick and start: 5 steps

  1. Create one account on the free plan. Tactiq says its Free plan needs no credit card. For other tools, check the official page.
  2. Connect your calendar or meeting tool. Make sure the tool supports the platform you actually meet on. If you use Google Meet, start with the best free AI note taker for Google Meet guide. If you’d rather not have a bot join your calls, see bot-free meeting recorders.
  3. Match the free-plan caps to your meeting load. Check whether the cap resets monthly, and compare the per-meeting length cap with your longest regular meeting.
  4. Plan for export and retention early. tl;dv deletes free recordings after 3 months. It notifies you 4 weeks before deletion, and you can download them during that window. MeetGeek keeps audio for 1 month. Fireflies requires you to delete older meetings once storage fills up.
  5. Decide what would make you upgrade. If you regularly hit a cap, such as Otter’s 30-minute conversation limit, MeetGeek’s 3 hours a month, or Bluedot’s 5 meetings, compare paid plans in the next section.

FAQ

Is the Fireflies free plan really free forever?

Fireflies lists the Free plan at $0, “Free forever” (fireflies.ai/pricing). Storage and other limits still apply.

Do free-plan limits reset every month?

It depends on the tool. Otter (300 monthly transcription minutes), MeetGeek (3 hours a month), and Tactiq (10 transcripts and 5 AI credits a month) describe monthly limits. tl;dv says its free limits apply for the lifetime of the account, and Bluedot allows 5 meetings in total.

What happens when I hit a cap?

MeetGeek Basic stops recording and analyzing. Tactiq keeps transcripts accessible but pauses AI features until the next month. Fireflies requires you to delete older meetings once storage is full.

Can I keep my recordings on a free plan?

tl;dv stores free recordings for 3 months and lets you download them during the 4 weeks before deletion. MeetGeek keeps transcripts for 3 months and audio for 1 month.

Are Black Friday or regional prices the regular price?

No. Treat them as limited offers and confirm current terms on the vendor’s official page.

Conclusion

Pick the one tool whose free-plan caps fit your typical week, connect it to the meeting platform you already use, and run it on a few real meetings. The six free plans cap different things, so choose based on fit rather than any ranking.
