Who should consider switching, and why

I am not telling every Otter user to leave. The reasons below come from Otter’s own pricing page and help center on 20 November 2026, not from a test of the product.

You are hitting the published caps. Basic is 300 transcription minutes a month, 30 minutes per conversation, 3 lifetime audio or video imports, and the 25 most recent conversations. Pro is 1,200 in-app recording minutes, 10 file imports a month, and 90 minutes per meeting. If those numbers are already tight, look at tools whose official pages publish a higher or unlimited allowance on a paid plan you can actually buy.

Your workspace is larger than five people, and you do not want Business yet. The comparison table lists workspace size as up to 5 members on Basic and Pro, and up to 25 members on Business. Business annual is $19.99 per user per month with “save 33%.” Monthly Business is shown as $30 and $24 with “20% off for 3 months.” The page does not, in the text I read, say what the monthly Business price is after those three months.

You do not want a visible notetaker in the call. Otter’s Notetaker overview, updated 21 August 2026, says Notetaker joins Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams as a guest participant, appears as “[Your Name]’s Notetaker (Otter.ai),” and cannot join anonymously or without other participants seeing it. The same article says the Otter Desktop app can record without Notetaker joining as a separate participant. If the usual bot path is the problem, a tool that leads with device capture may fit better — or Otter’s own desktop path may be enough. I have not tested how obvious either path is to other people on the call.

The paid prices on the page are hard to read. On 20 November 2026 the Pro annual price is shown in more than one way, including a $4.17* first-time figure next to $8.33. If you want a single list price for a yearly seat, some of the tools below state one annual figure more plainly, though several of them also have a monthly price or a footnote. Recheck the live page; promotions change.

Otter can still be the right tool if you want live transcription, Japanese listed on the pricing page (English, Spanish, French, German, Japanese, and Chinese), a free plan whose caps are written in one table, or a notetaker that uses your name. Stay if those points matter more than the caps or the visible guest.

What Otter’s plans say today

All figures below are from otter.ai/pricing on 20 November 2026, in USD.

  • Basic is free. The plan card lists 300 monthly transcription minutes, 3 lifetime audio or video file imports, live transcription, and apps for iOS and Android. The comparison table lists 30 minutes per conversation, 25 most recent conversations in history, up to 5 workspace members, and 20 Otter AI Chat queries per user a month (3 per conversation).
  • Pro is for individuals and small teams. The monthly card shows $16.99 per user per month. The same page also shows annual Pro as $8.49* per user per month with “save 50%,” as $8.33 with “save 51%” (the Annual tab itself is labeled “save 51%”), and as $4.17* per user per month next to a prior $8.33, with “save 50%.” A footnote reads: “Shared pool across all transcription types” and “Offer valid for first-time subscribers only.” I cannot tell, from the text alone, which of the annual figures is the ongoing list price and which is only the first-time offer. Pro also lists 1,200 in-app recording minutes, 10 monthly file imports, 90 minutes per meeting, up to 5 workspace members, and 50 AI Chat queries per user a month. A FAQ on the same page offers a 20% student and teacher discount on Pro for a .edu address: $6.67 per month billed $79.99 annually, or $13.59 monthly.
  • Business is for medium-sized teams. The monthly card shows $30 and $24 per user per month with “20% off for 3 months.” The annual card shows $19.99 per user per month with “save 33%.” The plan lists unlimited meetings and in-app recordings, unlimited audio or video file imports (with an asterisk; a comparison line also says “Unlimited in-app recordings. Limit applies only to imported files”), 4 hours per meeting, up to 25 workspace members, joining 3 concurrent meetings, and 200 AI Chat queries per user a month.
  • Enterprise is custom pricing, with SSO, SCIM, domain capture, and HIPAA as an add-on, among other items.

A closer reading of those Otter prices, including the conflicts I already logged on 19 November 2026, is in Otter.ai vs Fireflies.ai. The $4.17* annual Pro figure was on the page when I rechecked it on 20 November 2026.

Alternatives, with an honest “best for”

Fireflies — best for a bot notetaker with a $10 annual Pro seat

Fireflies is the closest like-for-like switch if you already accept a visible bot. On 20 November 2026 the Fireflies pricing page listed Free at $0 (“free forever”), Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually or $18 monthly, Business at $19 or $29 on the same pattern, and Enterprise at $39 per seat per month, annual only. The knowledge-base article Learn about the Fireflies’ pricing plans repeats those figures. The pricing table marks Free storage as 400 minutes per team, Pro storage as 8,000 minutes per seat, and Business and Enterprise storage as unlimited. Recording limits are 2 hours on Free and Pro, 3 hours on Business, and 4 hours on Enterprise. Transcripts and AI summaries are marked Limited on Free and Unlimited on paid plans.

Fireflies’ join FAQ, checked the same day, says the bot appears as Fireflies.ai Notetaker and cannot be hidden. You can record without the bot with a Google Meet Chrome extension or the mobile app. A researched overview, including free-plan pages that disagree, is in the Fireflies.ai review. Skip Fireflies if the bot itself is why you want to leave Otter, unless you will only use the no-bot paths.

Bluedot — best for capture that does not invite a bot

Bluedot is the clearest official “no bot” alternative. The homepage, checked 20 November 2026, calls it an “Invisible, privacy-first AI note taker” and, in the FAQ, says it “never invites a bot to your meeting or asks for calendar access,” and that it uses a Chrome extension or a desktop app instead. The pricing page that day listed Free at $0 with 5 meetings for the life of the account; Basic at $14 per user per month on the annual row or $18 monthly, with unlimited audio-only meetings, unlimited storage, and a 1-hour cap per recording; Pro at $20 or $25, adding video and unlimited recording duration; and Business at $32 or $39, adding CRM and ATS integrations, SSO, and SCIM. The FAQ says Bluedot charges only users who record, not every workspace member. A full researched overview is in the Bluedot review. Skip it if you need a bot to attend a meeting you cannot join, or if five lifetime free meetings are not enough to try it.

Circleback — best for unlimited meetings on a free plan, with a bot or a desktop app

Circleback may fit a small team that wants a free plan without a published meeting cap. On 20 November 2026 the Circleback pricing page listed Free at $0, with AI meeting notes, a bot or a bot-free desktop app for online meetings, 30-day meeting and recording history, and 2 teams. A FAQ on that page says every plan, including Free, has unlimited meetings. Pro is $15 per user per month, with unlimited meeting history, 1 year of recording storage, and automations that name Notion, HubSpot, Salesforce, Attio, and webhooks. Business is $25, with admin roles, unlimited free viewers, and 2 years of recording storage. Enterprise is $35, with volume pricing. The page’s JSON-LD lists annual offers of $180, $300, and $420, which equal $15, $25, and $35 a month billed yearly. A FAQ says that on annual billing, 50 users cost $9,000 a year on Pro. I did not find a separate monthly list price in the HTML or in that JSON-LD. See the Circleback review. Skip it if you need a published transcription-minute table like Otter’s, because I did not find minute caps on the pricing cards.

Laxis — best for meeting notes and a voice keyboard on one subscription

Laxis is the one whose pricing page puts a meeting assistant and a voice keyboard on every plan, drawing from the same pool of monthly transcription minutes. On 20 November 2026 the Laxis pricing page and its meta description listed Basic at $0 with 300 minutes a month, 30 days of conversation history, and a 45-minute cap per recording; Premium at $9.99 per month billed yearly, with 2,000 minutes, unlimited conversation storage, and a 2-hour cap; Business at $19.99 per month billed yearly, with unlimited transcription, a 4-hour cap, team admin, and CRM automation; and Enterprise as custom. New accounts get a 14-day Premium trial with no credit card, then move to Basic. The page has a Monthly/Yearly toggle labeled “Save up to 38%.” The static HTML I downloaded listed $9.99 and $19.99 only; I could not confirm a different monthly list price from that HTML. For recording options that official Laxis pages describe differently, see the Laxis review. Skip it if 2,000 minutes on Premium would still be tight, or if you need CRM automation without paying for Business.

Tactiq — best for a bot-free browser extension with an $8 annual Pro seat

Tactiq is a Chrome or Edge extension for Google Meet, Zoom, and Microsoft Teams. Official pages say no bot joins the call, that meeting audio is not recorded or stored, and that Tactiq uses the meeting platform’s captions. The download page also lists a Mac app for Zoom desktop calls only. A help article says the Microsoft Teams desktop application is not currently supported. I did not find an iPhone or Android app on the download page. As of 20 November 2026, the official pricing page listed Free at $0, with no credit card, 10 transcripts per month, and 5 AI credits per month; Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited transcripts and 10 AI credits; Team at $16.67 per user per month billed annually, with unlimited AI credits; and Business at $29.17 per user per month billed annually. A help article dated 30 June 2026 also lists monthly prices of $12, $20, and $40 for Pro, Team, and Business. Skip Tactiq if you need a bot to attend a meeting you skip, a phone app, or stored audio playback. A full researched overview is in the Tactiq review, and a side-by-side is in Tactiq vs Otter.ai.

Sonix — best for uploaded audio and video transcription, including Japanese, with pay-as-you-go at $10 an hour

Sonix is not a like-for-like meeting-note taker in the same sense as the tools above. Official Sonix pages, checked 20 November 2026, describe it as speech-to-text software for audio and video you upload, with an in-browser editor, subtitles, and translation. The pricing page that day, last updated October 2026, listed Pay As You Go at $10 per hour, Core at $25 a month or $275 a year for 5 hours a month (60 hours a year), Advanced at $50 a month or $550 a year for 20 hours a month, and Pro at $80 a month or $880 a year for 40 hours a month. Additional hours on subscription plans are billed at $10 per hour. The same page says every new account includes 30 free minutes, with no credit card required. The languages page lists Japanese among 54+ transcription languages, and says Japanese transcripts are returned in kanji, hiragana, and katakana. Sonix’s features page says it delivers up to 99% transcription accuracy on clear recordings; I have not tested accuracy. A full walkthrough of the list prices is in Sonix pricing explained. Skip Sonix if you mainly need a calendar bot or a bot-free meeting recorder rather than file transcription.

FAQ

Why would a small team leave Otter?

On 20 November 2026, Otter’s pricing page listed Basic at 300 transcription minutes a month, 30 minutes per conversation, 3 lifetime file imports, and the 25 most recent conversations, with workspaces of up to 5 members on Basic and Pro. Pro is 1,200 in-app recording minutes and 90 minutes per meeting. Otter’s Notetaker help, updated 21 August 2026, says the usual notetaker joins as a visible guest and cannot join unseen. Teams that hit those caps, need more than five workspace members without moving to Business, or do not want a visible bot in the call often look at other tools. I have not tested switching.

What does Otter cost in November 2026?

On 20 November 2026 the Otter pricing page showed more than one paid price. Pro monthly is $16.99 per user per month. The same page shows annual Pro as $8.49* with “save 50%,” as $8.33 with “save 51%,” and as $4.17* with a struck $8.33 and “save 50%.” A footnote says an offer is valid for first-time subscribers only. Business monthly shows $30 and $24 per user per month with “20% off for 3 months.” Business annual is $19.99 per user per month with “save 33%.” Enterprise is custom. Check the official page before you buy.

Which alternative records without a bot in the meeting?

Tactiq’s official pages say no bot joins, and that a Chrome or Edge extension transcribes from the meeting’s captions. Bluedot’s homepage says it never invites a bot and records with a Chrome extension or a desktop app. Circleback’s pricing page lists a bot or a bot-free desktop app for online meetings. Otter’s own help says the desktop app can record without Notetaker joining as a participant. Fireflies help says you can record without the bot using a Google Meet Chrome extension or the mobile app, and a separate help article documents desktop Take Notes. I have not tested any of these paths. See also the bot-free comparison on this site.

Which paid alternative has the lowest yearly list price?

Among the approved-affiliate tools on this page, the lowest yearly list price I found on 20 November 2026 was Tactiq Pro at $8 per user per month billed annually, then Laxis Premium at $9.99 per month billed yearly, then Fireflies Pro at $10 per seat per month billed annually, Bluedot Basic at $14 per user per month on the annual row, and Circleback Pro at $15 per user per month, which matches that page’s JSON-LD annual offer of $180. These are different products with different limits, not interchangeable seats. Otter’s own annual Pro prices are shown several ways, including a first-time $4.17* figure, so I am not ranking Otter in that list.

Should every Otter user switch?

No. Otter may still fit a team that wants live transcription, a clearly written Basic allowance (300 minutes, 30-minute conversations, and 25 saved conversations), Japanese listed on the pricing page, or a visible notetaker with your name on it. This page is a researched overview of official pages, not a recommendation to cancel. Recheck each official page before you buy.

What if I mainly transcribe uploaded files?

The meeting-note tools in the comparison table are not the only option. Official Sonix pages, checked 20 November 2026, list Pay As You Go at $10 per hour, a 30-minute free trial with no credit card, and Japanese among 54+ transcription languages. See Sonix pricing explained.
