Krisp Review for Small Businesses (2026)

I have not used Krisp on client calls, so this is not a report of accuracy, noise-cancellation quality, or day-to-day reliability. Where an official page is silent, or where two official pages disagree, I say so. Prices and limits change. Check the current pricing page before you buy. Quick pick

Krisp: Official pages checked 25 November 2026 describe noise cancellation and an AI note taker in one Meeting AI product, with a 7-day free trial and no ongoing free plan. Core is listed at $8 per user per month billed annually, or $16 billed monthly.

Who it is for, and who should skip it

Krisp may fit a small team that wants background-noise removal and meeting notes in the same product, and that prefers not to send a bot into client calls. The homepage FAQ says the AI Meeting Assistant is a bot-free AI note taker, because Krisp works at the audio level, and that it integrates with every conferencing app, naming Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, Slack Huddles, and more. The Core card also lists in-person meeting notes, a mobile app, and an outbound phone call recorder.

Skip it if you need a standing free plan. The $0 card on the Meeting AI pricing page is a 7-day Free Trial, not an ongoing free plan. Skip it if you need Japanese transcription: the named language list on the homepage FAQ does not include Japanese. Skip the mobile app if you need noise cancellation on a phone; the homepage FAQ says the mobile app does not include noise cancellation today, and that it is coming. I am repeating that wording. I have not tested the app.

Krisp also sells Call Center AI and a developer SDK or API. The pricing page treats those as separate products. This review covers Meeting AI only.

Plans and pricing

On 25 November 2026 the Meeting AI pricing page showed these cards. The Core and Advanced cards each displayed two per-user monthly rates, with the higher figure struck through next to the lower one, which matches a monthly price and an annual-billing equivalent. Enterprise did not show a public price. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

  • Free Trial: $0 for 7 days, no credit card required. The card lists unlimited transcription, unlimited noise cancellation, unlimited audio and video recording, unlimited AI notes and action items, and limited AI accent conversion and understanding.
  • Core: $8 per user per month, with $16 per user per month shown beside it. The card is labeled “Best for individuals and small teams.” It lists an unlimited AI note taker (bot-free, transcription, audio and video recording, AI notes and action items, and in-person meeting notes), unlimited noise cancellation, unlimited integrations and webhook, a mobile app, an AI note taker for online and in-person meetings, an outbound phone call recorder, multilingual transcripts, AI chat, 1 hour a day of accent conversion (the card names Indian, Filipino, South-African, and Latam accents on the speaker side), team and billing management, MCP integration, and 10 GB of storage.
  • Advanced: $15 per user per month, with $30 per user per month shown beside it. The card is labeled “Most popular.” It lists everything in Core, plus accent conversion of 4 hours a day on the speaker side and unlimited on the listener side, advanced integrations named as Salesforce and ConnectWise, advanced admin controls (meeting link sharing access and note-taking mode enforcement), a manager view, company deal grouping, trim and comment, team-level webhooks, and 60 GB of storage.
  • Enterprise: No public price on the card I read. It lists SSO/SCIM, SOC2 report access, BAA signing available for teams with 100+ seats, data-center location choice, a super admin role, a dedicated account manager, and “HIPPA Compliance” as written on that card.

The Meeting AI pricing FAQ says there is no separate plan for noise cancellation only, because noise cancellation is built into every Krisp plan, and that you can disable the Meeting Assistant if you only want Krisp for clean audio. The same FAQ says volume discounts are offered if you are buying a plan for more than 50 members, and asks you to contact Krisp. I have not confirmed those discounts.

Five people on the Core annual rate of $8 would be 5 times $8, or $40 a month before any discount, if each person needs a seat. The FAQ says any teammate you add in your Krisp team is counted as a seat, and that paid plans are charged per seat. I am repeating the FAQ. I have not checked invoicing in an account.

Features, as the official pages describe them

The homepage groups in-meeting features as transcription and recording, noise cancellation that “removes noise, echo, and cross-talk,” and AI accent conversion. After the meeting, it describes an AI note taker for transcripts, summaries, and CRM updates, plus centralized action items. It names Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, and more for pushing notes and action items. The same page also lists pre-meeting agendas based on previous conversations, recording presets (transcript-only, audio, or video), and on-time notifications.

The Advanced pricing card is the place that names Salesforce and ConnectWise as “Advanced Integrations.” The Core card says “Unlimited Integrations & webhook” and names MCP integration, but it does not name Salesforce on the card itself. I have not confirmed which paid plan includes which named integration, except where a pricing card says so.

The homepage also lists a Chrome extension for conferencing apps inside Chrome, such as Google Meet; audio or video upload; custom vocabulary of 750 workspace-level terms; and a “Record with bot” option if you prefer automated participation. So the product is described as bot-free by default, with an optional bot. This page has not tested either path, and I have not confirmed which plan includes the optional bot.

The homepage FAQ says the mobile app can record and transcribe in-person conversations, capture hybrid sessions, or upload audio files, and that it works offline. It also says the mobile app does not include noise cancellation today. In-person meetings can also be recorded with the desktop app, according to the same FAQ.

The AI Meeting Assistant page repeats noise cancellation on both sides of the call, accent conversion that “keeps the speaker’s voice with its natural characteristics,” meeting summaries, action items, key decisions, and searchable transcripts with speaker identification and timestamps. It also describes a Transcript Only mode, which you can turn on in the app, during a call, or from the Krisp widget.

Languages

The homepage says “Transcripts and summaries in 16 languages.” The homepage FAQ says Krisp supports on-device English transcription, plus server-based transcription in 15 additional languages: Spanish, French, German, Russian, Italian, Dutch, Polish, Portuguese, Hindi, Danish, Swedish, Norwegian, Czech, Ukrainian, and Korean. That named list does not include Japanese.

The AI Meeting Assistant page says Krisp can “Transcribe meetings in 17 languages in real time with 96% accuracy.” I am repeating that claim. I have not reconciled the 16-language, 17-language, and named-list counts, and I have not tested accuracy.

Privacy and security, as official pages describe them

The homepage security section lists SOC 2 Certified, GDPR Compliance, HIPAA Compliant, and PCI-DSS Certified. The Meeting AI pricing FAQ says all data is encrypted at rest using AES-256 and in transit using TLS 1.3, that Krisp is certified for SOC 2 Type II, GDPR, and HIPAA and adheres to PCI-DSS, and that “Your meeting data is never used to train Krisp’s AI models.” I am repeating those statements. I have not audited Krisp.

The Enterprise pricing card writes “HIPPA Compliance,” which is not the same spelling as “HIPAA” on the homepage and in the pricing FAQ. I am noting the difference in wording. I have not verified a certification. The Enterprise card also lists SOC2 report access, BAA signing for teams with 100+ seats, and a data-center location choice. The default region for Core and Advanced was not stated on the pages I read.

The pricing FAQ says signup requires a Google or Outlook calendar connection by default, and that Krisp also provides a way to connect with zero calendar permissions. If you need to skip the calendar step during a trial, the FAQ says to contact sales. I have not walked through that signup flow.

FAQ

Is Krisp free?

The Meeting AI pricing page lists a Free Trial card at $0, with no credit card required, free for 7 days. I did not find an ongoing free Meeting AI plan on that page. After the trial, the lowest public Meeting AI price I saw was Core at $8 per user per month on the annual rate, or $16 on the monthly rate.

How much does Krisp cost?

On 25 November 2026 the Meeting AI pricing page listed Core at $8 per user per month with $16 shown beside it, and Advanced at $15 per user per month with $30 shown beside it. Enterprise had no public price. Call Center AI and the developer API are priced separately and are not covered here. Check the current pricing page before you buy.

Does Krisp put a bot in the meeting?

The homepage FAQ describes the AI Meeting Assistant as a bot-free AI note taker that works at the audio level across Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, Slack Huddles, and more. The same homepage also lists “Record with bot” and says you can enable bots to join meetings when needed. This page has not tested either mode, and I have not confirmed which plan includes the optional bot.

Does Krisp support Japanese?

The named language list on the homepage FAQ does not include Japanese. The homepage says 16 languages; the AI Meeting Assistant page says 17 languages in real time with 96% accuracy. I have not reconciled those counts, and I have not tested accuracy.

Is there a noise-cancellation-only plan?

The Meeting AI pricing FAQ says no. Noise cancellation is built into every Krisp plan, and you can disable the Meeting Assistant if you only want Krisp for clean audio. I am repeating the FAQ. I have not confirmed how that disable switch works in the app.
