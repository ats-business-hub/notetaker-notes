How to tell meeting participants you’re using an AI note taker

If you use an AI note taker in meetings, the people you are recording should know before capture starts. This guide covers what to tell them, when to say it, wording you can adapt, and what Otter, Fireflies, and Fathom say their products show participants. Vendor help pages were checked on 26 November 2026. This is not legal advice and not a hands-on test. Recording rules vary by location and context, so check the rules that apply to your meetings, or ask your legal team.

Why a visible bot is not the whole answer

Many bot-based note takers appear in the participant list, and some post a chat message. People still miss these signals. They join late, join by phone, or do not read the chat. Bot-free capture can also run with no participant in the list at all. Fathom’s help says that you are responsible for getting participants’ consent before using Fathom. Fireflies’ desktop help says that you are responsible for following recording laws and notifying participants as required. Otter’s recording permissions article says that recording laws vary, and that some places require every participant to be notified and to give permission.

A short spoken notice at the start, plus a line in the invite, covers most of the gaps. It also gives people a real chance to say no.

What to explain

PointWhat to tell people
PurposeWhy you want automated notes (for example, an accurate summary)
What is capturedAudio, video, chat, or a transcript only
What is producedTranscript, summary, action items, recording
Who sees itOnly you, your team, or everyone invited
Sharing afterwardsWhether notes are sent automatically, and to whom
How to objectHow to ask for manual notes or a pause instead

Only promise what your settings actually do. If the tool emails the summary to all attendees automatically, do not say the notes stay private. If you do not know how long the vendor keeps the data, say so, or skip automated capture for that meeting.

Five steps before and during the meeting

  1. Check your settings first. Does the tool join scheduled meetings automatically? Does it start capturing on its own? Who gets the summary? If you plan to ask first, make sure capture does not start while you are still asking. Our guide to stopping Otter Notetaker from auto-joining covers one example.
  2. Add a line to the calendar invite. People can raise concerns before the call. Keep it short enough to read.
  3. Say it out loud at the start. Repeat the notice before capture begins, and ask if anyone has concerns.
  4. Tell late joiners. Someone who arrives after the announcement missed it. Repeat it briefly when they join.
  5. Handle the notes as you said you would. Review the AI output before you send it, and share it only with the people you named.

Wording you can adapt

These are examples of plain notices, not consent forms or legal text. Change them to match your actual tool, settings, and recipients.

Calendar invite

I’d like to use an AI note taker in this meeting to help write an accurate summary. It will process the meeting audio. If you have concerns or would prefer manual notes, let me know before the meeting.

Start of the call

Before we start, I’d like to use an AI note taker to capture our discussion and write a summary. Is everyone OK with that? If not, we’ll go ahead without it.

Client meeting

With your permission, I’d like to use an AI meeting assistant to document today’s discussion. The notes will go only to the people on this invite. Is that all right?

For client calls, also check whether an existing confidentiality agreement limits sending meeting content to an outside provider.

In person

I’d like to use a note-taking app on my phone to capture audio for a written summary. Is everyone comfortable with that, or should I take notes by hand?

In a room there is no participant list, so the spoken notice matters more. See AI note takers for in-person meetings.

Meeting chat

Heads-up: I’d like to use an AI note taker for a summary of this call. Please say so before we start if you’d rather I didn’t.

Sensitive part of the meeting

This next part is confidential, so I’m pausing the note taker. I’ll write a short summary by hand.

What the tools themselves show participants

Otter

  • Otter Notetaker joins as a named participant in the participant list.
  • When it joins, it posts a chat message with a link to the live transcript. Members manage this in Account Settings > Meetings.
  • Pre-recording emails: Otter can email calendar guests before the meeting. Otter’s “Enforce pre-meeting recording notifications” article says the workspace-wide version is Enterprise only, arrives 5–10 minutes before the meeting, and uses a disclaimer that cannot be customized. Otter’s recording permissions article also mentions an account-level toggle in Account Settings > Meetings. Check what your plan shows.
  • For Microsoft Teams, the Otter for Outlook extension can add a page where each participant must agree to recording before joining. Otter says this needs the Microsoft Enterprise license and the account manager.
  • If someone objects, the host can remove Notetaker like any other participant. Any participant can also type stop otter in the chat (see our Otter guide).

Fireflies

Fireflies separates its bot (Fireflies Notetaker) from the desktop app’s Take Notes mode, which captures from your device without a bot. Fireflies’ help says the bot uses its own consent flow. For Take Notes, the desktop app can use up to three signals:

  • A meeting chat message (“This meeting is being transcribed by Fireflies.ai.”). Fireflies says this is available on Mac and not on Windows.
  • An audio consent message that plays through your speakers so others hear it through your microphone.
  • A “Fireflies Watermark” virtual camera that shows an “AI Taking Notes” notice on your video once recording starts.

If you capture without a bot, nobody sees a participant in the list. That makes the spoken notice more important. Compare options in bot-free AI meeting note takers.

Fathom

  • Recording Notification Banner: applies only to Bot Video capture. The bot shows “Recording and taking notes.”
  • Auto Request Recording Consent: emails external attendees of scheduled calendar events, generally about 24 hours before (Fathom 3.0+). Team plan admins can turn this on for the whole organization. For Zoom, you must be the host for the email to go out.
  • If any attendee declines, Fathom turns auto-capture off for that meeting, even if others agree. Fathom says a lack of reply should not be treated as consent.
  • Consent Chat Message: the desktop app can post a notice in the meeting chat when recording begins, with or without a bot.

None of these tools say their notices replace your own responsibility to inform people and get any consent that applies.

When someone objects

  • Stop or remove the note taker before the discussion continues. Do not argue the point in front of the group.
  • Offer manual notes, or agree to a short written summary that the person can check.
  • If capture already started, tell the person what was captured and how you will handle it.
  • For recurring meetings with a standing objection, turn off auto-join for that series instead of asking every week.

Checklist

  • I know whether my tool joins and records automatically
  • I know who receives the transcript and summary
  • The invite says an AI note taker will be used and how to object
  • I announce it at the start, before capture, and repeat it for late joiners
  • I can stop or remove the note taker quickly
  • I review the AI notes before sharing them

FAQ

Is a line in the calendar invite enough?

Often not. People miss invites or receive forwarded ones. Repeat the notice at the start of the call, before capture begins.

Do I need to tell people when I use a bot-free note taker?

Yes. With bot-free capture there may be no visible participant at all, so your own notice is the main signal. Fathom and Fireflies both say you are responsible for informing participants.

What if someone says no?

Stop or remove the note taker and take notes another way. Fathom, for example, turns auto-capture off for a meeting when one attendee declines its consent email.

Does agreeing to AI notes mean agreeing to wide sharing?

No. Tell people who will receive the notes, and stick to that.
